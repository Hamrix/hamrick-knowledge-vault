# Native Confidence Rubric

Use the Hamrick Knowledge Vault's native labels.

The label applies to the **precise proposition in the note**, not to a whole person or pedigree.

## confirmed

Use when the proposition itself is directly established or is the best-supported conclusion after strong correlation and conflict review.

Examples:
- a reviewed guardianship record explicitly calls Major Selby the orphan of John Selby;
- two reviewed sources unquestionably contain conflicting auditor chronologies;
- a reviewed marriage license names a specific parent.

Do not extend `confirmed` beyond what the source actually establishes.

A confirmed household co-enumeration does not confirm household relationships if the schedule does not state them.

## strong

Use when evidence substantially supports the proposition and viable alternatives are limited, but a material evidentiary step remains.

Typical reasons:
- relationship is reported in a near-contemporary derivative source whose original page image has not yet been inspected;
- identity correlation across several records is persuasive but no single record contains all aliases;
- indirect evidence is compelling but the decisive record remains unavailable.

## moderate

Use when the proposition is meaningfully supported and fits the known record pattern, but competing explanations or a substantial evidence gap remain.

## weak

Use when there is some relevant evidence but the proposition is fragile, source-dependent, or has significant unresolved alternatives.

## speculative

Use for hypotheses/leads with insufficient evidence to affect the factual lineage.

An assertion may be worth researching without being allowed into a confirmed genealogical chain.

## contradicted

Use when reliable evidence materially weighs against the precise proposition and no adequate reconciliation has been established.

## Special claim types

### Conflict claim
A note saying "Source A conflicts with Source B" can be `confirmed` even if neither underlying historical version is resolved.

### Unproven claim
A note such as "Major Selby Sr parentage remains unproven" can be confirmed if the vault review definitively shows that no inspected evidence establishes the parentage at the current state.

### Source-report claim
A note saying "The 1878 biography reports X" may be confirmed as a report fact while X itself remains strong/moderate until independently checked.

## Long-lineage rule

A continuous descent cannot be stronger than the weakest required parent-child/identity bridge.

Do not promote a long descent because many endpoint historical relationships are confirmed.
