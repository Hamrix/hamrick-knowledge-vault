# Evidence Policy — Hamrick Aetheling Project

## Authority model

Prefer:

1. reviewed primary-source images, original records, exact transcriptions
2. structured source/claim notes based on reviewed evidence
3. correlated project conclusions and approved project-state notes
4. secondary compilations and derivative indexes with limitations
5. family tradition/user research/hypotheses labeled appropriately
6. online trees, GEDCOM, AI reports as discovery aids only

## Source / information / evidence

Keep separate:

- **source** — record/publication/artifact
- **information** — statement inside it
- **evidence** — use of that information for a specific proposition

A primary record can contain mistaken information.
A derivative source can accurately report valuable information.
Assess the exact proposition.

## Host vs record

An original probate image hosted on FamilySearch can be primary evidence.

A user profile relationship on FamilySearch is not.

## Direct evidence

Examples:
- guardianship explicitly identifying parent/child
- will naming child
- marriage record naming parent
- deed explicitly stating kinship

Still test identity.

## Indirect evidence

May prove a claim if multiple independent facts form a coherent, explicit argument.

## Source independence

Repeated copies are not independent.

Trace distinctive assertions backward.

## Negative evidence

Absence becomes evidence only after checking:
- expected coverage
- survival
- jurisdiction
- variant names
- indexing/transcription risk

Otherwise log a negative search only.

## Derivative biography discipline

A near-contemporary biography can have high identity value while containing chronology errors.

Do not accept/reject the entire source wholesale.
Assess each proposition separately.

## Claim/source split

A source may simultaneously:
- strongly support identity;
- directly report parentage;
- conflict with an official chronology;
- remain unverified for another biographical statement.

Preserve this granularity.
