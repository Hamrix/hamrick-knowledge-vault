# William Atkinson Selby colonial Maryland lineage claim

## Purpose

Preserve the proposed ancestry of **William Atkinson Selby (1757–1809)** as a discrete colonial-American Selby line. This note must not be used to bridge the Maryland family automatically into the medieval Selbys of Northumberland or Yorkshire.

## Proposed lineage

### 1. William Atkinson Selby — anchor person

- **Born:** 1757, Somerset County, Maryland.
- **Died:** 1809, Bourbon County, Kentucky.
- **Military claim:** Served as a private in the Maryland militia during the American Revolutionary War.
- **Marriage claim:** Married **Sarah White Townsend** on **28 February 1782** at Snow Hill, Worcester County, Maryland.

### 2. Parents — John Selby and Mary Atkinson

- Proposed father: **John Selby**, associated with Nassawadux/Nassawango and Pocomoke Hundred in Worcester County, Maryland.
- Proposed mother: **Mary Atkinson**.
- Naming inference: William's middle name, **Atkinson**, is proposed to derive from his mother's family name.
- Geographic context: This family lived in the Somerset–Worcester border region during the period in which Worcester County was formed from Somerset County.

### 3. Grandparents — John Selby and Margaret

- Proposed paternal grandparents: **John Selby** and a wife named **Margaret**.
- Landholding claim: John inherited tract lands in the Pocomoke Hundred region.
- Social characterization requiring proof: The family has been described as locally prominent tobacco planters. This characterization should remain provisional until supported by tax lists, land records, probate inventories, or comparable primary evidence.

### 4. Great-grandfather — Thomas Selby Jr.

- Proposed death: about **1758**, Maryland.
- Land/probate claim: He expanded the family's landholdings and devised property to his son John through his will.

### 5. Great-great-grandfather — Thomas Selby Sr.

- Proposed death: **1702**.
- Migration claim: He lived first in **Accomack County, Virginia**, then moved north into Maryland by approximately **1674**.
- Evidentiary role: He is presently treated as the **earliest proposed and potentially verifiable patriarch of this Southern colonial Selby line**.

## Working chain

**Thomas Selby Sr.** → **Thomas Selby Jr.** → **John Selby and Margaret** → **John Selby and Mary Atkinson** → **William Atkinson Selby**

Every parent-child step must be supported independently by probate, land, church, court, tax, military, or other contemporaneous records before the chain is marked verified.

## Critical contradiction / tree-contamination warning

Some online trees reportedly merge this colonial Maryland family with medieval Selbys of Twizell Castle or Northumberland and attach dates such as **1457** to a supposed ancestor named **William Atkinson Selbee**.

This must be treated as a **chronological and identity-conflation error**, not evidence. The likely mechanism is automated tree-merging that combines:

- William Atkinson Selby's **1757 Maryland birth year**;
- the **Atkinson** middle name from the Maryland line; and
- unrelated medieval or early-modern Selby entries from northern England.

No connection from Thomas Selby Sr. of the Virginia–Maryland line to the medieval Northumberland/Yorkshire Selbys is established by this note.
