## Evidentiary significance

This memorial is materially stronger than a burial-unknown memorial because it includes a cemetery location and a gravestone photograph. The grave marker can potentially support the name, death date, and age at death, subject to a close reading of the stone image.

The reported birth date of 10 February 1783 closely matches the 1929 Selby-Headen Bible transcription for John, son of William Atkinson Selby and Sarah White Townsend. That agreement increases the value of the memorial as corroborating derivative evidence, though it does not independently prove parentage.

The stated Bourbon County, Kentucky marriage in 1807 provides an important migration clue:

Somerset/Worcester County, Maryland
→ Bourbon County, Kentucky by 1807
→ Rush County, Indiana by death in 1871.

This route may help explain why the William Atkinson Selby memorial places William’s 1809 death in Bourbon County, Kentucky. It does not prove that William moved or died there, but it creates a specific family-migration hypothesis worth testing in tax, deed, probate, marriage, and court records.

## Internal conflicts requiring separation

The memorial text says John married Anna McCollie in 1807, while the linked spouse displayed is Elizabeth McCormack Selby, with a marriage indication of 1826.

Possible explanations:
- John married twice.
- Anna McCollie and Elizabeth McCormack were separate wives.
- One name is a transcription or profile-merger error.
- The 1826 date refers to a later marriage or remarriage.

Do not merge Anna McCollie and Elizabeth McCormack without original marriage records.

The displayed child list may belong to one or both marriages and should not be treated as complete until census, probate, Bible, guardianship, or church records identify the children and their mother.

## Current conclusion

John Atkinson Selby, born 10 February 1783 and buried in Rush County, Indiana, is a strong candidate for the John recorded in the Selby-Headen Bible as a son of William Atkinson Selby and Sarah White Townsend. The memorial’s gravestone image and exact chronology make it a valuable descendant lead.

The parentage, two possible spouses, child assignments, and Kentucky migration still require original records before being promoted from derivative reconstruction to proved genealogy.
