# Selby oral tradition: kidnapped Maryland planter, Letters of Marque, and Enoch George branch

Status: oral-family-history / derivative narrative lead; not accepted as documentary proof without supporting records.

## Purpose

Preserve user-supplied scanned/typescript pages containing Selby family tradition. The pages are important because they record how one Selby branch remembered its immigrant origin, Maryland indenture story, Revolutionary-era privateering/Letters of Marque claim, and later Enoch George Selby / Lloyd Selby / Hiram Selby family branch. They should be treated as clue sources and compared against original records.

## Source as supplied

The user supplied images of typed narrative pages. No title page, author, date, repository, or full surrounding document is visible in the supplied images.

The first page appears to contain family reminiscence, including statements attributed to the writer's father, **Clifford Warner Selby**. The second page continues the anecdote about **Cynthia Selby Price** and identifies a newly discovered half-brother, **Hiram Selby**.

## Main narrative claims preserved

### Medieval/Scottish Wallace story

The first page says an old history book recorded that **Sir William Wallace of Scotland** was declared an outlaw because he killed **Lord Selby**, an Englishman, after Lord Selby allegedly drunkenly insulted Wallace, picked a fight, and was killed.

Caution:
- This is a historical/folkloric story, not a genealogical link.
- It should not be used as proof of descent from any medieval Selby family.
- The identity of the alleged Lord Selby and the cited "old history book" must be identified before the story can be evaluated.

### Immigrant-origin family tradition

The first page reports a story told by the writer's father, **Clifford Warner Selby**, that the first Selby forefather who came to America:

- was kidnapped as a 14-year-old boy on a London street;
- was shipped to America;
- was sold to a Maryland planter;
- worked seven years to pay his indenture;
- married the daughter of his former owner;
- became owner of the estate after his wife's father died;
- during the Revolutionary War, received **Letters of Marque** from the Continental Congress;
- preyed on British shipping;
- was declared a pirate by the British.

Cautions:
- This story may preserve real elements of indentured servitude, Maryland settlement, marriage into land, or Revolutionary privateering, but it is not proof as written.
- The Revolutionary War detail cannot apply to the first immigrant if he was also a seventeenth-century Maryland immigrant; it may combine multiple generations.
- "Letters of Marque" should be checked in Continental Congress, Maryland naval/privateer, and admiralty records.
- The story may be attached to a different Selby branch than the William Atkinson Selby / Worcester County branch.

### Enoch George Selby branch

The first page says the writer's great-grandfather, **Rev. Enoch George Selby**, was a Methodist circuit rider.

It reports that:
- Enoch George Selby married **Annie Conard**.
- Annie Conard was said to be something of a shrew.
- They had five children.
- Enoch George Selby was a pronounced abolitionist.
- He went to visit his brother **Dr. Lloyd Selby** in Jackson, Mississippi.
- Lloyd Selby was an intimate friend of **Jefferson Davis** and a member of his staff.
- Jefferson Davis was a frequent visitor in Lloyd Selby's home.
- Dr. Lloyd Selby's first son was named **Jefferson Davis Selby**.
- Dr. Selby was said to have drowned while crossing the Mississippi River at night in a rowboat that overturned.

Further, the first page says Enoch George Selby wrote to his family saying he expected to start for home, failed to appear, and that his trunk containing his diary and possessions was found, but no trace of him was found until late in the 1920s, almost 80 years later. The family assumed he may have been killed because of his anti-slavery views.

Cautions:
- This is a family tradition with multiple verifiable anchors, but each claim requires original records.
- The abolitionist disappearance story is potentially historically significant and should be checked against Methodist conference records, newspapers, probate, correspondence, and death records.
- The Jefferson Davis connection must be tested carefully; proximity or friendship traditions often become exaggerated over time.

### Cynthia Selby Price and Hiram Selby half-brother story

The second page continues the **Cynthia Selby Price** anecdote. It reports:

- Cynthia Selby Price was in a grocery store in **Laddonia, Missouri**.
- A stranger addressed her as "Sister."
- She objected, and he explained that her maiden name was **Cynthia Selby** and that she was the daughter of **Enoch Selby**.
- He said his name was **Hiram Selby** and that he was her **half-brother**.
- Cynthia invited Hiram home and heard his story.

The second page then states that:
- Enoch Selby had started for home as planned but was not too far from the Ohio River when he met a 16-year-old girl in **Tucker County, West Virginia**.
- Enoch married her.
- By this later wife he had **nine children**.
- He outlived the later wife by 16 years and married a third time.
- He reportedly had composed a book of sermons that he intended to publish.
- Wife #3 became angry, threw his sermons into the fire, and burned them.
- The narrator and brother **Raymond** called on a daughter of Hiram's, **Ruth Dumire Selby**, in **Cleveland, Ohio**, in 1930.
- Ruth was married to **Dr. Ralph D. Voorhees**, who died in 1949.

Interpretation:
- This second page gives specific research anchors that can be checked in census, marriage, probate, church, and local records.
- The Hiram half-brother story may explain why Enoch's disappearance tradition persisted in the earlier family: he apparently formed a second family rather than returning home.
- The Tucker County, West Virginia location is an important geographic clue.
- Ruth Dumire Selby and Dr. Ralph D. Voorhees provide a twentieth-century verification target.

Cautions:
- The story appears to explain Enoch's disappearance, but it remains oral narrative until matched to records.
- The phrase "16 year old girl" should be handled as an age claim from oral history and checked against marriage/census records.
- Hiram Selby's full identity, mother, siblings, and exact relationship to Cynthia Selby Price require documentary proof.

## Relationship to the William Atkinson Selby proof project

These pages do not prove the William Atkinson Selby line. They may belong to a collateral or separate Selby branch. They should be compared against:

- the confirmed Worcester/Somerset Selby line from Thomas Selby to John Selby to William Atkinson Selby;
- the Prince George's Selby cluster;
- later Kentucky, Indiana, Missouri, Mississippi, Ohio, West Virginia, and Louisiana Selby branches;
- any line containing Clifford Warner Selby, Enoch George Selby, Lloyd Selby, Jefferson Davis Selby, Cynthia Selby Price, Hiram Selby, Ruth Dumire Selby, or Ralph D. Voorhees.

The privateering/Letters of Marque detail is chronologically suspicious if applied to the first immigrant and likely indicates generational blending.
