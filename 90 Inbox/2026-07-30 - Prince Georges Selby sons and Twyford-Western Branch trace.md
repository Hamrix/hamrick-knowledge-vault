# Prince George's Selby sons and Twyford-Western Branch trace

Status: preliminary record trace; no proved connection to Somerset/Worcester Selbys.

## Purpose

Trace the sons and land of William Selby of Prince George's County, whose will was dated 5 November 1698 and proved 25 February 1698/9, to determine whether any heir migrated to or interacted with the Somerset/Worcester County Selbys.

## Established starting household

William Selby's will names:
- wife Mary Selby;
- daughter Mary, wife of Robert Sallers;
- daughter Amy/Amee Hucker;
- sons William, Charles, and Samuel Selby;
- daughter Susannah Selby;
- tract `Twyford`, 100 acres, devised to Amy Hucker;
- 200 acres on the Western Branch devised to Robert Sallers and wife Mary.

## Son William Selby

A Prince George's County William Selby appears in later records and is a strong candidate for the testator's son, but the parent-child identity must still be checked against the original 1698 will and the younger William's probate.

Current evidence leads:
- William Selby served as constable and took a 1719 list of taxables in Mount Calvert Hundred, Prince George's County.
- William Selby married Sarah Magruder, daughter of Samuel Magruder and Sarah Beall, by 1734; the Maryland State Archives biographical entry for Samuel Magruder identifies daughter Sarah as wife of William Selby.
- A William Selby of Prince George's County made a will 8 October 1732, proved 1 August 1733.
- FamilySearch and compiled-family material attribute children to this younger William, but the complete child list must be taken from the original will and probate, not the online reconstruction.

Interpretation:
- This strongly suggests that at least one branch of the 1698 Prince George's family remained on the western shore and entered the Magruder-Beall kinship network.
- No evidence presently places this William or his proved children in Somerset or Worcester County.

## Son Samuel Selby

Current evidence leads:
- Ann Harris's 1751 Prince George's County will refers to 45 acres she had bought from Samuel Selby.
- A Samuel Selby of Frederick County made a will 28 August 1758. It names wife Amelia and children including Samuel, William, Harris, Cassandra, Mary, Anne, Amelia, and Barbary; witnesses included Samuel Selby and William Magruder Selby.

Interpretation:
- The naming and Prince George's/Frederick/Magruder network make this Samuel branch a plausible descendant branch of the 1698 household.
- The 45-acre conveyance should be located in the deed books to identify the tract, Samuel's residence, spouse release, and chain of title.
- No Somerset/Worcester migration has yet been found.

## Son Charles Selby

No sufficiently specific primary-record hit has yet been identified for Charles Selby that securely distinguishes him from other same-name men.

Priority targets:
- Prince George's deed index entries for Charles Selby;
- probate, administration, tax, and court records;
- deeds involving siblings William, Samuel, Mary Sallers, Amy Hucker, or Susannah;
- any record naming a residence outside Prince George's County.

## Daughter Amy/Amee Hucker and Twyford

The 1698 will devised 100 acres called `Twyford` to daughter Amy/Amee Hucker.

Important caution:
- Search results for the surname/place-name `Twyford` in Dorchester and Caroline Counties concern unrelated Twyford families and tracts unless a deed explicitly ties them to William Selby's Prince George's property.

Required trace:
1. Identify the original patent or earlier deed for `Twyford` in Prince George's County.
2. Locate the post-1699 deed or inheritance record for Amy Hucker.
3. Identify Amy's husband and all surname spellings: Hucker, Hacker, Hooker, or similar.
4. Follow the tract until sale outside the family.
5. Record every grantor, grantee, spouse, witness, adjoining owner, and stated residence.

A Maryland marriage-reference index notes an Ann, daughter of William Selby, married by 7 March 1710 to a Tucker, based on Prince George's Land Record F:62. This may concern a separate daughter, a variant reading, or another William Selby household and must not be merged with Amy Hucker without viewing the deed.

## Daughter Mary Sallers and Western Branch land

The 1698 will devised 200 acres on the Western Branch to son-in-law Robert Sallers and wife Mary.

Required trace:
1. Locate the exact deed, patent, or tract name for the 200 acres.
2. Search spelling variants: Sallers, Sellers, Sollers, Sellars, and related forms.
3. Trace later conveyances by Robert and Mary.
4. Determine whether heirs or purchasers moved to the Eastern Shore.
5. Compare witnesses and adjoining owners with known Somerset/Worcester Selby associates.

The broad phrase `Western Branch` is not enough to identify the tract. The original will image and subsequent land records are required.

## Network evidence

William Selby appears in Prince George's County court records in the late 1690s as a juror and subpoenaed witness, placing him in a western-shore legal network with men such as Ninian Beall, Samuel Magruder, Charles Busey, and William Conley.

This network is materially different from the early Accomack-Somerset record cluster around Thomas Selby/Solbie. Shared surname and chronology are not sufficient to merge the families.

## Current conclusion

The preliminary trace currently points toward a continuing Prince George's-to-Frederick/Montgomery branch:

William Selby Sr., died 1698/9
→ likely son William Selby, planter/constable, died 1733, married Sarah Magruder
→ a western-shore Magruder-Beall-connected descendant network

and a likely Samuel branch active in Prince George's and Frederick Counties through the 1750s.

No son or land conveyance has yet been found that reaches Somerset or Worcester County. The decisive next work is the deed-level chain for `Twyford`, the Western Branch acreage, Samuel's 45-acre sale, and the complete 1733 probate of the younger William Selby.
