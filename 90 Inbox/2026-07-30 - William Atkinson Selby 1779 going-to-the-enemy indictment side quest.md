# William Atkinson Selby and the 1779 “going to the enemy” indictment

## Status
Research lead requiring direct examination of the Worcester County court record and related Maryland archival material.

## Core claim
A Maryland biographical/archival summary reportedly identifies William Atkinson Selby, son of John Selby of Nassawadux in Pocomoke Hundred, Worcester County, as having been indicted by the Worcester County Court in June 1779 for “going to the enemy.”

If the indexed identification is correct, this creates a major conflict with the later family-history claim that William served as a private in the Maryland militia in 1780. Both records could concern the same man, but their relationship must be established rather than assumed.

## Proposed political interpretation
The phrase “going to the enemy” is interpreted in the research lead as a Revolutionary-era allegation of crossing to, aiding, supplying, or otherwise associating with British or Loyalist forces. This interpretation is plausible in the Eastern Shore context but must be confirmed from the statute, indictment language, and disposition of William’s individual case.

The lead proposes that William and his future father-in-law, Barkley Townsend, may therefore have shared Loyalist sympathies rather than standing on opposite sides of the Revolution. William married Sarah White Townsend on 28 February 1782.

## John Selby lead
William’s father, John Selby, is described as an established Worcester County landholder associated with:

- Nassawadux/Pocomoke Hundred;
- the tract called Benefield;
- lot 12 in Snow Hill;
- the 1783 Worcester County tax assessment.

These property claims should be checked against the original assessment and deed records. The absence of a known military role does not by itself establish political neutrality.

## Proposed resolution narrative
The supplied research suggests that William may have avoided long-term punishment through some combination of:

1. widespread Loyalist activity in Worcester County;
2. security or recognizance posted by established landholders such as his father;
3. fines, bonds, oaths, or postwar clemency rather than imprisonment;
4. reintegration through marriage and property holding;
5. postwar tax-list appearance.

At present, none of these mechanisms has been directly documented for William’s individual case. They are contextual hypotheses, not established facts.

## Claims requiring separate proof

### Strongest lead
- June 1779 Worcester County indictment of William Atkinson Selby for “going to the enemy.”

### Unresolved
- exact wording of the indictment;
- whether this William is conclusively the later husband of Sarah White Townsend;
- whether he appeared, pleaded, was tried, convicted, acquitted, pardoned, discharged, or failed to appear;
- whether John Selby or another person posted security;
- whether William took an oath of allegiance;
- whether a general amnesty legally disposed of his case;
- whether the 1780 militia-list entry reflects compulsory enrollment, rehabilitation, a different man, or an indexing error;
- whether the militia unit was Captain Quinton’s Company of the Wicomico Battalion, a Sinepuxent unit, or another organization.

## Priority record search

1. Worcester County Court criminal and recognizance records for June 1779 and subsequent terms.
2. Maryland State Archives biographical entry from the *Biographical Dictionary of the Maryland Legislature* or related database.
3. Docket, indictment, presentment, minute, and judgment books under Selby/Selbee/Selby variants.
4. Maryland confiscation, treason, oath, pardon, and Loyalist-property records.
5. 1779–1783 Worcester County security bonds and recognizances.
6. Original 1780 militia list naming William Atkinson Selby.
7. 1783 tax assessment entries for William and John Selby.
8. Barkley Townsend’s Revolutionary-era legal and confiscation records.

## Working historical model
A defensible provisional model is that William Atkinson Selby was accused in 1779 of associating with the British or Loyalist cause, yet remained or returned to Worcester County, married Sarah White Townsend in 1782, and later appeared in ordinary civil records. How the indictment was resolved is presently unknown.

## Publication-safe wording
> A Maryland archival index reportedly identifies William Atkinson Selby of Worcester County as having been indicted in June 1779 for “going to the enemy.” This raises a serious conflict with a later family-history account that places a man of the same name in the Maryland militia in 1780. The original indictment, disposition, and militia record must be compared before William’s Revolutionary allegiance can be characterized confidently.

## Research significance
This side investigation may materially change the interpretation of the Selby–Townsend marriage and William’s Revolutionary identity. It should remain separate from the royal-lineage proof chain, but it is important for an accurate biographical profile of William Atkinson Selby.
