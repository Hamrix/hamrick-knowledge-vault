# Stafford–Hamrick–Ethelred naming chain and pedigree-collapse evidence leads

Status: **Research lead / evidence acquisition plan**  
Date added: 2026-07-30  
Branch: `audit/aetheling-project-initial`

## Research purpose

Preserve the newly reported Ancestry-tree sequence that may explain why the modern descendant bears the name **Hamrick Ethelred Selby**, while clearly distinguishing online-tree assertions from record-proved relationships.

The central hypothesis is that the names **Ethelred**, **Hamrick**, and **Eveline** were already circulating together in a Louisiana Stafford ancestral network before entering the Selby line.

## User-reported Ancestry sequence

The user reports that Ancestry presents some or all of the following sequence:

1. An earlier **Stephen Stafford**.
2. A later **Stephen Stafford**, associated with **Betsey (Elizabeth) Peters**.
3. **Ethelred R. Stafford**, also shown in at least one profile presentation as associated with an Elizabeth Peters.
4. **John E. Stafford**, married to **Eveline M. Hamrick**.
5. **Ethelred H. Stafford**, married to **Frances B. Coe**.
6. Later Stafford descendants leading toward **Sarah Eveline Stafford**.
7. Sarah Eveline Stafford marrying into the Selby family.
8. Later Selby descendants carrying the name **Ethelred**, culminating in **Hamrick Ethelred Selby**.

This sequence is not yet accepted as a proved pedigree. It is preserved as a targeted research lead.

## Potentially significant naming pattern

If the reported relationships are confirmed, the following pattern would be genealogically important:

- **Ethelred** appears as a recurring Stafford given name.
- **Eveline** appears in the reported wife **Eveline M. Hamrick** and later in **Sarah Eveline Stafford**.
- **Hamrick** appears as a maternal surname within the Stafford line.
- **Ethelred H. Stafford** may have carried **Hamrick** as a middle name, but the expansion of the initial `H.` must not be assumed without a full-name record.
- The modern name **Hamrick Ethelred Selby** may preserve two ancestral naming elements that were already linked within the Stafford–Hamrick family network.

Working interpretation:

> The modern combination “Hamrick Ethelred” may reflect inherited naming traditions carried through repeatedly connected Stafford, Hamrick, and Selby branches rather than a recent arbitrary name choice.

## Important identity conflict: Elizabeth Peters versus Elizabeth Quine

Two separate women and couples may be involved:

### Reported Peters couple

- **Stephen Stafford**
- **Elizabeth / Betsey Peters**

### Reported Quine couple

- **John Stafford**
- **Elizabeth / Betsey Quine**

These couples must not be merged.

A further conflict remains because the Ancestry presentation may also place an **Elizabeth Peters** with **Ethelred R. Stafford**. Possible explanations include:

1. two distinct women named Elizabeth Peters;
2. a mother and daughter-in-law with the same name;
3. an online-tree attachment error;
4. conflation of Stephen Stafford and Ethelred R. Stafford;
5. repeated intermarriage between the same Stafford and Peters families.

No explanation should be selected without original records.

## Repeated intermarriage and pedigree collapse hypothesis

The user recalls a family tradition or prior research indicating that the same families married into one another multiple times.

If confirmed, this could explain:

- repeated appearances of the surnames Stafford, Hamrick, Peters, Selby, Parker, Townsend, White, or related families;
- recurrence of the names Ethelred, Eveline, and Hamrick;
- apparently duplicated couples that are actually cousins or namesakes;
- multiple genealogical paths to the same Stafford or medieval royal ancestors;
- pedigree collapse within the later Louisiana family network.

However, repeated intermarriage must be distinguished from:

- duplicate Ancestry profiles;
- incorrect merges;
- same-name individuals from unrelated families;
- copied unsourced trees.

## Connection to the Æthelred-name project

This lead is directly relevant to the project’s two separate questions:

### Naming-history question

Was the name **Ethelred** transmitted through the Stafford–Hamrick line into the Selby family, and was **Hamrick** preserved from Eveline M. Hamrick’s family?

### Descent question

Does the same Stafford line descend continuously from the medieval Stafford family that entered the Plantagenet and Wessex royal descent through Anne of Gloucester?

The naming chain can be historically meaningful even before the medieval pedigree is fully proved. Conversely, proving royal descent does not by itself prove why the name was chosen.

## Evidence classifications

### User-reported tree assertions

The following are currently **online-tree leads**, not proved facts:

- Stephen Stafford → later Stephen Stafford.
- Stephen Stafford married Elizabeth Peters.
- Ethelred R. Stafford’s exact parentage and spouse.
- John E. Stafford married Eveline M. Hamrick.
- Ethelred H. Stafford was their son.
- Ethelred H. Stafford married Frances B. Coe.
- the exact descent from this couple to Sarah Eveline Stafford.
- the exact descent from Sarah Eveline Stafford into the user’s Selby line.

### Historically suggestive evidence

The repeated use of **Ethelred**, **Eveline**, and **Hamrick** in the reported sequence is strong onomastic evidence of a family naming tradition if the identities and relationships can be verified.

Onomastic recurrence is supporting evidence only. It cannot independently prove parentage.

## Priority original records to acquire

For every couple, seek records that explicitly identify parents, spouses, or heirs:

1. Marriage record for Stephen Stafford and Elizabeth / Betsey Peters.
2. Probate, Bible, baptismal, guardianship, or land record proving Ethelred R. Stafford’s parents.
3. Marriage record and full-name evidence for Ethelred R. Stafford’s spouse.
4. Marriage record for John E. Stafford and Eveline M. Hamrick.
5. Census households linking John and Eveline to Ethelred H. Stafford.
6. Birth, baptismal, military, marriage, death, obituary, or probate record expanding `Ethelred H. Stafford` and `John E. Stafford` to their full names.
7. Marriage record for Ethelred H. Stafford and Frances B. Coe.
8. Probate and civil records connecting their descendants to Sarah Eveline Stafford.
9. Marriage and parentage records for Sarah Eveline Stafford and James Benjamin Sanford Selby.
10. Birth, obituary, Bible, military, and church records tracing the later transmission of **Ethelred** through Frederic/Frederick Ethelred Selby and subsequent generations.
11. Family letters, Bible annotations, newspaper profiles, or oral-history recordings explicitly explaining why the name Ethelred was chosen.

## Current conclusion

The reported sequence is one of the strongest leads for explaining the **specific modern name Hamrick Ethelred Selby**. It suggests that both names may derive from the same repeatedly intermarried Stafford–Hamrick family network.

It does **not yet prove**:

- the exact generational chain;
- that every repeated Elizabeth Peters entry is a separate real person;
- that `H.` means Hamrick;
- that the line reaches the medieval ducal Staffords;
- or that the name Ethelred was consciously chosen because of descent from Æthelred II.

The lead should be developed in parallel as both a naming-history investigation and a parent-child proof project.
