# Thomas Selby Sr. — Virginia and Maryland primary-source leads

**Status:** Claims under review; documentary citations and images pending  
**Confidence:** Mixed — individual record references may be testable, but the identity links and interpretations are not yet proved  
**Added:** 2026-07-30

## Purpose

Preserve a set of specific primary-source leads concerning an early colonial Thomas Selby, while separating record-level claims from later interpretation. Nothing in this note presently proves descent from the medieval Selbys of Northumberland, Twizell, Biddleston, or any royal or peerage line.

## Lead 1 — Northampton/Accomack, Virginia headright record, 1652

### Supplied claim

On 27 November 1652, John Brown of Northampton County reportedly received 1,000 acres near Matchepongo at Phillips Creek for transporting twenty persons into Virginia. One of the transported names is said to appear as **Thomas Solbie**, treated as a phonetic or clerical variant of Selby.

### Possible significance

If the record, date, spelling, and identification are confirmed, it would establish the presence of a person named Thomas Solbie on Virginia's Eastern Shore by 1652. It would also provide a plausible early colonial footprint for a Thomas Selby later associated with the Maryland Eastern Shore.

### Required verification

- Locate the original patent, patent-book entry, or authoritative abstract.
- Record the exact archive citation, volume, folio/page, patent description, acreage, and complete transported-person list.
- Confirm whether the name reads **Solbie**, **Selbie**, **Selby**, or another form.
- Determine whether the headright proves transport in 1652 or only that the claimant asserted an earlier transport by that date.
- Do not infer age, occupation, indenture status, or arrival circumstances without corroborating evidence.
- Establish whether this person can be connected to the later Maryland Thomas Selby through deeds, court appearances, associates, land adjacency, probate, or chronology.

## Lead 2 — *Batchelor* of Bristol and Maryland land-right assignments, 1674

### Supplied claim

The ship *Batchelor* of Bristol reportedly arrived in Maryland in 1674 under Samuel Gibbons. Gibbons is said to have petitioned for land rights based on transporting ninety named persons, producing 4,500 acres in headrights. The supplied narrative states that land rights passed from Gibbons to Robert Ridgely and were then assigned to Thomas Selby.

### Possible significance

A confirmed assignment to Thomas Selby could show participation in Maryland's land-right market and provide a dated link among Samuel Gibbons, Robert Ridgely, and Thomas Selby. It could also identify a commercial network touching Bristol and St. Mary's City.

### Required verification

- Locate the original 1674 Maryland warrant, petition, assignment, or land-office entry.
- Capture the exact wording and determine whether the asset assigned was acreage, warrants, certificates, or transport rights.
- Verify the vessel name, master or owner, arrival date, number of transported persons, and identities of assignor and assignee.
- Determine whether Thomas Selby bought land rights, received an assignment, patented land, or merely appeared in a chain of transfers.
- Identify the resulting tract names, acreage, county, survey dates, patent dates, and subsequent conveyances.
- Do not describe Thomas as an affluent planter-merchant solely from a land-right assignment. Wealth, occupation, and social standing require independent evidence such as tax lists, inventories, acreage held, servants or enslaved persons, offices, mercantile accounts, or probate.
- Bristol is a research lead, not proof of birthplace, baptism, or parentage.

## Lead 3 — Daniel Selby and a possible Anglo-Irish connection

### Supplied claim

A contemporary Daniel Selby reportedly appeared in the same broader Eastern Shore region by about 1670 and patented nearby land in the same hundred later associated with Worcester County, Maryland. The supplied narrative further claims that Daniel was of Anglo-Irish descent and proposes that Thomas and Daniel may have been brothers or close cousins.

### Current assessment

This is a **hypothesis**, not an established relationship. Geographic proximity, surname identity, and overlapping associates may justify investigation, but they do not prove kinship. The Anglo-Irish description also requires a direct source.

### Required verification

- Identify every known Daniel Selby record with exact date, jurisdiction, tract, office, witness, neighbor, and associate.
- Find the specific source that describes Daniel as Irish, Anglo-Irish, formerly resident in Ireland, or connected to an Irish family.
- Compare Thomas and Daniel in deeds, wills, administrations, guardianships, court suits, tax lists, headrights, witness networks, and naming patterns.
- Search for explicit kinship terms such as brother, cousin, heir, next of kin, executor, administrator, or guardian.
- Keep Irish gentry and peerage research separate unless a documented parentage or place-of-origin bridge is found.

## Myth correction

The proposed colonial chronology is incompatible with online trees that merge this Thomas Selby with a person supposedly born in **1457 at Twizell Castle**. That date is separated from the documented colonial period by roughly two centuries and should be treated as a corrupted or conflated tree entry.

However, disproving the 1457 claim does **not** by itself prove:

- that the 1652 Thomas Solbie and the later Maryland Thomas Selby were the same man;
- that Thomas was born about 1630;
- that he was an indentured servant;
- that Bristol was his birthplace or family origin;
- that Daniel Selby was his brother or cousin;
- or that either man descended from English or Anglo-Irish gentry.

## Evidence rules

1. Cite the original record or a reliable archival image before promoting any lead to documented status.
2. Distinguish a transported-person list from a passenger manifest; Virginia headright claims can record rights asserted after the actual voyage.
3. Distinguish purchase or assignment of headrights from personal transportation aboard the named ship.
4. Do not merge Thomas Selby, Daniel Selby, William Selby of Accomack, or later Worcester/Somerset Selbys without explicit evidence.
5. Maintain the medieval Northumberland Selbys as a separate lineage until a defensible transatlantic bridge is found.

## Priority repositories and record groups

- Library of Virginia land patents and grants
- Northampton and Accomack County order books, deeds, and court records
- Maryland State Archives land-office patents, warrants, certificates, and assignments
- Provincial Court and Council proceedings at St. Mary's City
- Somerset and Worcester County deeds, probate, tax, and court records
- Bristol port, merchant, apprenticeship, and parish records
- Irish deeds, prerogative wills, plantation records, and gentry pedigrees only after an Irish-origin source is identified
