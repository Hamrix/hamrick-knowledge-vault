# Session synthesis: Selby royal-lineage leads and unresolved claims

## Purpose

This note consolidates material supplied and discussed during the 30 July 2026 session that may not yet have been isolated in the vault. It is an audit map, not a pedigree conclusion.

## Core working conclusion

The current vault supports a credible multi-gateway royal-descent research program, but not yet a certification-ready continuous pedigree. The probability that at least one colonial branch eventually reaches medieval royalty may be materially better than the probability that any one currently proposed route is correct.

Do not convert this into a numerical confidence percentage without explicit, independently calibrated probabilities for every parent-child link.

## William Atkinson Selby anchor

Reported identity:

- born about 1757 or 1758 in old Somerset/Worcester County, Maryland;
- married Sarah White Townsend on 28 February 1782;
- died 2 August 1809 according to the Selby-Headen Bible transcription;
- named in a 1780 Worcester County militia list as William Atkinson Selby.

### Military contradiction requiring resolution

The 1929 Selby Family Notes reports:

- Captain Quinton's Company;
- Wicomico Battalion;
- Class 7;
- Worcester County militia, classed 15 July 1780.

A later AI-generated research transcript instead asserted:

- Sinepuxent Battalion;
- Captain William Purnell's company.

The Quinton/Wicomico version presently has the better identified archival provenance. Obtain the original militia-list image and do not merge the two unit descriptions without proof.

## Proposed colonial Selby chain

Working hypothesis:

Thomas Selby Sr. -> Thomas Selby Jr. -> Thomas Selby III, husband of Margaret Schoolfield -> John Selby, husband of Mary Atkinson -> William Atkinson Selby.

Each generation must be tied by wills, deeds, probate distributions, or equivalent primary evidence. The 1929 compilation itself explicitly stated that William Atkinson Selby's father was unknown to its editor and to the cited DAR application; therefore later parentage claims require sources beyond that compilation.

## Thomas Selby Sr. leads

### 1652 Thomas Solbie headright

Claim: a 27 November 1652 Northampton County patent to John Brown near Matchepongo/Phillips Creek lists Thomas Solbie among twenty transported persons.

Open question: whether Thomas Solbie is the later Thomas Selby of Somerset County, Maryland.

Do not infer indentured status, exact age, or English origin merely from inclusion in a headright list.

### 1674 Gibbons-Ridgely-Selby land-right transaction

Claim: Samuel Gibbons of Bristol transferred transportation rights through Robert Ridgely to Thomas Selby, enabling Maryland land acquisition.

Open questions:

- exact ship, passenger count, date, and acreage;
- whether this establishes a personal Bristol connection for Thomas;
- whether the rights were purchased with cash, tobacco, credit, or another arrangement.

The transaction may show capital and legal sophistication but does not by itself establish English gentry origin.

### Simpleton patent

Claim: Simpleton was surveyed in 1666 and patented in 1675, reportedly in Maryland Land Office Liber 15, folio 294.

Proposed geography includes Mattapony Hundred, marsh boundaries, Selbys Hammocks, Watermelon Point, and neighboring Outten property.

Required work:

- obtain the patent image and full transcription;
- map the metes and bounds against historical surveys;
- verify the modern Watermelon Point identification;
- separate later local place-name tradition from the patent's original language.

### 1701/1702 will

Claim: Thomas signed a will on 28 October 1701, probated in 1702, reportedly in Maryland Wills Liber 11, folio 216.

Research targets:

- exact wife and children;
- witnesses and overseers;
- land descriptions;
- overseas property or kin language;
- seals, marks, inventory, and social-status evidence.

No claim of a coat of arms, noble title, or English estate should be made unless visible in the original documents.

## Wife Mary and surname contamination

Online claims reportedly include Mary Arms, Ames, Amis, Cornelius, Roper, Parker, and combinations thereof.

Current safe position:

- Thomas's wife was named Mary in colonial records;
- her maiden name is not presently proved in the vault;
- Arms may be a data-field corruption or transcription error, but Ames/Amis remains only a hypothesis until a source is found;
- Cornelius and Roper may reflect conflated women or prior marriages, but this also requires documentary analysis.

## Daniel Selby and Irish hypothesis

Claim: Daniel Selby held adjacent land and may have had Anglo-Irish associations.

Possible value:

- cluster research may reveal shared witnesses, transport networks, religion, migration, or kinship.

Unproved:

- that Daniel and Thomas were brothers or cousins;
- that either belonged to Anglo-Irish gentry;
- that Irish Selby patents connect them to British peerage.

## English Selby-Neville reality versus American bridge

It is plausible and documentable that some English Selby branches of Biddlestone, Branxton, Twizell, or related northern houses intermarried with Grey, Ogle, Neville, and other noble-descended families.

That fact does not identify Thomas Selby Sr. as a member of those branches.

The principal error to avoid is using a valid medieval Selby-Neville marriage to fill the missing Atlantic bridge by surname alone.

## Alice Neville and Anne Neville confusion

The transcript repeatedly shifted between different women named Alice Neville and Anne Neville.

Separate at minimum:

- Alice Neville, wife of Sir Thomas Grey III;
- Alice Neville, wife of Henry FitzHugh;
- Anne Neville, queen consort of Richard III;
- other Neville women in later collateral pedigrees.

No Selby connection should be accepted until the exact Alice or Anne, spouse, dates, descendants, and marriage path are stated.

## Parker gateway hypothesis

Proposed noble chain:

Edward III -> John of Gaunt -> Joan Beaufort and Ralph Neville -> Alice Neville and Henry FitzHugh -> Elizabeth FitzHugh and William Parr -> Alice Parr and Thomas Vaux -> Nicholas Vaux -> Alice Vaux and Richard Sapcote -> Anne Sapcote and Henry Parker, Baron Morley -> later Barons Morley and William Parker, Lord Monteagle -> an unidentified gentry branch -> George Parker of Accomack.

Critical gap:

The phrase "gentry branch" conceals the decisive generations between the documented Barons Morley and the colonial George Parker.

Also distinguish:

- medieval Barons Morley;
- later Earls of Morley of Saltram;
- unrelated Parker families in Southampton, Staffordshire, Lancashire, and Virginia.

The colonial-to-English identification is not yet proved in the vault.

## Schoolfield or Scholefield gateway hypothesis

Claims include:

- Margaret Schoolfield married Thomas Selby III;
- an older widow Margaret Schofield appears in Somerset County by 1687;
- the surname may connect to Schofield Hall or Rochdale-area armigerous Scholefields;
- a 1664/1665 Lancashire visitation contains a Scholefield pedigree and arms;
- neighboring gentry marriages may eventually reach Neville-descended families.

Required proof:

- identify Margaret's parents in Maryland;
- identify the immigrant Schoolfield or Schofield;
- connect that immigrant to a specific English parish and armigerous pedigree;
- prove every marriage from that pedigree to medieval royalty.

The rarity of a surname is useful for searching but is not genealogical proof.

## Atkinson and Laws collateral route

The AI transcript proposed Mary Atkinson as daughter of Angelo Atkinson and Rachel Laws.

This branch was not developed during the session and remains a distinct research target. It should be checked independently rather than assumed from online trees.

## Townsend and White gateway

A separate note now preserves the Sarah White Townsend, Barkley Townsend, Jeremiah Townsend, Stevens White, Capt. John White, and Caldicott Manor hypothesis.

This route is valuable because it may bypass the Selby Atlantic brick wall entirely, but it currently contains its own unproved colonial and transatlantic links.

## Social status of Thomas Selby Sr.

The session raised a legitimate interpretive question: how could an immigrant with undocumented parentage acquire substantial land and local influence?

Possible explanations include:

- rapid mobility in the tobacco economy;
- land speculation and acquisition of transferable headrights;
- literacy and court-system competence;
- merchant credit and kinship networks;
- marriage into established colonial families;
- prior capital or status not preserved in American records.

These mechanisms explain plausibility but do not identify his European parents or social rank.

Avoid assuming either extreme:

- transported person therefore poor servant;
- large landowner therefore English nobleman.

## Probability framework

The vault may reasonably distinguish:

1. probability that the user has some medieval royal ancestry;
2. probability that at least one colonial gateway in the tree is valid;
3. probability that a particular proposed chain is correct;
4. documentary strength sufficient for publication or lineage-society submission.

Multiple independent gateway candidates increase the chance that one will prove valid, but correlated online-tree claims do not count as independent evidence.

For a chain with several uncertain links, overall confidence is multiplicative. A series of individually plausible relationships can still yield a weak total pedigree.

## Publication rule

Until these bridges are proved, public-facing material should say:

"The research identifies several plausible royal-gateway candidates in the Selby family's colonial network, including Parker, Schoolfield, Townsend, White, Atkinson, and Laws lines. The medieval pedigrees on the English side may be documentable, but the colonial-to-English identifications remain under active audit."

Do not publish "fully verified," "legally proved," "100 percent," or similar language for the Parker, Schoolfield, White, or Thomas Selby royal connections at this stage.
