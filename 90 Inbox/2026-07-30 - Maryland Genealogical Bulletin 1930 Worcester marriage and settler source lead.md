### Worcester County marriage licenses

The January 1, 1930 issue begins with **Worcester County, Md., Marriage Licences, 1795-1865**.

The text says there is no record of marriage licenses in Worcester County before 14 April 1795 because earlier records had burned.

Implication:
- This explains why the 1782 William Atkinson Selby / Sarah White Townsend marriage must be proved from alternate records, such as church records, Bible records, family records, court records, land records, or later compiled sources, not from surviving Worcester marriage-license books.

### Some Early Settlers to Maryland

The Bulletin also includes a section titled **Some Early Settlers to Maryland**, described as names taken from twenty-one volumes of original manuscripts translated from about thirty volumes in 1718, then transcribed and added to by commissioners and chief clerks of the Land Office.

The text states that the list does not include those who were transported or marked service, and that the first figure is the Liber and the second figure the folio, with books in the Land Office of Maryland.

Relevant entries visible in OCR include:
- Anderson, John, of Somerset County, formerly of Virginia, 1672;
- various early Maryland settler entries useful as pattern/source context;
- Atkinson/Attkins-type entries in the A section.

## Relationship to direct William Atkinson Selby project

This source does **not** directly prove the William Atkinson Selby line from the visible passages.

It is valuable because it explains and supports the research environment:

1. Surviving Worcester marriage licenses begin only in 1795, so a 1782 marriage will require alternate evidence.
2. The 1795-1797 Worcester license lists show Selby, Townsend, Atkinson, Schofield/Schoolfield, White, Ennis, and Cottingham surnames active in the county soon after the direct Selby/Townsend generation.
3. The early-settler list and landholder list may point to original land-office and county records worth retrieving.
4. The index references may contain additional Selby/Schoolfield/Atkinson entries elsewhere in the volume.

## Relationship to Prince George's Selby exclusion file

The Calvert County landholder list and Bussey/Busey entries may be useful for the separate William Selby of Prince George's County cluster, especially because that line has ties to Calvert/Patuxent land and a possible Mary Busey/Bussey hypothesis.

However, this is contextual support only. It does not merge the Prince George's Selbys with the Worcester/Somerset William Atkinson Selby branch.
