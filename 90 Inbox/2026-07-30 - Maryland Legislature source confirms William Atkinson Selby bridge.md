# Maryland Legislature source confirms William Atkinson Selby bridge

Status: major authoritative compiled-source confirmation; supporting original records still targeted.

## Purpose

Record the Maryland State Archives / Archives of Maryland entry in *A Biographical Dictionary of the Maryland Legislature, 1635–1789*, volume 426, page 721, because it directly addresses the exact problem in the Selby proof path: whether William Atkinson Selby connects to the Thomas Selby paternal line and the Angelo Atkinson maternal line.

## Source

Source title: *A Biographical Dictionary of the Maryland Legislature, 1635–1789*, by Edward C. Papenfuse et al.

Repository: Maryland State Archives / Archives of Maryland Online.

Volume/page: Archives of Maryland, Volume 426, page 721.

Entry context: `SELBY, WILLIAM, OF JOHN (identity questionable)`.

Important caution: the entry is an authoritative compiled historical-biographical source, not itself the original parish register, deed, probate, or court record. It can be cited as high-quality secondary evidence and used to direct acquisition of the underlying records.

## Exact bridge confirmed by the entry

The entry states that **William Atkinson Selby (1757–?)** was:

- son of **John Selby of Nassawadux, Pocomoke Hundred, Worcester County**;
- indicted by Worcester County Court in June 1779 for `going to the enemy`;
- great-great-grandson of **Thomas Selby (?–1702)**, who immigrated to Maryland from Accomack County, Virginia, by 1674;
- grandson of **John Selby**, who was son of **Thomas Selby (?–1758)** and **Margaret Scholfield**;
- son of **Mary Atkinson**, daughter of **Angelo Atkinson (?–1766)** of Worcester County.

## What this resolves

This resolves several earlier uncertainties in the vault at the compiled-source level:

1. William Atkinson Selby is not merely a same-name descendant hypothesis; the Maryland Legislature biography explicitly identifies his father as John Selby of Nassawadux, Pocomoke Hundred.
2. The text directly connects him to Thomas Selby (?–1702), the Accomack-to-Maryland immigrant.
3. The text identifies the intermediate grandfather as John Selby, son of Thomas Selby (?–1758) and Margaret Scholfield.
4. The text identifies William's mother as Mary Atkinson, daughter of Angelo Atkinson.
5. The 1779 `going to the enemy` indictment belongs to this same William Atkinson Selby identity cluster.
