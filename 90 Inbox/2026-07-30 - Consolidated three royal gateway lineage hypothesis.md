# Consolidated three royal gateway lineage hypothesis

## Status

**Classification:** research-lead synthesis; not a proven pedigree.

This note preserves a proposed three-branch descent structure connecting the modern Selby family to medieval royal and noble families. The medieval peerage portions contain many historically established relationships, but the colonial and modern bridges must still be proved generation by generation from original or near-original records.

No branch below should be treated as verified merely because it appears in an online tree, FamilySearch profile, Ancestry tree, compiled genealogy, or AI-generated lineage.

---

14. Capt. George Parker Jr., reportedly c. 1620–1674, and Florence Cade; proposed immigrant to Accomack County, Virginia, c. 1650
15. John Parker Sr., reportedly c. 1658–1720, and Bridget Sacker; proposed move to Somerset County, Maryland
16. Tabitha Parker, reportedly c. 1688–after 1757, and Thomas Selby III, reportedly c. 1687–1757

18. William Atkinson Selby Sr., reportedly 1757–1828, and Sarah White Townsend

## Assessment

The medieval sequence from Edward III through the Nevilles, FitzHughs, Parrs, Vauxes, Sapcotes, and Parkers is broadly consistent with known noble genealogy, but every specific relationship and title should be sourced individually.

The decisive unproved bridge is the descent from the Barons Morley to George Parker Sr. of Southampton and then to Capt. George Parker Jr. of Accomack. The vault must not assume that a colonial George Parker belonged to the noble Parker family merely because of surname, chronology, geography, or heraldic similarity.

The proposed Tabitha Parker → Thomas Selby III → John Selby → William Atkinson Selby sequence also requires direct probate, deed, Bible, church, or court evidence for each parent-child link.

## Priority evidence targets

- Wills, inquisitions post mortem, visitations, and peerage records for the medieval Parker segment.
- Southampton parish and probate records for George Parker Sr. and Abigail Barlow.
- Accomack and Northampton County records for Capt. George Parker Jr. and Florence Cade.
- Somerset and Worcester County deeds, wills, and orphan-court records for John Parker, Tabitha Parker, Thomas Selby, and John Selby.
- Original evidence proving Tabitha Parker’s parentage.

## Assessment

The Neville, St. Leger, Horsmanden, Byrd, and Beverley families contain documented elite and royal-descended lines, but the proposed White descent must not be accepted without an exact source chain.

The most serious structural concern is the placement of Elizabeth Beverley as wife of a Thomas White of Caldicott Manor and the subsequent identification of Capt. John White of colonial Maryland as their son. These are precisely the kinds of Atlantic-bridge claims that are frequently merged incorrectly in online trees.

The sequence Sarah White → Stevens White → Capt. John White → English Caldicott family remains a hypothesis until original Maryland records and English parish/probate evidence establish identity and parentage.

Barkley Townsend’s historical role in laying out Laurel, Delaware, may be independently supportable, but his exact parentage, wife, political activity, and relationship to Sarah White Townsend require separate evidence.

## Priority evidence targets

- Original will, probate, deed, and church records for Stevens White and Sarah White.
- Marriage or probate evidence connecting Sarah White to Jeremiah Townsend.
- Probate and land evidence for Barkley Townsend and Jemima.
- Immigration, land, and probate records for Capt. John White in Maryland.
- English parish, probate, and manorial records for the Caldicott White family.
- Direct evidence that colonial John White was the son of the proposed English couple.

## Assessment

The medieval descent from Edward III through Thomas of Woodstock, Anne of Gloucester, Edmund Stafford, and Humphrey Stafford is historically established.

The proposed descent after Humphrey Stafford requires careful correction and verification. In particular:

- The identity and parentage of the claimed William Stafford immigrant must be proved.
- The spouse entry “Christopher [Unknown]” is anomalous and may be a corrupted profile field, mistaken given name, or merged person.
- The connection from the immigrant William Stafford to the Blatherwycke Stafford line is not established by the supplied lineage.
- The proposed John → William → Elisha sequence requires original Virginia, Carolina, Mississippi, and Louisiana records.
- The large chronological gap between Elisha W. Stafford III, reportedly born 1833, and Sarah Eveline Stafford, reportedly born 1890, suggests at least one omitted generation unless Elisha was her father late in life. This must be checked directly.

The vault also contains a separate Stafford/Hamrick naming-chain lead involving Stephen Stafford, Elizabeth Peters, Ethelred R. Stafford, John E. Stafford, Eveline M. Hamrick, and Ethelred H. Stafford. That structure may conflict with or replace the Elisha Stafford route. The two Stafford reconstructions must not be merged without evidence.

## Priority evidence targets

- Sarah Eveline Stafford’s birth, marriage, death, census, and obituary records.
- Her parents’ marriage, census household, probate, and succession records.
- James Benjamin Sanford Selby’s parentage and marriage record.
- Original records for each Elisha Stafford generation.
- Records testing the alternative Stephen Stafford–Ethelred Stafford–Hamrick route.
- Passenger, muster, land, probate, and parish records for the 1622 William Stafford.
- English probate and visitation records for any claimed Blatherwycke connection.

Proposed sequence:

1. William Atkinson Selby Sr., reportedly 1757–1828, and Sarah White Townsend, reportedly 1762–1809
2. William Atkinson Selby Jr., reportedly c. 1788–1840, and Mary, maiden name unknown
3. William Henry Selby, reportedly 1829–1912, and Mary Jane Payne; proposed migration from Maryland to New Orleans

5. James Benjamin Sanford Selby, reportedly 1888–1952, and Sarah Eveline Stafford, reportedly 1890–1942
6. Later direct family line

## Assessment

This modern bridge is not yet proved.

The 1929 Selby–Headen Bible transcription previously reviewed in the vault lists a William Selby born 9 June 1791, not a William Atkinson Selby Jr. born c. 1788. Therefore, the identity and parentage of the proposed William Atkinson Selby Jr. remain unresolved.

The links William Atkinson Selby Jr. → William Henry Selby → Frederick Payne Selby → James Benjamin Sanford Selby require original Louisiana and Maryland records. Similar names, migration narratives, and matching chronology are not sufficient.

## Priority evidence targets

- Probate and heir records for William Atkinson Selby Sr.
- Original Bible, baptismal, deed, and court records identifying his children.
- Marriage and death records for the proposed William Atkinson Selby Jr.
- Birth, marriage, census, obituary, and succession records for William Henry Selby.
- Orleans Parish marriage and civil records for Frederick Payne Selby and Elizabeth Dolan.
- Birth, marriage, census, death, and succession records for James Benjamin Sanford Selby.

# Current research conclusion

The supplied structure presents **three serious candidate royal gateways**:

1. Selby/Parker → Neville/Beaufort/Edward III
2. Townsend/White → Neville/St. Leger/Horsmanden/Byrd/Beverley/Edward III
3. Stafford → Anne of Gloucester/Thomas of Woodstock/Edward III

All three ultimately connect to royal families that themselves descend from the House of Wessex and Æthelred II through established medieval royal succession.

However, this does **not** yet prove the user’s personal descent from Æthelred II. The remaining proof burden is concentrated in the colonial and modern parent-child links, especially:

- George Parker of colonial Virginia to the Barons Morley;
- Capt. John White of Maryland to the proposed English Caldicott family;
- Sarah Eveline Stafford to a documented colonial Stafford line and then to the medieval Stafford dynasty;
- William Atkinson Selby’s descendants to the Louisiana Selby family.

Repeated intermarriage and pedigree collapse may eventually reveal multiple descents from the same royal ancestors, but repeated surnames and reused given names are supporting clues, not substitutes for direct evidence.

## Required terminology

Until the missing links are proved, describe this material as:

> A consolidated three-gateway royal-descent hypothesis with established medieval endpoints and unresolved colonial-to-modern bridges.

Do not describe it as an undisputed, academically verified, legally inherited, or fully proven royal pedigree.
