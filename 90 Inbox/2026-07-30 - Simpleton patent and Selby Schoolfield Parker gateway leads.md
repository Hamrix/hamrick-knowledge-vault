# Simpleton patent and Selby–Schoolfield–Parker gateway leads

**Status:** User-supplied research leads; primary-source verification pending  
**Added:** 2026-07-30  
**Research area:** Colonial Selby origins, Maryland land patents, potential English gentry gateways

## Purpose

This note preserves several new leads concerning Thomas Selby Sr., the tract called **Simpleton**, the recurring Alice Neville association in online Selby trees, and three possible routes for bridging the colonial Eastern Shore families to documented English gentry or noble pedigrees.

These leads must remain separated by evidentiary status. A plausible historical context or surname association is not the same as a proved parent-child link.

---

## 1. Simpleton land-patent lead

### Claim supplied

- The tract called **Simpleton** was reportedly surveyed for Thomas Selby Sr. in **1666** and patented in **1675**.
- The cited record is **Maryland Land Office Patent Records, Liber 15, folio 294**.
- The tract is described as lying in **Mattapony Hundred**, old Somerset County, in the area later assigned to Worcester County, Maryland.
- The supplied interpretation places it near coastal marshes and open water, with a marshy point later associated locally with the name **Selbys Hammocks**, possibly near present-day **Watermelon Point** close to the Maryland–Virginia boundary.
- The bounds reportedly use natural and neighboring-property markers, including a marked post, pine, marsh edge, and neighboring Outten-family lines.

### Evidentiary significance

If the patent text and location are confirmed, it would establish a geographically specific colonial footprint for Thomas Selby and would help distinguish his Maryland property history from unrelated English manor traditions.

### Required verification

Obtain and transcribe the complete patent and any related survey, assignment, warrant, rent-roll, resurvey, or later conveyance. Confirm:

1. exact grant date and survey date;
2. acreage;
3. grantee and any assignees;
4. spelling of the tract name;
5. hundred and county;
6. every metes-and-bounds call;
7. neighboring owners;
8. whether “Selbys Hammocks” or “Watermelon Point” appears in a contemporary source or only in later local usage;
9. the relationship, if any, between Simpleton and other Selby tracts.

### Caution

Until the original Liber 15, folio 294 image is examined, the modern geographic identification should be treated as a mapping hypothesis rather than an established fact.

## 2. Alice Neville and the English Selby association

### Historical explanation supplied

English Selby families of Biddlestone, Twizell, and related northern branches appear in pedigrees connected by marriage to aristocratic families descending from the Nevilles and, through them, from Edward III and John of Gaunt.

This provides a credible explanation for why online trees repeatedly place **Alice Neville** or other Plantagenet-descended figures above some English Selby branches.

### Critical distinction

A documented noble descent for a specific English Selby family does **not** prove that Thomas Selby Sr. of Virginia and Maryland belonged to that family.

The present colonial brick wall remains the absence of a primary source connecting Thomas Selby Sr. to:

- Biddlestone;
- Twizell;
- a northern English Selby parent;
- an armigerous Selby pedigree;
- an English inheritance;
- or a recognized coat of arms.

### Online-tree contamination risk

The same given name, approximate chronology, and surname occurring in both English and colonial records may have encouraged automated tree systems to merge distinct men named Thomas Selby. Any claim that the Maryland planter was born into a northern noble house must be rejected unless supported by a document that explicitly bridges the Atlantic.

## 3. Avenue One — Northumberland and Durham Selby papers

### Research objective

Search wills, probate files, recusant records, estate papers, correspondence, settlements, rentals, and pedigrees of the Biddlestone and Twizell Selbys for a Thomas Selby who emigrated to Virginia or Maryland between approximately 1645 and 1660.

### Current supplied assessment

The lead reports that no known surviving will or estate record has yet been found naming Thomas Selby of Virginia as a son or kinsman of the principal northern Selby families.

### What would constitute a breakthrough

A contemporary English record stating any equivalent of:

- “my son Thomas in Virginia”;
- “my kinsman Thomas beyond the seas”;
- a bequest to Thomas Selby in Accomack, Northampton, Somerset, or Maryland;
- a colonial power of attorney tied to a northern estate;
- or an apprenticeship, shipping, probate, or recusancy record that identifies the same colonial man.

### Current classification

**Open but unproved.** The absence of an obvious reference does not establish non-relationship, but it prevents use of the paternal Selby surname as a proven royal gateway.

## 4. Avenue Two — Margaret Schoolfield / Scholefield

### Research objective

Investigate the reported Margaret Schoolfield or Scholefield associated with later colonial Selbys and determine whether she descends from an armigerous Lancashire or Yorkshire Scholefield family.

### Lead supplied

- The surname is associated with the Rochdale area and a place or manor called Scholefield.
- Heraldic visitation material reportedly contains recognized Scholefield pedigrees.
- A “Widow Margaret Schofield” is said to appear in Somerset County, Maryland records by 1687.

### Required proof sequence

The following must be demonstrated independently:

1. the identity of the colonial Margaret Schoolfield/Schofield;
2. her marriage and children;
3. the exact Selby generation through which the user descends;
4. her place of origin;
5. her parents;
6. a documented link to a visitation pedigree;
7. continuity from that pedigree to any Neville or Plantagenet-descended family.

### Caution

Surname rarity is useful for prioritizing research, but it is not proof. Heraldic visitations record particular families, not every person sharing the surname.

### Current classification

**Promising collateral-line lead; unproved.**

## 5. Avenue Three — Parker families of Accomack and Northampton

### Research objective

Determine whether a Parker family that intermarried with the colonial Selbys is itself a documented gateway family descending from English gentry or nobility.

### Claims supplied

- Early Eastern Shore Parkers included men named George, Robert, and John Parker.
- A George Parker is said to have been christened in Southampton, England, in 1634 and to have migrated to Virginia during the English Civil War era.
- The family is claimed to connect to Park Hall in Staffordshire, the Earls of Morley, the Earls of Monteagle, the Nevilles, and descendants of Edward III.
- Selby and Parker families reportedly intermarried repeatedly on the Eastern Shore.

### Major caution

These are high-value claims but presently exceed the evidence preserved in the vault. The phrase “verified gateway ancestor” should not be used until the complete lineage is reconstructed generation by generation from reliable sources.

The following must be proved:

1. which colonial Parker is the immigrant ancestor;
2. his English baptism and parentage;
3. whether he was actually a member of the Park Hall family;
4. every parent-child link from the English immigrant to the Parker who married into the user’s proven Selby line;
5. every generation from Park Hall to the peerage connection;
6. the precise royal-descended ancestor and accepted scholarly source for that descent.

### Current classification

**Potentially high-priority collateral gateway; not yet verified.**

## 6. Proposed lineage model to test

The supplied working model is:

- medieval Neville or royal-descended ancestry;
- an English gentry or peerage-connected Parker or Scholefield line;
- a documented immigrant to the Eastern Shore;
- colonial Parker/Schoolfield intermarriage with the Selbys;
- Thomas Selby III and Margaret Schoolfield;
- John Selby and Mary Atkinson;
- William Atkinson Selby.

This is a hypothesis map only. Each marriage, parent-child relationship, immigrant identity, and English pedigree link must be supported separately.

## 7. Research priorities

### Priority A — Simpleton

- Obtain the original image of Maryland Land Office Liber 15, folio 294.
- Search for the original survey certificate and later tract conveyances.
- Plot the calls against period maps and adjacent patents.

### Priority B — Schoolfield

- Identify the earliest proven Schoolfield/Schofield woman in the colonial Selby line.
- Extract her records from Somerset/Worcester probate, land, tax, church, and court material.
- Only then compare her identity against Lancashire and Yorkshire parish and visitation records.

### Priority C — Parker

- Build the colonial Parker pedigree from original Eastern Shore records before attaching an English pedigree.
- Test the claimed Southampton baptism.
- Verify whether Park Hall, Morley, Monteagle, Neville, and Edward III connections apply to the same Parker line.

### Priority D — Northern Selby papers

- Search archival calendars and catalogues for overseas, Virginia, Maryland, Accomack, or plantation references.
- Examine younger sons, collateral branches, recusant migrations, merchant connections, and wills rather than only the main heirs.

## 8. Working verdict

- The **Simpleton** patent is a concrete and potentially verifiable colonial lead.
- The **Alice Neville** association may be historically valid for some English Selby branches but does not bridge Thomas Selby Sr. to them.
- The **Schoolfield** route is a plausible collateral-line investigation.
- The **Parker** route may be the strongest claimed gateway, but the complete documentary chain has not yet been demonstrated.
- No royal descent should be presented as established from these leads until the colonial-to-English bridge and every intervening generation are proved.
