# William Selby, Thomas Outten, and the Simpeton tract, 1675-1755

## Conclusion

**Update from the original land records:** The complete Somerset deed at **Liber GH, folios 312-313** confirms that on **12 October 1725**, **Thomas Selby and Margaret his wife** conveyed to **William Selby** all the land and marsh then unsold or unconveyed within the 1,250-acre patent called **Simpleton**, estimated at **520 acres, more or less**. The deed recites Thomas Selby's heir-at-law succession from his father and grandfather. This establishes the transfer; it is no longer an index-based inference.

The original Thomas Outten will confirms that **William Selby** received a small parcel of marsh and land in fee simple in 1725. Its boundary ran along the sand gut between the tract called **“Simpaltun”** and William Selby. An earlier Land Office patent identifies the tract as **“Simpleton”** in the record’s margin and **“Simpeton”** in the modern Maryland State Archives tract index: **1,250 acres patented to Thomas Selby in 1675**.

Together, the patent, will, deeds, and 1728 judicial petition establish a documented Simpleton land chain from the original 1675 patent through the Bishop, Hammond, and William Selby transactions. They do **not** establish a parent-child relationship between Thomas and William Selby, and the acreage of every partition is not yet fully reconciled.

## 1. Original will: Thomas Outten to William Selby

Testator: **Thomas Outten of Somerset County**  
Will date: **3 January 1725**  
Probate date: **19 November 1726**  
Record: **Prerogative Court Wills, Liber C.C. No. 2, volume 19, pages 51-52**

The dispositive clause begins on page 51. Clear phrases from the manuscript read:

> “unto William Selby a small parcell of Marsh Land & Trees thereon”

> “the Sand Gutt lying between the Tract of Land called Simpaltun & the said William Selby”

> “running along the said Line South fifty four Degrees Westerly”

> “the said small parcell of Marsh & Land to William Selby & his Heirs for ever.”

The parcel began at a mulberry in the marsh, ran from a watercourse to the sand gut, followed the boundary beside Simpaltun southwest to a small creek, and returned along the creek to Mulberry Point. A few words in the first call are paleographically uncertain; the quoted boundary and fee-simple language are clear.

The will also names Outten’s wife as executrix and refers to children John, Thomas, Samuel, Elizabeth, and Abraham. Witnesses were George Truitt, Abraham Outten, and Samuel Truitt. No kinship term is used for William Selby.

## 2. Original patent: Thomas Selby and Simpeton/Simpleton

The Maryland State Archives Worcester County tract index gives:

- tract: **Simpeton**;
- acreage: **1,250 acres**;
- record type: **Patent**;
- date: **1675**;
- record locator: **Patent Record 19, page 24**;
- index item: **MSA S1602-6006**.

The patent-record transcript begins on book page 24. The marginal tract label on page 25 reads **“Simpleton.”** The body grants the 1,250-acre tract to **Thomas Selby of Somerset County**, his heirs and assigns. Its survey language places it in Somerset County in the Pocomoke/Pitts Creek area and refers to Selby’s Bay and the sea. The patent concludes on page 26 and is dated **1 May 1675**.

### Spelling reconciliation

- **Simpaltun** — spelling/reading in the 1725 Outten will.
- **Simpleton** — marginal label in the patent-record transcript.
- **Simpeton** — controlling searchable spelling in the modern MSA tract index.

These variants refer to the same tract with strong confidence because the 1725 boundary record and the 1675 tract record occur in the same Selby/Pocomoke land context, and no separate tract under the other spellings appears in the MSA tract index. This is a correlation, not a complete chain of title.

## 3. Later William Selby patent-index entries

The statewide patent index contains later Worcester-area entries under William Selby:

- **Selbys Security**, 500 acres, 1734; certificate E.I. 3:240; patent E.I. 4:140.
- **Middle**, 600 acres, 1737; certificate E.I. 3:530; patent E.I. 3:553.
- **Broad Neck**, 210 acres, 1755; certificate B.C. & G.S. 9:343; patent B.C. & G.S. 6:335.

These cards establish later William Selby land activity in the area, but the name alone cannot establish that every entry concerns the William Selby named in the 1725 will. None of the cards explicitly carries the Simpeton/Simpleton name.

## 4. Deed-index breakthrough: Thomas Selby to William Selby

The Maryland State Archives Somerset County grantee index, 1665-1876, directs `Selbe-Selby` researchers to printed index page 242. That page contains the following entry:

- grantee: **William Selby**;
- grantor: **Thomas Selby**;
- deed book: **GH**;
- folio: **312**;
- record serial: **015**;
- instrument: **Deed**.

The original deed at GH 312-313 confirms:

- deed date: **12 October 1725**;
- grantors: **Thomas Selby and Margaret his wife**;
- grantee: **William Selby**, all of Somerset County;
- recitals: the grantor's grandfather held the 1,250-acre patent; the grantor describes himself as heir at law to his father and grandfather;
- property: the unsold land and marsh within the patent called **Simpleton/Singleton**, estimated at **520 acres**, beginning at the sea or water side and binding on Selby Bay;
- consideration: **150 pounds** in the deed's stated current-money form.

The deed continues through folio 313, where Thomas and Margaret Selby sign or mark and acknowledge the conveyance. The 1948 work cites the record as Somerset County Deeds `O-15`/`0-15`, folio 312. The current official locator is **GH 312-313**; `015` is the index serial number, not the liber.

### Earlier partitions in the original deeds

The original grantee index places Thomas Selby's deed to John Bishop at **W.W. 59**. The complete instrument runs through **W.W. 68**. It is dated and acknowledged in June 1677, recites the 1,250-acre Simpleton patent, and conveys a bounded parcel to John Bishop. The acreage clause is faint, but it reads consistently with the 1948 transcription of **250 acres**.

The complete Thomas Selby-to-Edward Hammond deed runs from **W.W. 455 through 469** and is dated **23 May 1679**. It recites the 1,250-acre Simpleton patent, calls Hammond's parcel **one half or moiety**, and describes boundaries involving Selby Bay and land then in John Bishop's possession. No unambiguous 530-acre statement was located in the original pages; **530 acres remains the later source's interpretation or transcription**, not an independently confirmed figure from this reading.

The published figures 250 + 530 + 520 total 1,300 acres, 50 more than the patent. Because the Hammond original describes a moiety but has not yielded a clear 530-acre clause, that arithmetic cannot yet be treated as the legal partition. The discrepancy may reflect the later transcription, survey variation, marsh measurement, overlap, or the meaning of "moiety" in the deed's specific context.

### Corroborating 1728 possession statement

The original June 1728 entry is at **Somerset Judicial Records, 1727-1730, folio 88 verso (Archives of Maryland Online volume 841, page 88b)**. William Selby states that he possesses parts of three tracts, including **Simpleton**, "lying on the north side" of **Durram house**, a tract granted to Henry Bishop. He asks for commissioners to preserve the uncertain bounds; the court grants the petition and appoints commissioners. This independently confirms William's possession and the Simpleton/Durram House adjacency.

## Proof assessment

### Established

- Thomas Selby received the original 1,250-acre Simpeton/Simpleton patent in 1675.
- Thomas Outten’s 1725 will describes William Selby as adjoining Simpaltun and devises him a small marsh-and-land parcel in fee simple.
- The original will spans pages 51-52, not page 51 alone.
- The official Somerset grantee index records a deed from Thomas Selby to William Selby at GH 312.
- GH 312-313 is the 12 October 1725 conveyance to William Selby of the unsold or unconveyed Simpleton land and marsh, estimated at 520 acres more or less.
- W.W. 59-68 is the June 1677 Thomas Selby-to-John Bishop conveyance of a bounded part of Simpleton.
- W.W. 455-469 is the 23 May 1679 Thomas Selby-to-Edward Hammond conveyance of one half or moiety of part of Simpleton.
- The June 1728 judicial entry at folio 88 verso records William Selby's possession of part of Simpleton north of Durram House.

### Strong inference

- “Simpaltun,” “Simpleton,” and indexed “Simpeton” are spelling variants of the same tract.
- The faint acreage clause in W.W. 59-68 reads consistently with the later transcription of 250 acres.

### Not established

- A fully reconciled acreage/title chain for the 1677, 1679, and 1725 partitions.
- The relationship, if any, between Thomas Selby and William Selby.
- The relationship, if any, between Thomas Outten and William Selby.
- Whether the 1734, 1737, and 1755 patent cards all refer to the same William Selby.
