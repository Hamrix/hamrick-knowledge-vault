# FamilySearch Lincolnshire-to-Maryland Selby bridge profiles

Status: research lead only; not accepted as a proved transatlantic identity chain.

## Purpose

Preserve the specific FamilySearch profiles and visible assertions that connect an English Selbye family to Thomas Selby of colonial Maryland. These profiles provide exact names, dates, places, and profile IDs that can be tested against original parish, probate, immigration, land, and court records.

## Profile 1: Elizabeth Morlayne

FamilySearch ID: `L1DP-7TX`

Profile assertions visible in screenshot:
- Styled as "Lady Elizabeth Morlayne."
- Born 28 October 1555, Bishop Middleham, Durham, England.
- Married John Selbie on 14 May 1583 at Bishop Middleham.
- Died in 1657 in Durham, allegedly aged 102.
- Children shown: Robert Selbye, John Selbie, Cuthbert Selbie, Christopher Selby.

Visible source types:
- England Marriages, 1538–1973.
- A second marriage-index entry for John Selbye.
- Ancestry Family Trees.

Evidence cautions:
- The honorific "Lady" is not proved by the visible sources.
- The exact birth date and claimed death at age 102 require original records.
- The profile does not prove parentage, noble status, or connection to the Fenwick/Newcastle Selbys.
- The marriage index may support a marriage event but does not prove the complete reconstructed family group.

## Profile 2: Robert Selbye

FamilySearch ID: `MGZ8-KYC`

Profile assertions visible in screenshot:
- Born 2 February 1583, Bishop Middleham, Durham.
- Parents shown as John Selbie and Elizabeth Morlayne.
- Married Ann Emursun Jones in 1604 at Bluntisham, Huntingdonshire.
- At least ten children.
- Died in 1675 in England, allegedly aged 92.

Visible source types:
- England Births and Christenings, 1538–1975.
- England Marriages, 1538–1973.
- Indexed baptism entries for children.

Profile concerns:
- Robert's stated birth predates the parents' stated marriage in the same year.
- The move from Bishop Middleham, Durham, to Bluntisham, Huntingdonshire, requires identity continuity evidence.
- Duplicate or near-duplicate children may reflect child-name reuse, distinct siblings, or merged family groups.
- The profile does not prove that the Durham Robert was the same man who married in Bluntisham.

## Profile 3: Thomas Selby

FamilySearch ID: `M2ZK-YRQ`

Profile assertions visible in screenshot:
- Born 21 July 1623 at St Peter at Arches Church, Lincoln, Lincolnshire.
- Father shown as Robert Selbye; mother shown as Ann Emursun Jones.
- Married Mary Cornelius Roper after 21 April 1656 in Accomack, Virginia.
- Died 24 February 1702 in Somerset County, Maryland.
- Children shown include Elizabeth, Davis, Grace, Parker, an unnamed Selby daughter, and additional children.

Visible source types:
- An England baptism/christening entry naming Thomas Selby.
- A Delaware church-record item dated 1707–1939.

Evidence cautions:
- The English baptism proves, at most, that a Thomas Selby associated with a Robert existed in Lincolnshire.
- It does not prove that this Thomas was the colonial Thomas Selby of Accomack and Somerset.
- The Delaware church record postdates the claimed 1702 death and cannot by itself establish identity.
- "Mary Cornelius Roper" may combine competing maiden-name hypotheses. The vault already records Mary Arms, Ames, Parker, Cornelius, and Roper as conflicting claims.
- The marriage date phrased as "after 21 April 1656" may be inferred from another event rather than a marriage register.
- Several child profiles are vague or incomplete, suggesting a compiled online-tree family group.

## Exact online-tree hypothesis to test

The profiles collectively assert:

John Selbie + Elizabeth Morlayne
→ Robert Selbye + Ann Emursun Jones
→ Thomas Selby, baptized/born 1623 in Lincoln
→ Accomack County, Virginia by the 1650s
→ Somerset County, Maryland
→ died 1702.

This is a specific, testable hypothesis, not a proven chain.

## Current conclusion

The FamilySearch profiles provide a useful candidate bridge:

> Thomas Selby of colonial Maryland may have been the Thomas Selby baptized in Lincoln in 1623, son of Robert Selbye and Ann Emursun Jones.

The present screenshots do not prove that identity. Keep the English Selbye family and colonial Maryland Selby family separate until an original record establishes continuity across the Atlantic.
