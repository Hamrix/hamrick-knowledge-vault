# Selby kidnapped boy, pirate/privateer, and Townsend rebellion oral tradition audit

Status: active research audit / oral tradition plus targeted public-source search. Not direct proof yet.

## Research question

The user asked to research the family account that an early Selby ancestor was kidnapped at about age 14, sold/indentured to a Maryland planter, later married the planter's daughter, and was later regarded as a pirate because he received letters of marque and preyed on British shipping. The user also noted that this connects to an earlier rebellion lead.

## Source tradition already captured in vault

The oral-history page previously supplied by the user says, in summary:

- An early Selby forefather came to America after being kidnapped as a boy of about **14** on a London street.
- He was shipped to America and sold to a Maryland planter.
- He allegedly worked seven years to pay his indenture.
- He married the daughter of his former owner.
- He later became owner of the estate after his wife's father died.
- During the Revolutionary War he was granted **Letters of Marque** by the Continental Congress and preyed on British shipping.
- He was declared a pirate by the British.

Important: this account comes from oral/family-history material attributed through the Enoch George Selby / Lloyd Selby / Cynthia Selby Price / Hiram Selby branch, not yet a primary record.

### 2. The "pirate" wording likely means British view of an American privateer, not literal criminal piracy

A person operating under a valid commission or **letter of marque** was a privateer under the issuing government, but could be treated as a pirate or rebel by the enemy if captured, especially in wartime.

Therefore the oral phrase "declared a pirate by the British" may preserve a real privateering/private-vessel tradition, but it does not prove criminal piracy.

### 3. The strongest connected record is not privateering yet; it is the Worcester County Townsend rebellion / salt expedition testimony

The key connection to the user's earlier "rebellion" lead is the **1775 Worcester County depositions about William B. Townsend / William Barclay Townsend**.

Relevant public-source records found:

- **Archives of Maryland, Volume 12**, pages 371-372, contains depositions before the Maryland Council of Safety.
- **Joshua Selby** gave a deposition dated **25 November 1775**.
- Joshua Selby said he was at **William B. Townsend's** on Sunday 12 November 1775.
- He heard **Levin Townsend** say he had been aboard the **Man of War** for five days.
- Joshua Selby described being asked to go to Townsend's under the pretext that salt was cheaper there than at Snow Hill.
- At Townsend's he was taken upstairs and sworn to secrecy by Levin Townsend.
- He saw or believed names were being collected for an expedition connected to obtaining salt.
- People named in the testimony include **William Dickerson**, **Benjamin Selby**, **Thomas Moore**, **Thomas Cluff**, **Hugh Vestry**, and **Solomon Butler**.
- The deposition was taken before **John Selby**.

This directly connects the Selby family to the Townsend loyalist/resistance/salt-excursion episode.

### 4. William B. Townsend / Barkley Townsend as "fire-eating Tory"

The 1929 *Selby Family Notes* material says, from the Minnie Andes Linderman / Alberta Brown Latch quotation:

- **William A. Selby married Sarah White Townsend**.
- Her father was **Barkley or Barclay Townsend**, described as a **"fire-eating Tory"**.
- The Newberry Library-derived material listed a local family context including Joshua Selby, Thomas Selby, John Selby, Benjamin Selby, Jesse Selby, Joseph Selby, and William Selby.

This is important because the user's direct William Atkinson Selby line married into the Townsend family associated with Tory/rebellion/salt-expedition material.

### 5. The William Selby / letters of marque search is still unresolved

A search hit in **Naval Documents of the American Revolution, Volume 5** indexes **William Selby** as receiving/from Maryland Council of Safety material, but the indexed entry alone does not prove letters of marque.

The oral tradition says the ancestor received Letters of Marque from the Continental Congress and preyed on British shipping. So far, the strongest concrete revolutionary records found are:

- William Atkinson Selby / William Selby militia-type references in family notes;
- Joshua Selby deposition tied to the Townsend salt episode;
- Maryland militia commissions and Newberry Library references in the 1929 compilation;
- a possible unrelated or not-yet-linked **William Selby Jr. of Pitts Landing, Virginia**, noted in Founders/Washington context as possibly a captain attached to the French fleet by June 1778.

The actual privateer commission / letter-of-marque record for a Selby has not yet been found.

## Interpretation

The oral tradition may be a composite of several separate historical layers:

1. **Immigrant/indenture layer**
   - kidnapped boy from London;
   - sold to Maryland planter;
   - seven-year service;
   - marriage to planter's daughter.
   - No primary record yet.

2. **Privateering/pirate layer**
   - letters of marque;
   - British called him pirate;
   - possibly a real Revolutionary privateer tradition, but unnamed and unverified.

3. **Townsend rebellion/Tory salt-expedition layer**
   - William B. / Barkley Townsend and sons;
   - Levin Townsend aboard a man-of-war;
   - armed secret salt expedition;
   - Joshua Selby deposition;
   - Benjamin Selby named among participants;
   - John Selby taking deposition.
   - This layer has documentary support in Archives of Maryland.

4. **William Atkinson Selby direct-line layer**
   - William A. Selby married Sarah White Townsend;
   - Sarah's father appears in family tradition as Barkley/Barclay Townsend, a Tory;
   - this connects the user's direct line to the Townsend political-conflict context, but not necessarily to the kidnapped/privateer story unless a record names the same Selby.

## Why the rebellion connection matters

The rebellion-like material is not vague. The Council of Safety depositions show a local wartime conflict in Worcester County involving secrecy oaths, arms, a man-of-war, salt, and refusal to submit to Committee authority. This is precisely the kind of political conflict that later family memory could compress into labels like Tory, rebel, pirate, privateer, or outlaw.

However, the evidence currently points most strongly to the Townsend side and allied/neighboring Selbys, not yet to the kidnapped-ancestor story.

## Working conclusion

The account is worth serious pursuit but should be divided into separately provable claims:

- **Claim A:** A Selby ancestor was kidnapped from London at about age 14 and sold/indentured in Maryland. Status: oral tradition only.
- **Claim B:** That person married the planter's daughter and inherited/controlled the estate. Status: oral tradition only.
- **Claim C:** A Selby or related ancestor received letters of marque and was regarded by the British as a pirate/privateer. Status: plausible but unverified; no direct commission found yet.
- **Claim D:** The direct family was tied to Worcester County Revolutionary conflict through the Townsend family and Selbys. Status: strongly supported by Archives of Maryland depositions and Selby Family Notes.
