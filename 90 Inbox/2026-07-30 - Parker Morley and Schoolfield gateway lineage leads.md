# Parker–Morley and Schoolfield gateway-line leads

Status: user-supplied research leads; documentary verification pending
Date added: 2026-07-30

## Purpose

Preserve two proposed transatlantic gateway-line avenues connected to the Eastern Shore Selby network while keeping proven peerage descents separate from the unproven colonial bridges.

---

## Avenue A — Proposed George Parker of Accomack gateway

### Claimed colonial anchor

- George Parker Jr., said to have lived approximately 1620–1674.
- Said to have immigrated to Accomack County, Virginia, around 1650.
- Claimed father: George Parker Sr. of Southampton, England.

### Proposed royal descent

The supplied lead gives this sequence:

1. King Edward III
2. John of Gaunt, Duke of Lancaster
3. Joan Beaufort = Ralph Neville, 1st Earl of Westmorland
4. Alice Neville = Henry FitzHugh, Lord FitzHugh
5. Elizabeth FitzHugh = Sir William Parr
6. Alice Parr = Sir Thomas de Vaux
7. Sir Nicholas de Vaux, 1st Baron Vaux of Harrowden
8. Alice de Vaux = Sir Richard Sapcote
9. Anne Sapcote = Henry Parker, 10th Baron Morley
10. Sir Henry Parker, 11th Baron Morley = Elizabeth Stanley
11. Edward Parker, 12th Baron Morley = Elizabeth Stanley of Monteagle
12. William Parker, 13th Baron Morley / Lord Monteagle
13. An unspecified gentry branch
14. George Parker Sr. of Southampton
15. George Parker Jr. of Accomack

### Important peerage distinction

Do not confuse:

- the medieval Barons Morley, created in 1299; and
- the later Earls of Morley of Saltram, created in 1815.

The proposed descent concerns the medieval baronial family, not the Saltram earls.

### Evidence status

The medieval sequence from Edward III through the Neville, FitzHugh, Parr, Vaux, Sapcote, and Parker families may be independently documentable in peerage and heraldic sources. However, the decisive colonial bridge remains unproved in this note.

The following must be established with primary or high-quality scholarly sources before this can be treated as a gateway line:

- George Parker Jr.’s identity, dates, and residence in Accomack;
- his documented parentage;
- the identity and pedigree of George Parker Sr. of Southampton;
- every generation between the Barons Morley and George Parker Sr.;
- the exact marriage or descent connecting the user’s documented Eastern Shore ancestry to this Parker branch.

Until those links are demonstrated, the statement that the line is “fully validated” or that the user “legally captures” the royal bloodline is not accepted as proved.

## Avenue B — Margaret Schoolfield / Scholefield of Lancashire

### Claimed colonial anchor

- Margaret Schoolfield or Schofield, reportedly a widow in Somerset County, Maryland, by 1687.
- Proposed role: paternal grandmother or earlier ancestress in the William Atkinson Selby line.
- Reportedly possessed substantial liquid wealth or property.

### Proposed English origin

The lead associates the surname with the Schofield or Scholefield family of Rochdale, Lancashire, and with Schofield Hall near Hollingworth.

Claimed evidence includes:

- a 1314 deed witnessed by Thomas de Schofield;
- recognition of a Scholefield pedigree during the 1664/1665 Visitation of Lancashire under Sir William Dugdale;
- armorial bearings described as a fess between three bulls’ heads;
- gentry marriage networks involving neighboring Lancashire families such as Radcliffe, Assheton, Byron, Molyneux, and Neville.

### Evidence status

The existence of an armigerous Scholefield family in Lancashire does not by itself identify Margaret Schoolfield of Maryland as a member of that house. The claim that every bearer of the surname descends from one manor is also too broad unless supported by specialized surname and manorial research.

Required bridge evidence includes:

- a Maryland record identifying Margaret’s maiden name, husband, children, or place of origin;
- probate, land, court, or church records establishing her relationship to the Selbys;
- a passenger, will, administration, deed, or correspondence record connecting her to Lancashire;
- a precise place in the 1664/1665 visitation pedigree;
- proof of every generation from the visitation family to Margaret;
- then, if relevant, proof of any descent from Neville or Plantagenet-connected gentry.

### Priority archival search

Search Prerogative Court of Canterbury and Lancashire probate records, approximately 1660–1690, for Scholefield/Schofield testators whose wills mention:

- Maryland, Virginia, or the Chesapeake;
- a daughter or widow Margaret;
- heirs overseas;
- funds, goods, or property sent to the province of Maryland.

Also examine Somerset County, Maryland court, land, probate, and tax records for Margaret Schoolfield/Schofield and variant spellings.

## Comparative assessment

### Parker avenue

Potential strength: the upper peerage chain may be well documented.

Primary weakness: a large, currently unspecified gap between William Parker, Lord Monteagle, and George Parker of Accomack.

### Schoolfield avenue

Potential strength: a distinctive surname and a known armigerous Lancashire family may make targeted archival work possible.

Primary weakness: no demonstrated identity between the Maryland widow and the Rochdale gentry family.

## Research rule

A documented royal descent on the English side is not evidence of descent for the colonial family unless every parent-child or equivalent genealogical link across the Atlantic is proved. Treat both avenues as research hypotheses, not established ancestry.
