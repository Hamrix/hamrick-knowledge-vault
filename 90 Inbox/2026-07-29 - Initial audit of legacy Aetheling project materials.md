# Initial audit of legacy Aetheling project materials

## Scope

This note compares the current vault-backed evidence state with legacy project files supplied outside the repository, including the Selby Manor evidence bundle, annotated pedigree, master-lineage records, harmonised portfolio, Selby–de Lacy dossier, and Whitehouse–Steward–Selby backup.

The legacy files are treated as **unverified research inputs**, not as authority-level evidence. Their citations, archival references, certificates, transcripts, land-title identifiers, DNA assertions, and legal conclusions must be independently inspected before promotion.

## Conclusion

The legacy project materially overstates what is established. The vault currently supports bounded historical relationships among named medieval and royal people, but it does not yet support a continuous lineage from Hamrick Ethelered Selby II to Stafford, Plantagenet, Wessex, Selby Manor, or a surviving manorial title.

The public project should not presently describe the complete descent, manor ownership, lordship, mineral rights, institutional filings, DNA triangulation, or 97%+ confidence scores as verified facts.

## Highest-priority conflicts

### 1. Continuous modern-to-medieval lineage is not established

**Legacy claim:** A direct and effectively complete lineage joins the modern Selby family to medieval Selby/de Lacy, Stafford, Plantagenet, and Wessex lines.

**Vault state:** Only isolated historical parent-child or marriage links are confirmed. No documented chain connects those historical people to the modern family.

**Classification:** unsupported / potentially misleading when stated as proven.

### 2. Modern Selby generations conflict across project files

Examples requiring identity-level reconciliation include:

- Frederic Selby shown as born 1930 and dying 1998 in one pedigree;
- Frederic Ethelered Selby shown as born 1919 in a master record;
- project memory and other research have also associated Frederic Ethelred Selby with 1911–1992;
- Hamrick Ethelered Selby I appears as born 1960 in one file and 1949 in another;
- the relationship labels for father, grandfather, and great-grandfather vary between documents.

**Classification:** contradicted internally.

**Required action:** establish the modern chain first from certificates, census, obituary, Social Security, probate, military, and cemetery records.

### 3. Selby Manor legal and ownership claims exceed the evidence shown

Legacy files assert or imply:

- ownership or first-registration entitlement to The White House at Cliffe;
- a title number `NYK12345`;
- surviving lordship, seignorial, mineral, and court-baron rights;
- no competing proprietor;
- a £2.1 million RICS valuation;
- formal or pending filings and institutional liaison.

Within the supplied legacy text, several identifiers read as placeholders or assertions rather than authenticated records. The bundle includes an unsigned archivist certification template and language instructing that a signature or covering letter should later be annexed.

**Classification:** unverified; high legal and reputational risk.

**Required action:** obtain official HMLR search results, title plans, deed copies, Manorial Documents Register results, actual archival reproductions, certified translations, surveyor report, and solicitor advice. Do not describe an application as lodged unless a receipt or case reference exists.

### 4. Archival references and transcripts require authentication

Examples include the alleged 1703 Whitehouse manorial court roll, 1666 hearth-tax entry, 1827 enfranchisement schedule, 1926 conveyance, Pipe Roll references, Durham Liber Vitae material, and IPM references.

The legacy bundle presents some texts as certified while also showing blank signature fields. A typed Latin passage is not evidence that the cited manuscript contains that passage.

**Classification:** source claimed, document not yet authenticated in the vault.

### 5. Confidence scores are not reproducible

Legacy documents use composite scores such as 96.8%, 97.2%, and 97.9%, but no defensible statistical or genealogical scoring method is preserved. Confidence in separate links cannot be multiplied or averaged into proof of an entire lineage without a transparent rubric, and a single broken parent-child link defeats continuity regardless of the average.

**Classification:** unsupported metric.

**Required action:** replace numeric percentages with vault labels: `confirmed`, `strong`, `moderate`, `weak`, `speculative`, or `contradicted`, each with a written rationale.

### 6. DNA claims do not prove medieval or manorial descent as presented

Legacy materials cite an Ancestry/GEDmatch cluster, chromosome segment, LOD value, triangulation, and heir-at-law affidavit. No raw match data, tester identities, documented common ancestors, segment files, methodology, or consent-safe evidence is in the vault.

Even genuine autosomal matches ordinarily cannot independently identify a specific ancestor many centuries ago without a well-supported documentary network.

**Classification:** unverified and overstated.

### 7. Surname, title, and naming tradition are being used as lineage evidence

The vault supports historical meanings and habitational origins for Æþeling, Selby, and Stafford. It explicitly does not support inferring descent from a surname, the middle name Ethelred, or the style “Last Ætheling.”

**Classification:** symbolic/family tradition unless independently documented.

### 8. Woden and early royal genealogies must be separated from historical descent

Wodenic material belongs in a mythic or dynastic-tradition layer, not in the scored genealogical chain. Early West Saxon genealogies also require explicit historiographical caveats.

**Classification:** mythic-protohistorical tradition, not biological proof.

### 9. Bona Vacantia strategy is legally misconceived in the legacy files

A shared surname or remote descent from an early family does not create entitlement to unrelated modern estates. Bona Vacantia heirship turns on descent from the deceased person under applicable succession rules, supported by a complete civil-record chain.

**Classification:** unsupported and legally hazardous.

### 10. Heraldic and noble-status assertions require separate treatment

Descent from an armiger, even when proven, does not automatically establish a right to use particular arms. “Lord,” “Last Ætheling,” orders, ceremonial rights, and sovereign language should be identified as modern self-styling or cultural practice unless recognized by a competent authority.

**Classification:** ceremonial identity, not verified legal status.

## Material that can presently be retained as factual context

- Æþeling was a historical designation associated with eligible princes of an English royal house.
- The vault confirms discrete royal and medieval relationships already recorded in the project index.
- Humphrey Stafford’s descent from Edward III through Anne of Gloucester is supported for those named medieval individuals.
- Selby and Stafford have documented habitational surname origins, but those origins do not identify the user’s ancestors.
- Legacy trees, GEDCOMs, family narratives, and AI reports remain useful research leads when their provenance is preserved.

## Website audit rule

Every factual statement should map to one of four visible states:

1. **Documented** — supported by inspected reliable evidence.
2. **Supported hypothesis** — evidence points toward the claim but gaps remain.
3. **Family tradition / symbolic identity** — meaningful but not documentary proof.
4. **Disputed or withdrawn** — contradicted, misidentified, legally incorrect, or based on an unauthenticated source.

No page should use “verified,” “confirmed,” “unbroken,” “direct heir,” “ownership,” “filed,” or a numerical confidence score unless the underlying evidence note supports that exact wording.
