# William Selby 1732 Prince George's will transcript

Status: uploaded will transcript; high-value primary-record transcript lead; original image still should be checked.

## Purpose

Preserve the user-uploaded transcript of the will of **William Selby of Prince George's County, Maryland**, dated 8 October 1732 and proved 1 August 1733. This record is critical for tracing the next generation of the western-shore Prince George's Selby cluster after William Selby Sr. died in 1698/9.

## Source as supplied

The uploaded transcript gives:

- Date range: **8 Oct. 1732 – 1 Aug. 1733**
- Record reference: **Book T-1, folio 165**
- Testator: **William Selby of Prince George's County, Maryland, planter**
- Wife/executrix: **Elizabeth Selby**
- Witnesses: **Henry Boteler**, **Charles Boteler**, **Thomas Hodgkins**
- Probate endorsement: 1 August 1733; Henry Butler/Boteler and Thomas Hodgkins appeared.

## Children named in the will

The will names the following children:

- **William Magruder Selby**
- **Sarah Selby**
- **Susannah Selby**
- **Samuel Selby**
- **Joseph Selby**
- **Thomas Selby**
- **John Selby**
- **Nathan Selby**

## Wife and executrix

William names his wife **Elizabeth Selby** as his dear and loving wife and appoints her full and sole executrix.

Important interpretation:
- This supports a later wife named Elizabeth.
- It does not by itself resolve whether William earlier married Sarah Magruder, but the son **William Magruder Selby** strongly supports a Magruder maternal or kinship connection.
- Elizabeth's maiden name remains unproved from this transcript alone.

## Land devised

### Chew's Folly

- To son **William Magruder Selby**: 200 acres of land, part of a tract bought from **Mr. Joseph Chew**, called **Chew's Folly**, on the fork of the Eastern Branch in Prince George's County.
- To daughters **Sarah Selby** and **Susannah Selby**: the remaining 184 acres of Chew's Folly, equally divided, with survivorship language and reversion to William Magruder Selby if both died without issue.

### Leith

The will continues the Leith land from the earlier William Selby Sr. estate.

- To son **Samuel Selby**: 200 acres, part of the tract called **Leith**, laid out on the west part, including the improvements on that part.
- If Samuel died without issue, the land was to be divided among sons **Joseph**, **Thomas**, and **John Selby**.
- To son **Joseph Selby**: 100 acres of Leith, laid out between the upper and lower parts.
- If Joseph died without issue, the tract was to be divided between sons **Thomas** and **John Selby**.
- To son **John Selby**: 200 acres, the remaining part of **Leith** lying next to the Patuxent River, being the part where William then dwelled, subject to wife Elizabeth's life possession and use without waste.
- If John died without issue, the land was to be divided between sons **Joseph** and **Thomas Selby**, subject to Elizabeth's life use.

### Ossax / Essex Lodge

- To son **Thomas Selby**: all the tract called **Ossax Lodge** / likely **Essex Lodge**, adjoining the land where William lived, containing 150 acres.
- If Thomas died without issue, the tract was to be divided between sons **Joseph** and **John Selby**.

### Toogood / Towgood

- To son **Nathan Selby**: all the tract called **Toogood**, lying on the west side of the Patuxent River in the woods.
- The will states this was the same tract William formerly bought from **his brother Samuel Selby**.

This is a particularly important internal-kinship clause because it identifies the testator's brother Samuel and confirms that Toogood/Towgood moved within the same Prince George's Selby sibling group.

## Trustees

William appoints friends:

- **[given name unclear] Magruder**
- **Thomas Hodgkin**

as trustees to see the will executed and to prevent waste or improper division of the estate during his children's minority or vulnerability.

The Magruder trustee reference should be checked against the original image. It may be a key clue to the earlier Magruder marriage connection.

## Proof impact

This will directly strengthens the internal western-shore Prince George's Selby branch:

William Selby Sr., died 1698/9
→ probable son William Selby, died 1733
→ children William Magruder, Sarah, Susannah, Samuel, Joseph, Thomas, John, Nathan
→ continued ownership and division of **Leith**, **Essex/Ossax Lodge**, **Toogood/Towgood**, and **Chew's Folly**.

It also confirms that the younger William had a brother **Samuel Selby**, because the Toogood clause says William bought that tract from `my brother Samuel Selby`.

## Relationship to the Somerset/Worcester Selby project

The uploaded will continues to point away from the Somerset/Worcester line:

- The land remains in Prince George's County and the Patuxent/Eastern Branch network.
- The named children do not match the William Atkinson Selby / John Selby / Thomas Selby / Margaret Schoolfield chain.
- The will carries forward land from the Prince George's William Selby Sr. estate rather than lands such as Benefield, Nassawadox/Pocomoke Hundred, Bogerternorton, or other Worcester/Somerset tracts.

Current interpretation: this is strong evidence for a separate, well-documented Prince George's western-shore Selby cluster.
