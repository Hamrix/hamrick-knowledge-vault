## Etheldred Peters — 1786 original-image correction

A high-resolution image of the 1786 Swift Creek tax list was retrieved from Google Drive.

### Source identity

- Craven County, North Carolina
- Swift Creek tax district
- Treasurer's and Comptroller's Papers, County Settlements with the State, Settlements of Individuals
- Filename: `Treasurer's and Comptroller's Papers_County Settlements with the State_Settlements of Individuals_1786_SR.204.11.50_3.jpg`
- Google Drive file ID: `1QIxPNKFR-W8K6iIUe5E5gIoF1Wqw7D8p`
- Image size observed in session: 13,434 × 9,658 pixels; approximately 17 MB.

### Correct reading

The entry begins `Etheldred Peters his Inventory` and reports three polls. The operative language reads approximately:

> three polls my self son John Peters and one negro

The numerical structure identifies:

1. Etheldred himself;
2. his son John Peters;
3. one enslaved person.

### Corrected conclusion

- **Documented:** Etheldred Peters had a son named John Peters in his 1786 household.
- **Documented:** the household assessment included one enslaved person.
- **Supported:** Etheldred was assessed for about 400 acres at his residence and about 120 acres near Little Contentnea/Contentney or Warehouse Landing.
- **Unresolved:** Etheldred's parents.
- **Withdrawn:** the interpretation that this tax list calls Etheldred a “son of John Peters.”

The source does not name Betsey or Elizabeth Peters and does not prove she was Etheldred's daughter.

## Major Selby colonial break

Current evidence forms a geographic and land-title cluster but does not prove parentage:

- 1783 Maryland assessment: John Selby held Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged, totaling 855 acres.
- 1783 assessment: Major Selby held part of Choice, 140 acres.
- 1797 chancery: Daniel Patrick et al. v. Major Selby, filed 22 December 1797; involved sale of Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged; Maryland State Archives S512-6-4300.
- 1810 chancery: George Hall v. William Selby of Zadock; concerned the estate of Zadock Selby and the same tract pair; Maryland State Archives S512-3-2427.

The recurring land names make John, Major, Zadock, and William part of a strong research cluster, but they do not establish that John or Zadock was Major's father. Required records include the full chancery packets, wills, administrations, deeds, and distributions.
