# William Selby Alderminster baptism, immigration, and Prince George's probate identity cluster

Status: mixed evidence cluster. The Prince George's County will is a contemporary primary record for the Maryland testator; identity with the Alderminster child and 1659 immigrant remains unproved.

## Purpose

Preserve and audit the six sources attached to FamilySearch profile `L6TT-KK3`, styled William Selby Sr., male, about 1645 to before 25 February 1699. The source set appears to combine an English baptism, a 1659 Maryland immigration-index entry, and original late-seventeenth-century Prince George's County probate records. Because William Selby is a recurring name in several unrelated Selby branches, the records must be evaluated as separate events until identity continuity is proved.

This index supports the existence of a male child recorded as Willia. Selby, son of James and Mary, christened at Alderminster in 1630. It does not show that this child survived, emigrated, or became the Prince George's County testator. The exact original parish entry remains an acquisition target.

## Source 2: 1659 immigration-index entry

Title: `Passenger and Immigration Lists Index, 1500s–1900s - William Selby`.

Source date: 1659.

Underlying cited work:

> Gust Skordas, editor, *The Early Settlers of Maryland: An Index to Names of Immigrants, Compiled from Records of Land Patents, 1633–1680, in the Hall of Records, Annapolis, Maryland* (Baltimore: Genealogical Publishing Co., 1968; reprinted 1986), 525 pages.

FamilySearch attachment note: `1659 immigration citation for William Selby.`

### Evidence assessment

This is a derivative index pointing back to Maryland land-patent records, not a passenger list in the modern sense. The original patent or land-right entry must be located. Until the underlying record is read, the entry does not establish:
- age;
- birthplace;
- vessel;
- family group;
- destination within Maryland;
- whether William transported himself or was claimed by another person;
- identity with either the Alderminster child or the later Prince George's County testator.

### Will chronology

- Testator: William Selby of Prince George's County, Maryland.
- Will dated: 5 November 1698.
- Recorded/proved: 25 February 1698/9.

The dual-year notation reflects the English legal calendar then in use. It should not be simplified in a way that creates a false one-year discrepancy.

### Named family members and heirs

The attached source notes report the will named:
- wife Mary Selby;
- daughter Mary, wife of Robert Sallers;
- daughter Amee/Amy Hacker or Hucker;
- son William Selby;
- son Charles Selby;
- daughter Susannah Selby;
- son Samuel Selby.

Spelling must be checked against the original handwriting. In particular, `Sallers`, `Hacker`, and `Hucker` may have variant readings.

### Land and property

Reported abstract details include:
- 100 acres called `Twyford` devised to daughter Amy Hucker;
- 200 acres on the Western Branch devised to son-in-law Robert Sallers and his wife Mary.

These tract and location references are high-value identifiers. They can be followed through patents, deeds, rent rolls, resurvey records, and later probate to reconstruct the family and distinguish this branch from Somerset/Worcester County Selbys.

### Evidence assessment

The will is a contemporary primary source and establishes, subject to accurate transcription:
- a William Selby living in Prince George's County in 1698;
- wife Mary;
- the named children and married daughter;
- a son-in-law Robert Sallers;
- ownership or disposal of Twyford and Western Branch land;
- death before probate on 25 February 1698/9.

The will does **not** establish:
- the testator's birth year or birthplace;
- that his father was James Selby;
- that he was the Alderminster child baptized in 1630;
- that he was the William Selby in the 1659 land-right index;
- relationship to Thomas Selby/Solbie of Accomack and Somerset;
- relationship to William Atkinson Selby.

## Candidate identity sequence

The FamilySearch profile appears to imply:

James Selby + Mary
→ Willia. Selby christened 1630 at Alderminster
→ William Selby appearing in a 1659 Maryland immigration/land-patent index
→ William Selby of Prince George's County
→ will dated 5 November 1698 and proved 25 February 1698/9.

The chronology is possible, but no presently preserved record proves any of the identity arrows.

## Relationship to the principal Maryland Selby project

This Prince George's County household must remain separate from:
- Thomas Selby/Solbie of Accomack and Somerset;
- the proposed Lincolnshire-to-Maryland Thomas Selby line;
- William Selby, son of Thomas Selby III and Margaret Schoolfield;
- William Atkinson Selby, born 1757/1758;
- the Newcastle William Selby who married Elizabeth Fenwick.

Prince George's County is geographically distinct from the core Somerset/Worcester research. A relationship remains possible, especially through earlier Maryland migration, but surname, chronology, and shared given names are insufficient.

## Research paths created by the will

1. Transcribe the full will from images 7–8, including witnesses, executor, residual clauses, debts, and signatures or marks.
2. Examine image 34 to determine whether it is an inventory, account, bond, distribution, or later probate entry.
3. Locate the patent and deed history of `Twyford`.
4. Identify the 200-acre Western Branch property and trace Robert Sallers and Mary Selby.
5. Trace Amy/Amee Hucker or Hacker and her spouse.
6. Trace sons William, Charles, and Samuel and daughter Susannah in Prince George's records.
7. Search inventories, administration bonds, final accounts, guardianships, and distributions.
8. Compare witnesses, neighbors, and tract names with the 1659 Skordas entry and other Selby land-right claims.
9. Search whether any heir later moved to Somerset, Worcester, Delaware, Virginia, or North Carolina.
10. Obtain the original Alderminster register image and English probate for James Selby or Mary.

## Current conclusion

The strongest result is now clear:

> William Selby of Prince George's County left a contemporary will dated 5 November 1698 and proved 25 February 1698/9, naming wife Mary and children Mary Sallers, Amy Hucker, William, Charles, Susannah, and Samuel, with property including Twyford and land on the Western Branch.

That family unit is documentable and should be reconstructed independently. The attached 1630 Alderminster baptism and 1659 Maryland immigration index remain plausible identity leads, but neither presently proves the English origin of the Prince George's County testator or a connection to the Somerset/Worcester Selby line.
