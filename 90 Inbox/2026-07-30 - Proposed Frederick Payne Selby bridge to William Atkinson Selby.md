# Proposed Frederick Payne Selby bridge to William Atkinson Selby

## Status
Research lead only. The Louisiana generations may be real and independently documentable, but the asserted bridge to William Atkinson Selby and Sarah White Townsend is not established by the evidence presently cited.

## User-supplied proposed chain

William Atkinson Selby (1757–1809) = Sarah White Townsend
→ William Atkinson Selby Jr. (claimed c. 1788–1840)
→ William Henry Selby (claimed 1829–1912)
→ Frederick Payne Selby (claimed 1859–1921)
→ Benjamin Selby = Sarah Stafford

## Claimed Louisiana anchor

### Frederick Payne Selby
- Claimed born August 1859 in New Orleans, Louisiana.
- Claimed married Elizabeth Dolan on 20 December 1884 in Orleans Parish.
- Claimed died in 1921 in New Orleans.
- Proposed as father of Benjamin Selby.

### William Henry Selby
- Claimed born April 1829 in Worcester County, Maryland.
- Claimed died 23 June 1912 in New Orleans.
- Proposed as father of Frederick Payne Selby and as the migration bridge from Maryland to Louisiana.

### William Atkinson Selby Jr.
- Claimed born c. 1788 in Worcester County, Maryland.
- Claimed died c. 1840.
- Proposed as son of William Atkinson Selby and Sarah White Townsend and father of William Henry Selby.

## Critical conflicts

1. The 1929 Selby–Headen Bible transcription lists a son only as **William Selby**, born 9 June 1791, not a William Atkinson Selby Jr. born c. 1788.
2. The same Bible-derived child list does not establish that this William was the father of a William Henry Selby born in 1829.
3. The asserted Worcester County birth of William Henry Selby and his migration to New Orleans require direct records.
4. Benjamin Selby’s exact identity, dates, spouse, and relationship to Frederick Payne Selby must be proved before this chain can be attached to the user’s modern lineage.
5. The statement that this chain “secures” Parker, White, Neville, or Edward III gateways is premature because those gateway lines themselves remain under investigation.

- Obtain Benjamin’s birth, marriage, and death records.

### William Henry Selby → Frederick Payne Selby
- Obtain Frederick’s civil or church baptism record naming both parents.
- Locate William Henry’s household in the 1850–1910 censuses.
- Obtain William Henry’s 1912 death record, obituary, succession, and cemetery record.
- Identify spouse and all children to distinguish him from same-name Selbys.

### William Selby → William Henry Selby
- Identify the William Selby born 9 June 1791 from the family Bible in tax, census, deed, marriage, and probate records.
- Determine whether he ever used the middle name Atkinson.
- Establish whether he migrated from Maryland/Kentucky toward Louisiana.
- Prove William Henry’s parentage through a contemporary record rather than surname continuity.

## Publication-safe conclusion

Frederick Payne Selby may provide a valuable Louisiana anchor, but the proposed route from him through William Henry Selby to a son of William Atkinson Selby is currently a hypothesis. No royal gateway should be inherited through this route until each Louisiana-to-Maryland generation is independently documented.
