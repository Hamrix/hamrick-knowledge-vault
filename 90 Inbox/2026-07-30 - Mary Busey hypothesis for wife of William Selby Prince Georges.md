# Mary Busey hypothesis for wife of William Selby of Prince George's County

Status: strong compiled-record hypothesis; original probate and administration records required before treating Mary Busey as proved wife.

## Purpose

Preserve the user-supplied pages headed **Mary Busey's Storey** and related estate-record excerpts. These pages argue that the wife of William Selby of Prince George's County was probably **Mary Busey**, not Mary Stewart/Steward, and that she later married **Joshua Cecil/Cecill**.

This note is a companion to the larger Prince George's Selby sons and land-trace note.

## Source as supplied

The user supplied three photographed pages. The pages appear to be a compiled research note posted 28 June 2018 by `CaroleB1524`, with copied estate-record extracts and references to Carol Collins, the Busey website, Owingsstone, and Prince George's County probate material.

This is not an original record image, but it quotes or abstracts several original-record targets.

## Core claim

The supplied pages propose that:

- **Mary Busey** was born about 1660.
- She was likely the daughter of **Paul Busey** and widow **Susanna Busey**.
- She married **William Selby** about 1680.
- William Selby died in 1698/1699 in Prince George's County, Maryland.
- Mary then married **Joshua Cecil/Cecill** before 30 July 1701.
- Mary was pregnant with **Ann Selby** when William Selby died.
- Mary inherited or held the 100-acre tract **The Farme** from William Selby; after her remarriage to Joshua Cecil, Ann Selby received part or all of The Farme.
- Mary died between 1712 and 1717 or by 1718 depending on the estate-account interpretation.

## Evidence arguments preserved

### 1. Paul Busey estate-administration clue

The page says that when widow **Susanna**, Mary Busey's mother, was granted letters of administration for the estate of **Paul Busey**, **William Selby** and **Richard Bowen** acted as sureties for her bond.

Interpretation:
- If accurate, this places William Selby inside the Busey estate network before his own death.
- The compiler interprets this as suggesting William Selby was Susanna Busey's son-in-law through marriage to Mary Busey.
- This is plausible but not proof of Mary Busey's parentage without the original bond and associated Busey probate records.

### 2. William Selby administration bond, 1699

The supplied extract says:

- **Mary Selby**, as executrix of her husband **William Selby**'s estate, with **Paul Busey** and **Joshua Cecell/Cecil**, was bound in the sum of 300 pounds sterling.
- Mary was to present a complete inventory by 25 May.
- Witnesses: **David Small**, **Benjamin Stephen**, and **Charles Busey**.
- Signed: **Mary Selby**, **Paul Busey**, and **Josh Cecell**.
- Cited as Prince George's County Administration Bonds, 1698-1757, p. 16, per Carol Collins.

Interpretation:
- Paul Busey serving as surety and Charles Busey witnessing are strong network evidence for a Busey connection to widow Mary Selby.
- The note's parenthetical says Charles and Paul Busey could be Mary's brothers, while Joshua Cecil was her future husband. This is a hypothesis, not yet a proved kinship statement.

### 3. Inventory, 23 May 1699

The supplied page says the inventory of **William Selby of Prince George's County** was appraised on 23 May 1699 by **R. Bradly** and **Samuell Magruder** at **£437.13.11**.

Cited as Prerogative Court 19 1/2 A.92 abstract.

Interpretation:
- This aligns with the *Selby Families of Colonial America* extract and confirms the estate's substantial value.
- It does not by itself identify Mary's maiden name.

### 4. Estate account, 30 July 1701

The supplied page says that in the 30 July 1701 accounting of William Selby's estate:

- payments were made to **Charles Beale**, **Richard Groome**, **Richard Jones**, **Joshua Cecil**, and others;
- the executrix was **Mary Cecill**, wife of Joshua Cecil;
- cited as Prerogative Court 20.237 abstract;
- compiler note: this proves the marriage of widow Mary Selby to Joshua Cecil.

Interpretation:
- This is strong evidence that William Selby's widow Mary remarried Joshua Cecil by 30 July 1701.
- It does not prove Mary was born a Busey, but it ties together the Selby estate and Cecil remarriage.

### 5. The Farme and Ann Selby

The supplied pages say:

- Mary was pregnant with **Ann Selby** when William Selby died.
- Mary owned or held 100 acres called **The Farme**, inherited from her late husband, with reversion to her son **William Selby** when she died.
- A 1722 deed reportedly confirms her son William Selby owned a portion of **The Farme** at that time, Land Records Liber I, folio 309.
- Joshua Cecil's 1717 will reportedly made provision for his stepdaughter **Ann Selby** and gave her part of **The Farme**.

Interpretation:
- These are important land-chain targets for proving Mary Selby/Cecil's household and the posthumous daughter Ann.
- The 1722 deed at Liber I, folio 309 is a new high-value target because it may confirm William Selby's continued ownership of part of The Farme.

## Mary Stewart / Mary Steward competing claim

The supplied note says many researchers claim William Selby's wife was **Mary Stewart**, but there is no documentation; however, a **Mary Steward** was transported at the same time as William Selby by Thomas Truesman.

Interpretation:
- The Mary Steward transport record is a name-proximity clue only.
- Same transport list plus compatible age is not sufficient to prove she married William Selby.
- The Busey estate-bond evidence is currently stronger than the Stewart/Steward claim, but the Busey identity still requires original probate correlation.

## Working evidence assessment

Current best status:

- **Mary [unknown]**, wife of William Selby, is proved by William's will and estate records.
- **Mary Selby later Mary Cecill/Cecil**, wife of Joshua Cecil, is strongly supported by the 1701 estate account.
- **Mary Busey as William Selby's wife** is a strong hypothesis supported by Busey sureties/witnesses and Busey-administration context, but it still needs original-record proof or a kinship statement.
- **Mary Stewart/Steward** remains weaker unless a direct marriage, deed, probate, or kinship record is found.

## Relationship to Somerset/Worcester Selby project

This evidence concerns the separate **Prince George's/Calvert/Anne Arundel western-shore Selby cluster**. It does not connect William Selby of Prince George's County to the confirmed Thomas Selby → John Selby → William Atkinson Selby line in Somerset/Worcester.

The evidence actually strengthens the distinction between the two clusters by placing William Selby's wife and estate network in the Busey/Cecil/Magruder/Beall/Calvert-Prince George's orbit.
