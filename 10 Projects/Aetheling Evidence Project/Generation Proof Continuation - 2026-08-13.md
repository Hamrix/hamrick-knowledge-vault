# Generation Proof Continuation - 2026-08-13

## Result

This continuation followed the older Worcester generation downward. After authenticated FamilySearch access became available, it retrieved and visually reviewed the 1761 Charles Parker administration bond and inventory and a separate 1788 Charles Parker will, administration bond, and inventory. The 1787 will directly proves that the later Charles was father of Selby Parker, identifies wife Ann and a larger child group, and explains the 1788 probate roles. It does not connect this Parker family to Margaret Selby's Kentucky brother Charles or resolve any Major Selby identity.

## Upstream Thomas Selby hypothesis

Bunce's 1948 compiled genealogy lists Major as one of six children of Thomas Selby and Elizabeth, tentatively called Houston. The page attributes the family list to a statement of Capt. John Selby, transmitted through Rev. Reuben Frame to the compiler. The compiler acknowledges that destroyed records made the information difficult to substantiate.

This is the first located source that expressly proposes Thomas and Elizabeth as parents of a Major Selby in the relevant Worcester network. It is derivative family testimony, not independent proof. No link has been created between that reported child and any of the adult-1783, minor-1785, Kentucky, or Indiana Major identity clusters.

The same volume cites Worcester deeds showing Thomas and Elizabeth associated with *Choice* and *Dull Folly* from 1749 through 1767. That tract context makes the 1783 assessment of an adult Major on 140 acres of *Choice* more important, but not self-proving. The compiled chain appears to place a roughly 136-acre residue with Thomas Jr., so the title must be reconstructed from originals before using acreage or tract names as kinship evidence.

## Charles Parker probate cluster

The official estate index and original records show **two distinct Charles Parker estates**, not one estate with two administration phases:

- The original bond dated 11 February 1761 names Collins Parker, a living Charles Parker, and John Parker as bound parties for the estate of deceased Charles Parker. Letters of administration were granted to Collins on 12 February 1761. The 12 March 1761 inventory names Samuel Parker and Phillip P. Parker under "Kin," and Collins swore to it on 13 June 1761.
- A separate Charles Parker made his will on 7 June 1787. He appointed his son John and wife Ann as executors; named sons John, Charles, Selby, and Thomas; named daughters Winnefred, Lize, Peggy, Sophia, Sarah, and Ann; and referred to the heirs of daughter Polly. The will directly calls Selby his son.
- The later Charles's will was proved on 16 February 1788. Selby Parker's bond on that date names Selby, Schoolfield Parker, and John Selby (of Ezekiel) as bound parties and grants Selby administration with the will annexed.
- The 29 April 1788 inventory names John Parker and Ann Parker under "Nearest of Kin," Schoolfield Parker and William Parker as appraisers, and Selby Parker as administrator. The will resolves John as a son and Ann as the testator's wife.
- The separate 1788 index row points to a 28 May 1790 account and a second inventory filed 12 March 1793.

The earlier index reading of 16 February 1786 was incorrect; column alignment and the original 1761 records establish an 11 February bond and 12 February grant of letters in 1761. A second correction separates the 1761 row on index image 55 from the 1788 row on image 58. The 1787 will makes the same-person interpretation chronologically impossible. Both corrections are now preserved in the source notes.

The will directly establishes Charles-to-Selby as father and son, Charles-to-John as father and son, and Charles-to-Ann as husband and wife. It does not establish that John Selby was Ezekiel's son, define Schoolfield Parker's relationship, connect Selby Parker to any Major Selby, or identify this Maryland Charles as the Kentucky Charles whom Margaret Selby called her brother. The third signature on the 1788 bond is also difficult or overwritten, although its opening text clearly names John Selby (of Ezekiel).

## 1761-1762 Testamentary Proceedings follow-up

The Maryland colonial probate index assigns Testamentary Proceedings Liber 38 folios 93, 133, 163, and 328 to both Charles Parker and Collins Parker. Authenticated review of all four originals establishes a narrower result:

- folio 93 independently abstracts Collins Parker's administration of deceased Charles Parker's estate, with living Charles Parker and John Parker as sureties;
- folio 163 records that Charles Parker's inventory was exhibited from Worcester County and ordered recorded;
- folio 133 names Charles Parker and Samuel Dreaden as sureties for Mary Marsden in the Angus Marsden estate; and
- folio 328 names Charles Parker and Adam Stevenson as sureties for Daniel Burgess in the Micajah Burgess estate.

The shared index string is not four kinship-bearing references for both Charles and Collins. Only folio 93 names Collins, and none of the four states his relationship to the decedent. Folios 133 and 328 are collateral appearances by a Charles Parker, not continuations of Charles Parker's estate.

## Original 1762 account follow-up

The Maryland Prerogative Court accounts index locates Charles Parker's entry at **Liber 48, folio 128, Worcester County**. Direct visual review of [[Maryland State Archives - Charles Parker 1762 Worcester administration account]] establishes that Collins Parker filed the account as administrator and that it was sworn and passed on 21 May 1762. Folios 128-129 record the inventory charge, debts and administrative payments, and a small balance in the administrator's favor.

The account names no heirs, makes no distribution to a family class, and does not state Collins's relationship to Charles. It strengthens the bounded estate timeline but does not supply the missing generational link. The separate 28 May 1790 account or distribution remains targeted to Worcester distribution book JW 21, and the 12 March 1793 second inventory to inventory book LH 2; the Maryland State Archives guide currently marks the relevant images as searchroom-only.

## Reviewed collateral hit

[[FamilySearch - James Campbell 1766 Worcester will]] names Charles Parker in a clause concerning plantation-sale proceeds, a debt due to William Truitt, and costs of suit. The will separately names Campbell's children and executors without identifying Charles Parker as either. It is retained as a reviewed financial or legal association, not kinship evidence and not an identity bridge among the different Charles Parker records.

## Account and will-copy search

[[FamilySearch - Charles Parker account search negative result 2026-08-13]] records the authenticated full-text searches for the indexed 1762 and 1790 accounts, 1793 second inventory, and will copy. The will was subsequently found by manually browsing the exact 1783-1790 will volume, proving the search result was a false negative. The 1762 account was then recovered through the Maryland State Archives accounts index and original-volume route. A further manual review of the Hudson Worcester balance-book compilation found Samuel and George Parker entries but no Charles Parker entry at the alphabetical surname transition. The remaining 1790 and 1793 targets require the original Worcester distribution and inventory volumes or an archive request.

## JW 21 Orphans Court follow-up

The Worcester County Library Digital Archive exposes the complete JW 21 Orphans Court Proceedings volume despite the Maryland State Archives guide's searchroom limitation on its own image interface. The archive's film header identifies JW 21 as covering 1789-1791 and including guardian bonds, annual valuations, and distributions.

Visual review of the June Court 1790 index pages and manuscript pages 44-48 found no Charles Parker entry at the indexed 28 May 1790 target. That absence is not treated as proof that the account is missing: the estate index may point to the separate distribution series contained elsewhere on the film, and the June docket includes continuations from prior court terms.

The same review recovered [[Worcester Digital Archive - Parker Selby children 1790 guardian bond]]. On 11 June 1790, George Selby and Elizabeth Selby were each expressly described as orphans of Parker Selby, deceased, and placed under Nancy Gunby's guardianship. This directly proves [[Parker Selby was father of George and Elizabeth Selby|Parker-to-George and Parker-to-Elizabeth]]. It does not yet connect those children to the Parker identified in 1791 as a son of Captain John Selby.

## Parker estate differentiation and downward proof

Direct review of [[FamilySearch - Parker Selby 1790 Worcester executor bond]] establishes that James Selby qualified as executor under one deceased Parker Selby's will on 29 January 1790, with William Selby and Dennis Hudson bound with him. [[Maryland State Archives - Parker Selby Worcester estate index]] places that bond, a 24 June 1790 inventory, and a 15 May 1792 account in a row labeled Parker "of Wm."

The separate [[FamilySearch - Parker Selby 1791 Worcester de bonis non bond]] appoints James Selby and John Gunby administrators de bonis non and says John Selby, Esquire, had been the former administrator. The index places it in a separate Parker "of John" row. [[The 1790 and 1791 Parker Selby records describe different estate histories]].

The third track is now supported by its underlying records. [[FamilySearch - Nancy Selby 1789 Parker Selby executor bond]] names Nancy Selby as executrix of Parker's will on 8 April 1789. [[FamilySearch - John and Nancy Gunby 1792-1795 Parker Selby estate accounts]] identifies John Gunby and Nancy his wife as accountants and repeats Nancy's executrix role on 31 December 1792 and 28 April 1795. The final account refers back to a third account exhibited 30 November 1793. These originals prove [[Nancy wife of John Gunby was executrix of Parker Selbys will in 1792|Nancy's marriage to John Gunby and administration of Parker's estate]], but they name no heirs and do not call Nancy Parker's widow. The guardian bond still does not state its docket, so the children's assignment to this exact estate remains unproved.

John Selby's 1790 will supplies a maternal cluster. It calls Nancy Gunby his daughter, George her son, Betsey Selby her daughter, and John Selby her son. Read with the June guardian bond and Nancy's now-documented administration of Parker's estate, this [[Nancy Gunby is strongly identified as mother of George and Elizabeth Selby|strongly identifies Nancy as mother of George and Elizabeth or Betsey]], but no single reviewed record yet calls Nancy Parker's widow or expressly equates Elizabeth with Betsey.

[[Scarborough 1951 - George Selby 1802 Pleasant Grove deed transcription]] reports an enrolled Worcester deed calling George the eldest son and heir at law of deceased Parker Selby. It independently corroborates Parker-to-George and identifies Liber V, folios 236-238 as the original target. Because only the printed transcription has been reviewed, the deed is not yet used to extend Parker's paternal line.

## Exact deed-film and estate-docket retrieval targets

[[FamilySearch Catalog - Worcester Land Records Liber V-W]] now identifies the original 1802 deed volume as FamilySearch film 14543 / DGS 8141714. The Maryland State Archives independently describes volume V as CE30-21, covering 1801-1803. The signed-in FamilySearch catalog marks the film and its full-text search as available only at a FamilySearch Center or affiliate library. The original folios were not reviewed, so the reported eldest-son and heir-at-law language remains derivative corroboration.

[[FamilySearch - Worcester estate docket Parker Selby index page]] adds a stable image for Estate Docket T4215/C1993-1, DGS 105017200, image 69. Its opening and account entries are now confirmed by the original 1789 bond and 1792-1795 accounts. The remaining underlying targets are the 25 August 1791 inventory, 30 November 1793 third account, and 1 November 1793 distribution. None of the reviewed records calls Nancy Parker's widow, names George or Elizabeth as heirs, or assigns their guardian bond to this estate.
