# Generation Proof Continuation - 2026-08-13

## Result

This continuation followed the older Worcester generation downward. After authenticated FamilySearch access became available, it retrieved and visually reviewed the 1761 Charles Parker administration bond and inventory and a separate 1788 Charles Parker will, administration bond, and inventory. The 1787 will directly proves that the later Charles was father of Selby Parker, identifies wife Ann and a larger child group, and explains the 1788 probate roles. It does not connect this Parker family to Margaret Selby's Kentucky brother Charles or resolve any Major Selby identity.

## Upstream Thomas Selby hypothesis

Bunce's 1948 compiled genealogy lists Major as one of six children of Thomas Selby and Elizabeth, tentatively called Houston. The page attributes the family list to a statement of Capt. John Selby, transmitted through Rev. Reuben Frame to the compiler. The compiler acknowledges that destroyed records made the information difficult to substantiate.

This is the first located source that expressly proposes Thomas and Elizabeth as parents of a Major Selby in the relevant Worcester network. It is derivative family testimony, not independent proof. No link has been created between that reported child and any of the adult-1783, minor-1785, Kentucky, or Indiana Major identity clusters.

The same volume cites Worcester deeds showing Thomas and Elizabeth associated with *Choice* and *Dull Folly* from 1749 through 1767. That tract context makes the 1783 assessment of an adult Major on 140 acres of *Choice* more important, but not self-proving. The compiled chain appears to place a roughly 136-acre residue with Thomas Jr., so the title must be reconstructed from originals before using acreage or tract names as kinship evidence.

## Charles Parker probate cluster

The official estate index and original records show **two distinct Charles Parker estates**, not one estate with two administration phases:

- The original bond dated 11 February 1761 names Collins Parker, a living Charles Parker, and John Parker as bound parties for the estate of deceased Charles Parker. Letters of administration were granted to Collins on 12 February 1761. The 12 March 1761 inventory names Samuel Parker and Phillip P. Parker under "Kin," and Collins swore to it on 13 June 1761.
- A separate Charles Parker made his will on 7 June 1787. He appointed his son John and wife Ann as executors; named sons John, Charles, Selby, and Thomas; named daughters Winnefred, Lize, Peggy, Sophia, Sarah, and Ann; and referred to the heirs of daughter Polly. The will directly calls Selby his son.
- The later Charles's will was proved on 16 February 1788. Selby Parker's bond on that date names Selby, Schoolfield Parker, and John Selby (of Ezekiel) as bound parties and grants Selby administration with the will annexed.
- The 29 April 1788 inventory names John Parker and Ann Parker under "Nearest of Kin," Schoolfield Parker and William Parker as appraisers, and Selby Parker as administrator. The will resolves John as a son and Ann as the testator's wife.
- The separate 1788 index row points to a 28 May 1790 account and a second inventory filed 12 March 1793.

The earlier index reading of 16 February 1786 was incorrect; column alignment and the original 1761 records establish an 11 February bond and 12 February grant of letters in 1761. A second correction separates the 1761 row on index image 55 from the 1788 row on image 58. The 1787 will makes the same-person interpretation chronologically impossible. Both corrections are now preserved in the source notes.

The will directly establishes Charles-to-Selby as father and son, Charles-to-John as father and son, and Charles-to-Ann as husband and wife. It does not establish that John Selby was Ezekiel's son, define Schoolfield Parker's relationship, connect Selby Parker to any Major Selby, or identify this Maryland Charles as the Kentucky Charles whom Margaret Selby called her brother. The third signature on the 1788 bond is also difficult or overwritten, although its opening text clearly names John Selby (of Ezekiel).

## 1761-1762 Testamentary Proceedings follow-up

The Maryland colonial probate index assigns Testamentary Proceedings Liber 38 folios 93, 133, 163, and 328 to both Charles Parker and Collins Parker. Authenticated review of all four originals establishes a narrower result:

- folio 93 independently abstracts Collins Parker's administration of deceased Charles Parker's estate, with living Charles Parker and John Parker as sureties;
- folio 163 records that Charles Parker's inventory was exhibited from Worcester County and ordered recorded;
- folio 133 names Charles Parker and Samuel Dreaden as sureties for Mary Marsden in the Angus Marsden estate; and
- folio 328 names Charles Parker and Adam Stevenson as sureties for Daniel Burgess in the Micajah Burgess estate.

The shared index string is not four kinship-bearing references for both Charles and Collins. Only folio 93 names Collins, and none of the four states his relationship to the decedent. Folios 133 and 328 are collateral appearances by a Charles Parker, not continuations of Charles Parker's estate. The indexed May 1762 account remains unlocated.

## Reviewed collateral hit

[[FamilySearch - James Campbell 1766 Worcester will]] names Charles Parker in a clause concerning plantation-sale proceeds, a debt due to William Truitt, and costs of suit. The will separately names Campbell's children and executors without identifying Charles Parker as either. It is retained as a reviewed financial or legal association, not kinship evidence and not an identity bridge among the different Charles Parker records.

## Account and will-copy search

[[FamilySearch - Charles Parker account search negative result 2026-08-13]] records the authenticated full-text searches for the indexed 1762 and 1790 accounts, 1793 second inventory, and will copy. The will was subsequently found by manually browsing the exact 1783-1790 will volume, proving the search result was a false negative. A further manual review of the Hudson Worcester balance-book compilation found Samuel and George Parker entries but no Charles Parker entry at the alphabetical surname transition. Because that film is a later distribution abstract rather than the original account book, the account and second-inventory targets remain unresolved and require original-volume or archive-request work.
