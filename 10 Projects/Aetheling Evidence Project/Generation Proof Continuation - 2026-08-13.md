# Generation Proof Continuation - 2026-08-13

## Result

This continuation followed the older Worcester generation downward. After authenticated FamilySearch access became available, it retrieved and visually reviewed the original 1761 administration bond and letters, 1761 inventory, 1788 administration bond and letters, and 1788 inventory for one Charles Parker estate. The records prove several probate roles and kin designations, but they do not state the exact relationships needed to connect this estate to Margaret Selby's Kentucky brother Charles or to resolve Major Selby's ancestry.

## Upstream Thomas Selby hypothesis

Bunce's 1948 compiled genealogy lists Major as one of six children of Thomas Selby and Elizabeth, tentatively called Houston. The page attributes the family list to a statement of Capt. John Selby, transmitted through Rev. Reuben Frame to the compiler. The compiler acknowledges that destroyed records made the information difficult to substantiate.

This is the first located source that expressly proposes Thomas and Elizabeth as parents of a Major Selby in the relevant Worcester network. It is derivative family testimony, not independent proof. No link has been created between that reported child and any of the adult-1783, minor-1785, Kentucky, or Indiana Major identity clusters.

The same volume cites Worcester deeds showing Thomas and Elizabeth associated with *Choice* and *Dull Folly* from 1749 through 1767. That tract context makes the 1783 assessment of an adult Major on 140 acres of *Choice* more important, but not self-proving. The compiled chain appears to place a roughly 136-acre residue with Thomas Jr., so the title must be reconstructed from originals before using acreage or tract names as kinship evidence.

## Charles Parker probate cluster

The official estate index and original records show that one Charles Parker estate had two administration phases:

- The original bond dated 11 February 1761 names Collins Parker, a living Charles Parker, and John Parker as bound parties for the estate of deceased Charles Parker. Letters of administration were granted to Collins on 12 February 1761. The 12 March 1761 inventory names Samuel Parker and Phillip P. Parker under "Kin," and Collins swore to it on 13 June 1761.
- Selby Parker's bond was dated 16 February 1788. Its opening text names Selby Parker, Schoolfield Parker, and John Selby (of Ezekiel) as bound parties and grants Selby administration with a copy of Charles Parker's will annexed.
- The 29 April 1788 inventory names John Parker and Ann Parker under "Nearest of Kin," Schoolfield Parker and William Parker as appraisers, and Selby Parker as administrator.
- The index also points to a 28 May 1790 account and a second inventory filed 12 March 1793.

The earlier index reading of 16 February 1786 was incorrect; column alignment and the original 1761 records establish an 11 February bond and 12 February grant of letters in 1761. The correction and date refinement are now preserved in the source notes.

The exact relationships remain unstated. In particular, the records do not establish that Selby Parker was Charles's son or brother, that John Selby was Ezekiel's son, that Ann Parker was Charles's wife, or that this Maryland Charles was the Kentucky Charles whom Margaret Selby called her brother. The third signature on the 1788 bond is also difficult or overwritten, although its opening text clearly names John Selby (of Ezekiel).

## Reviewed collateral hit

[[FamilySearch - James Campbell 1766 Worcester will]] names Charles Parker in a clause concerning plantation-sale proceeds, a debt due to William Truitt, and costs of suit. The will separately names Campbell's children and executors without identifying Charles Parker as either. It is retained as a reviewed financial or legal association, not kinship evidence and not an identity bridge among the different Charles Parker records.

## Account and will-copy search

[[FamilySearch - Charles Parker account search negative result 2026-08-13]] records the authenticated full-text searches for the indexed 1762 and 1790 accounts, 1793 second inventory, and will copy. No identifiable image of those targets was located. Because the same search returned false negatives for already reviewed instruments, the result is a retrieval limitation rather than evidence that the papers do not survive.
