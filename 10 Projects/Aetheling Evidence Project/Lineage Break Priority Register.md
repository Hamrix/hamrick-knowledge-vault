# Lineage Break Priority Register

This register ranks unresolved parent-child or identity links by evidentiary importance and likelihood of resolution. A distant medieval connection is not actionable until every nearer link required to reach it is supported.

## Priority 1 - modern and nineteenth-century paternal Selby chain

### Frederic Ethelred Selby -> James Benjamin Sanford Selby

**Current status:** supported hypothesis; Sarah Eveline Stafford's maternity is better supported than James's paternity.

**Current evidence:** 1930 and 1940 derivative census transcriptions, GEDCOM family assignment, James-Sarah marriage chronology, direct family testimony.

**Decisive evidence:** Frederic's SS-5, birth or delayed-birth record, obituary, baptism, James's obituary or probate, original census images.

### James Benjamin Sanford Selby -> Frederick Payne Selby

**Current status:** moderate, based on a specific 1900 census household transcription.

**Decisive evidence:** original 1900 census image, James's birth record, marriage record, obituary, Frederick Payne's probate or obituary.

### Frederick Payne Selby -> Robert Benjamin Selby

**Current status:** moderate, based on 1870 co-residence and an 1880 relationship transcription identifying Frederick as Robert's son.

**Decisive evidence:** original 1880 image, Frederick's birth or baptism, Robert's obituary or succession, family Bible with provenance.

### Robert Benjamin Selby -> Benjamin Selby of Adair County

**Current status:** weak and unresolved. The uploaded GEDCOM supplies the relationship but no inspected parentage record.

**Decisive evidence:** Robert's 1902 obituary, Orleans Parish succession, death certificate, cemetery record, marriage record, earliest census, Henry County records, or a Benjamin estate/guardianship record naming Robert.

**Research rule:** this is the present paternal Selby stopping point. No earlier Selby branch may be appended until this link is established.

## Priority 2 - Stafford/Peters bridge into Louisiana

### Sarah Eveline Stafford -> Ethelred Hamrick Stafford

**Current status:** supported by GEDCOM citations to New Orleans birth, marriage and census collections, but original images are not yet inspected.

**Decisive evidence:** Sarah's birth certificate/index entry, marriage record, obituary or SS-5, and the relevant census images.

### Ethelred Hamrick Stafford -> John Evans Stafford

**Current status:** derivative household hypothesis. Multiple census, marriage, death and land collections are attached, but the relationship has not been audited from original images.

**Decisive evidence:** Ethelred Hamrick's birth record, 1880 household image, obituary, marriage record, or John Evans Stafford's probate.

### John Evans Stafford -> Ethelred R. Stafford

**Current status:** derivative family assignment; not yet independently proved.

**Decisive evidence:** 1850-1880 census sequence, John Evans's marriage/death records, land and probate files, Ethelred R.'s estate or obituary.

### Ethelred R. Stafford -> Stephen Stafford and Elizabeth/Betsey Peters

**Current status:** compiled-tree claim with a specific reported marriage date for Stephen Stafford and Elizabeth Peters, 19 September 1794, Craven County. The relationship to Ethelred R. remains unverified.

**Current leads:** Stafford Society compiled profile cites `North Carolina Marriages to 1825`; GEDCOM family F317 assigns Ethelred R. as their child.

**Decisive evidence:** original marriage bond/return, Ethelred R.'s death or obituary, Stephen's probate, migration records, church register, family Bible with provenance, or court/guardianship papers.

### Elizabeth/Betsey Peters -> Etheldred Peters

**Current status:** unproven. The GEDCOM assigns Betsey as a daughter of Etheldred Peters, but no direct source has been inspected.

**Important correction:** the original 1786 Swift Creek tax-list image identifies a **son John Peters** in Etheldred's household. It does not identify Etheldred as a son of John Peters and does not name Betsey.

**Decisive evidence:** Etheldred's 1788 Craven estate file, administrator's bond, inventory, sale, guardianship, distribution, land division, or a marriage bond naming Betsey's parent or bondsman.

### Etheldred Peters -> William Peters and Elizabeth Bayley

**Current status:** unsupported compiled-tree assignment.

**Problem:** the GEDCOM asserts an English marriage at Barthomley, Cheshire, in 1726 and then places Etheldred in colonial North Carolina, but no inspected migration, probate, baptism or relationship record connects those English people to the Craven County man.

**Research rule:** quarantine this English parentage unless direct transatlantic evidence is found.

## Priority 3 - Major Selby and Worcester County network

### Major Selby Sr. parentage

**Current status:** unresolved.

**Strongest record cluster:** Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged appear in the 1783 John Selby assessment, the 1797 Major Selby chancery case, and the 1810 Zadock Selby estate litigation.

**Decisive evidence:** full 1797 Major Selby packet, 1810 Zadock estate packet and Chancery Record 91 p. 233, John Selby's 1790 will/trust, Parker Selby estate files, and chain-of-title deeds.

**Identity warning:** distinguish the adult Maryland Major Selby from later compiled-tree men allegedly born around 1781-1783.

## Priority 4 - medieval Selby/Biddlestone succession

The surviving medieval research has established discrete Selby people, deeds and property associations, but it has not connected them to the modern American paternal line.

### Key unresolved medieval links

- Walter of Broxa -> later Biddlestone Selbys: plausible network association, not descent.
- John the elder -> John the younger in the 1467 material: distinct men are documented; fatherhood is not.
- later John -> Percival Selby: estate succession is strong, biological fatherhood remains unproved.
- Alexander Selby in Burke-derived pedigrees: no located medieval record.
- Walter III -> James -> Alexander -> 1432 John: unresolved and constrained by the unsupported Alexander link.

### Highest-yield records

- Stanford M2652 original deeds, especially 1432, 1495 and 1500 items;
- TNA E 321/30/86, Lyle v Selby;
- Percival Selby's IPM and related estate papers;
- complete Biddlestone heraldic genealogy provenance and original text.

### Governing limit

Even a fully reconstructed Biddlestone pedigree would not connect to the user unless the colonial and modern Selby chain reaches that family through separately documented parent-child links.

## Priority 5 - royal, de Lacy, Stafford and Wessex claims

**Current status:** no documented bridge from the user's proven or provisional modern ancestry to a medieval noble or royal line.

Reliable medieval pedigrees for de Lacy, Stafford, Neville, Percy, Plantagenet or Wessex figures do not prove descent unless the entire intervening chain is demonstrated.

No surname match, coat of arms, recurring forename, tract association, DNA ethnicity estimate, compiled tree or modern ceremonial identity can substitute for the missing links.

## Public-use rule

A lineage chart must end at the last documented or explicitly provisional person. Dotted or hypothesis lines must be labelled. No medieval, noble, royal or property claim may be presented as a continuation of the modern line while Priority 1 and Priority 2 links remain unresolved.
