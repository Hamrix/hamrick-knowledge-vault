# Walter de Ver and Sibilla Source Audit - 2026-08-21

## Research question

What does the record trail actually prove about Walter de Ver, Sibilla, their family, and the reported `de Kyme` identification?

## Findings

1. **Strong direct core:** [[Lincolnshire final concord CP 25-1-128-15 no 8 - 1220]] explicitly identifies Sibilla, then wife of Nicholas de Schauencurt, as formerly wife of Walter de Ver. The original image and the 1896 abstract were visually reviewed.
2. **Citation corrected:** the concord is on printed page 156, no. 8a, not page 150 as cited in [[The Genealogist Volume 20 - 1904]].
3. **`de Kyme` unresolved:** the original concord calls her Sibilla without that surname. Massingberd's `Sibilla de Kyme` statement depends on Harley MS 301, folio 171b, which was not located for image review.
4. **Harley source classified:** the 1808 [[Catalogue of the Harleian Manuscripts Volume 1 - Harley MS 301]] describes MS 301 as an early modern collection of Exchequer-record transcripts, not a contemporary medieval pedigree.
5. **Simon lead:** [[Wilkinson 1999 - Thirteenth-Century Women in Lincolnshire]] reports Simon as Walter's son and heir from *Curia Regis Rolls* VII, pages 18-19, with related litigation at page 160. The volume is restricted in the reviewed FamilySearch catalogue, so the claim remains moderate.
6. **Paternal link proved strongly:** [[Loyd and Stenton 1950 - Sir Christopher Hattons Book of Seals entry 73]] transcribes Walter's charter calling him son of Adam of Goxhill, Robert son of Erneis his grandfather, and Adam his father. This proves the immediate paternal relationships at strong confidence.
7. **Adam was Guy's son-in-law:** [[Hunter 1844 - Pipe Roll 1 Richard I page 61]] twice describes Adam as Guy de Ver's son-in-law. The daughter through whom they were related is not named.
8. **Gundreda was Nicholas's wife:** [[Fowler 1893 - Coucher Book of Selby Volume II page 296]] transcribes Nicholas de Chavencurt explicitly calling Gundreda de Ver his wife. The charter does not mention Adam or Walter.
9. **Maternal lead remains tentative:** [[Farrer 1916 - Early Yorkshire Charters Volume III Goxhill pedigree]], printed page 61, reconstructs Gundreda as Adam's wife and Walter's mother. Direct review of its cited Pipe Roll and Selby charter proves the two adjacent relationships above but not that the unnamed daughter of Guy was Gundreda. *Registrum Antiquissimum* II, pages 211-212, remains unreviewed.
10. **Simon remains moderate:** Farrer also reports that Walter left by Sibyl a son Simon, then under age, citing Curia Regis Roll 58, membrane 11. The plea roll remains unreviewed.
11. **Repeated litigation:** [[Whiting 2021 - Lincolnshire eyre dower litigation]] identifies nine 1218-1219 dower entries for Nicholas and Sybil. These are useful corroborative targets but do not name Walter in the reviewed passage.

## Unreviewed conflict lead

A search-result extract for Emilia Jamroziak's 2001 Rievaulx thesis calls Sibilla de Kyme the wife of Simon de Vere while also describing Simon as her son. The thesis PDF and its cited *Curia Regis Rolls* pages were not reviewed in this audit. The wording is internally inconsistent with the direct concord and is recorded only as a lead requiring inspection, not evidence.

## Access limits

- FamilySearch catalog 284589 identifies *Curia Regis Rolls* volume VII as covering 1213-1215, but its film row reports current digital-access restrictions.
- No usable image of Harley MS 301, folio 171b, was located in this search.
- The Lincoln Record Society describes *Registrum Antiquissimum* volume II, but the required page was not available through the reviewed public access routes.
- These access results do not prove the records are unavailable elsewhere.

## Conclusions

The evidence must remain split into atomic claims. The Walter-Sibilla-Nicholas marital sequence, Walter's father Adam and grandfather Robert, Adam's status as Guy de Ver's son-in-law, and Gundreda's marriage to Nicholas are strong. Sibilla's `de Kyme` identification and Simon's son-and-heir claim are moderate. Gundreda's maternity remains tentative because no reviewed record identifies Guy's unnamed daughter as Gundreda or calls Gundreda Adam's wife or Walter's mother. None of these medieval findings establishes a Selby-surname bridge or a connection to the user's proven ancestry.
