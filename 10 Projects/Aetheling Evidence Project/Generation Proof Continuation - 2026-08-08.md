## Added proof value

### Older Atkinson and Selby generations

John Atkinson's recorded Worcester County will, dated 21 March 1779 and proved 12 May 1779, supplies reciprocal and corroborating relationship evidence above the unresolved Major Selby generation:

- John calls Angelo Atkinson his father and Joshua Atkinson his brother. Angelo's 1766 will independently names John and Joshua as sons.
- John names Joshua's sons Angelo and John and daughter Rachel. Joshua's 1773 will independently names those children.
- John expressly identifies John Selby's two sons as John Selby and William Atkinson Selby. Angelo's 1766 will independently identifies William Atkinson Selby as John Selby's son and Angelo's grandson.

These are verified older-generation links. The 1779 will does not identify John Selby's wife, name Major Selby, or identify John Selby's son John with the John Selby Jr. estate opened in 1782.

Parker Selby's 1773 will was also reviewed. It names wife Mary; children Samuel, David, John, Sarah Atkinson, Mary, Leah, Ann, and Parker; and brothers James and John. This is a useful collateral Selby-Atkinson household, but no reviewed record connects Parker's son John to the target John or Major.

### Parker Selby to eldest son John, 1746-1747

Parker Selby's original recorded York County will, made 8 November 1746, describes him as living in Worcester County near Snow Hill and directly calls John Selby his eldest son. Parker devised the 750-acre home plantation to John, named sons William, James, Micajah, Parker, and Zadock and daughter Tabitha Baily Selby, and appointed wife Mary and sons John and Parker as executors. John presented the will when it was proved on 16 March 1746/47.

A Worcester County probate record from 1747 independently shows Mary relinquishing administration of deceased husband Parker's estate to the other executors named in his will. The following bond names John and Parker Selby as executors. Together, these records confirm the bounded generation **Parker Selby -> eldest son John Selby**.

The further identification of this eldest son as the Worcester legislator and 1790 testator John Selby Esq. is strong but not yet direct. It rests on place and chronology, the repeated John-Parker-James sibling group in Parker's 1773 will, executor continuity, the 750-acre inheritance, and the Maryland State Archives biography. No reviewed later primary record yet calls the 1790 John "son of Parker."

### John Selby to a minor Major Selby

The reviewed 1785 Worcester guardian bond and estate-view certificate directly identify a minor Major Selby, about seven, as the orphan of a deceased John Selby. This is a verified father-child statement for the people in those records.

The follow-up from the generation above now supplies the missing grandfather link:

- The original John Selby Jr. estate docket has now been manually reviewed. It records administrator Leah Selby, sureties Jacob Selby and Nehemiah Dorman, a bond filed 20 October 1782, an inventory filed 18 October 1783, and accounts dated 31 October 1783 and 13 March 1793. It does not list a sale or distribution date.
- The original inventory identifies the decedent as "John Selby (of John, Capt.)," names James and Parker Selby as nearest kin, and records Kendal Smock and Leah his wife in the 18 October 1783 return, with Leah as administratrix.
- The recorded 13 March 1793 instrument is the estate's third account, not a distribution to heirs. It identifies the accountants as Kendal Smock and Leah his wife, carries forward a second account exhibited 23 March 1784, and names no child, heir, guardian, distributee, or Major Selby.
- John Selby Esq.'s will, dated 13 November 1790 and proved 10 December 1790, expressly calls Major a grandson and names him as "Major Selby son of John."
- The final account of John Selby Esq.'s estate, passed 10 March 1795, paid Major's legacy to "John Selby son of Parker guardian to Major Selby Orphan of John Selby" and says the legacy was left by Major's grandfather's will.
- Maryland's official sheriff list places a John Selby Jr. in office in Worcester County from 1779 through 1782. This remains compatible identity context rather than a relationship statement.

The will and final account directly prove **John Selby Esq. -> son John -> minor Major Selby**. Correlation with the 1782 estate is strong: the decedent's date, junior designation, and "of John, Capt." identifier fit the proved father and grandfather chain. Because no single record cross-references Major to that estate file, the estate-file identity is rated strong rather than confirmed. Leah's status as widow or mother remains unproved.

### One 1820 Nicholas County Major household

Full review of both preserved census images corrected the earlier two-entry treatment. Page 111 contains one Major Selby household: one male age 26-44, one female age 10-15, and one female age 26-44. The preserved page 129 image contains no Major Selby entry.

The page 111 male's age bracket is compatible with the 1785 minor, who would have been about 41 or 42 in 1820. Later evidence for Henry Selby's estimated 1784 birth, however, means this household cannot automatically be assigned to the 1833 testator who called Henry his son. A deed, tax sequence, migration record, or other person-specific bridge is still required.

### Major Selby guardian-account continuity, 1797-1800

Four consecutive Worcester County guardian accounts have now been reviewed. John Selby's fourth, fifth, sixth, and seventh accounts, passed at the February terms of 1797, 1798, 1799, and 1800, each identify him as guardian to **Major Selby, orphan of John Selby**. The fourth account carries forward an April 1796 balance, and each later account carries forward its immediately preceding annual balance.

This creates direct court-record continuity for the 1785 child through February 1800. The seventh account is not labeled final and contains no ward's receipt or release. It therefore narrows the later-identity problem without closing it.

### Major Selby received Ann Selby's estate share, 1803

Polly Selby's account as administratrix of James Selby of John, sworn 30 September 1803, records distributions from Ann Selby's estate, which James had administered. It credits John Selby Jr. with his share from his grandmother Ann, identifies John Gunby's children John and Nancy as Ann's grandchildren, and expressly pays **Major Selby, grandson of Ann**, his part of her estate under a receipt and order.

John Selby Esq.'s 1790 will already names wife Ann, son James, and grandson "Major Selby son of John." The 1803 account therefore supplies reciprocal evidence from Ann's estate and carries the same proved family group into Major's adult receipt. It does not state Major's later residence, but it moves the last Worcester family anchor from February 1800 to September 1803.

### Adult Major Selby in Bourbon County by 1807

An original Bourbon Circuit Court packet identifies Major Selby as plaintiff against William Gray on a five-pound debt. A summons issued at Paris on 5 August 1807 directed the Bourbon County sheriff to bring Gray before the court. This moves the earliest reviewed Kentucky anchor from the 1811 Bourbon deed back to 1807.

The rare full name, adult legal capacity, same county, and four-year interval strongly correlate the 1807 plaintiff with the 1811 purchaser. The case does not state Major's residence, birthplace, parents, or prior location. The remaining Worcester-to-Kentucky gap is now less than four years, from the 30 September 1803 estate payment to the 5 August 1807 action.

### Worcester marriage-index lead, 1808

A visually reviewed DAR-compiled Worcester marriage index reports **Hester (Hetty) Cottingham and Major Selby, 8 December 1808** in reciprocal COTTINGHAM and SELBY entries. These are two cross-index entries in one derivative compilation, not independent sources and not the original license, bond, or return. Because the reported Worcester event follows the Bourbon lawsuit, it complicates the simple migration model: the entry may represent return travel, another adult of the same name, or a compilation error. No identity assignment is made until the original is found.

The original-record target is now exact: **Worcester County Court (Marriage Licenses), 1795-1851, MSA C2027**, a paper series available through the Maryland State Archives circulation desk. A request should specify the reported 8 December 1808 date and both parties.

### Bourbon tax corroboration, 1811

The original 1811 Bourbon County tax list contains a handwritten Major Selby entry. Its volume year was verified from the original Bourbon County 1811 cover. This independently corroborates the same-year deed describing Major as of Bourbon County, although neither record names kin or a former residence.

### Bourbon tax, land-possession, and house continuity, 1811-1815

The 1811 purchase is now reinforced by a related Dudley-heirs deed proved 15 July 1811 on which Major Selby served as a marked witness alongside Armiger Lilly. Original Bourbon tax lists then record Major in three consecutive reviewed years, 1811, 1812, and 1813.

An ejectment record shows Major among the Bourbon land occupants served on 3 November 1812. The same dispute's survey and plat, dated 1 August 1815, labels **Major Selby's house & Improvement** and refers to a Major Selby deed supplied by Thomas Rule. This extends the reviewed Bourbon identity cluster from 1807 through 1815 and reduces the unexplained interval before the 1820 Nicholas household from nine years after the 1811 purchase to five years after the survey.

The land records do not state that Major moved to Nicholas County. Henry Selby's nearby appearance is not treated as a father-son statement and is not merged with the Henry named in Major's 1833 will.

### Thomas Rule associate bridge into Nicholas County, 1817

Nicholas County Deed Book E now supplies a specific land-network bridge two years after the 1815 survey. On 25 August 1817, Thomas Rule of Bourbon County acquired 92 acres described as surplus in Haw's survey, on the north side of Hinkston in Nicholas County, "formerly Bourbon," with Brushy Fork and the Shipp-Haws division in the bounds. On 14 October 1817, Laban Shipp and wife Elizabeth conveyed Rule about 58 acres in Samuel Haws's 5,000-acre survey on Brushy Fork. That tract was bounded in part by Valentine Cress.

This matters because Thomas Rule furnished the Major Selby deed used in the 1815 Bourbon survey, while Valentine Cress was served with Major in the 1812 ejectment action. The originals therefore carry two named members of Major's Bourbon land-dispute network into a precise Nicholas County Haws-survey context. They improve the geographic and associate correlation but do not name Major, prove a move, identify the 1820 household, or close the identity gap.

Annie Walker Burns's 1936 Nicholas deed abstract also pointed to Barkley Selby of Nicholas County conveying to Elijah C. Dazey on 26 June 1817, Deed Book E, page 103. The original and related deeds have now been reviewed:

- Thomas and Elizabeth Buckner conveyed Barkley 116 acres in Samuel Haws's survey on Brushy Fork of Hinkston Fork in April 1816.
- Barkley and wife Patsey conveyed 18.5 acres from that tract to Elijah C. Dazey on 26 June 1817.
- An October 1820 Marshall-Gunnell title-confirmation deed repeated the 116-acre boundary, excepted Barkley's prior 18.5-acre Dazey sale, and described the 97.5-acre residue as in Barkley's possession.

This places a collateral Selby landholder in the same Haws-survey system throughout the 1815-1820 transition and names Barkley's wife. It does not state any relationship between Barkley and Major, and Patsey is not conflated with Major's wife Peggy.

The Nicholas general deed index then supplied book-and-page targets that the full-text search had missed. The originals extend Barkley's title chronology:

- On 2 August 1824 Bartley Selby and **Milly his wife** conveyed William Dyer 53 acres on Brushy Fork. The deed expressly recites the Humphrey Marshall and October 1820 title sequence, joining this sale to the already reviewed Haws-survey residue.
- On 23 September 1830 William and Catharine Royse conveyed Barkley a small adjoining tract on Brushy Fork.
- The same day Bartley conveyed Royse about one acre on Bushy Fork of Hinkston and conveyed Daniel and Hugh J. McClintock 92 acres, 3 roods, and 27 poles. **Martha Selby** relinquished dower in the McClintock deed.

The 1817 **Patsey** and 1830 **Martha** appearances are strongly compatible because Patsey was commonly used for Martha, but no reviewed deed explicitly equates them. The 1824 **Milly** reading is a separate unresolved wife-name conflict. None of these deeds names Major or states Barkley's relationship to him.

A full-text pass over the same deed-film group also found William Selby and wife Priscilla, both of Nicholas County, conveying Armstrong McClintock a small tract on the waters of Brushy Fork on 5 May 1819. This proves a second collateral Selby couple in the land cluster but supplies no relationship to Major or Barkley. A separate OCR search for "Major" returned no result in this film group; that is an OCR-dependent negative search and not proof that the deed books contain no Major Selby reference.

### Nicholas County will and decisive identity separation, 1833

Major Selby's original recorded will, dated 4 January 1833 and proved at the Nicholas County Court's August 1833 term, names **wife Peggy Selby**, **son Henry Selby**, and **daughter Polly Anterbus, formerly Polly Selby**. It appoints Charles Parker executor. A February 1835 inventory again identifies Parker as executor of Major Selby deceased.

A later Nicholas deed adds a carefully bounded follow-down clue. Henry Selby and wife Eleanor conveyed Parker Selby Henry's Brush Fork home place of about 89 acres and an adjoining tract of about 45 acres. The instrument is dated 1 March 1838 and was acknowledged and recorded 1 March 1839. Its $1,200 and natural-affection consideration does not define Parker's relationship to Henry.

Three earlier deeds place a Henry Selby in this Nicholas land system before Major made his will. On 6 December 1828, a Henderson heir of Franklin County, Indiana, conveyed Henry one equal share, identified as lot 4, of Jesse Henderson's divided land. The handwritten given name appears to read Leven or Levin but remains uncertain. In July 1830 Joseph Williams and wife Susan, formerly Susan Henderson, conveyed Henry Susan's 6.25-acre lot 1; William G. Henderson separately conveyed Henry another 6.25-acre inherited share. All three deeds call Henry a resident of Nicholas County and place the property on Brushy Fork and Hinkston.

The automated transcript renders William's 1830 parcel number as lot 4, which conflicts with the 1828 lot-4 deed and the estate-division transcript. William's exact numeral is therefore not asserted until the manuscript can be read more clearly. The deeds still directly prove three acquisitions from Jesse Henderson heirs and Henry's pre-will Nicholas chronology, not Henry's Selby parentage.

The grantor's name, county, chronology, and Brush Fork setting make him a strong candidate for the son Henry named in Major's 1833 will. The deed does not mention Major, Peggy, Polly, Charles Parker as executor, or Major's estate. It therefore extends the Nicholas Selby cluster but does not directly prove that the 1838 grantor was Major's son or that Parker was kin.

The will independently confirms the 1825 Major-to-Polly relationship and establishes a bounded Nicholas County family group. It does not name Major's parents or Benjamin Selby. Most importantly, the Kentucky testator dead by August 1833 cannot be the Hamilton County, Indiana, Major enumerated alive in 1850 and 1860. The Kentucky and Indiana identity clusters are now conclusively separate. The two original 1820 schedules, one in Nicholas County and one in Somerset County, independently show that at least two adult Major Selbys were already living concurrently.

### Henry Selby marriage and household chronology, 1809-1850

The original Bourbon County marriage bond dated 5 June 1809 names **Henry Selby** and **Nelly Henderson** as the intended couple and **Jesse Henderson** as co-obligor. The bride's given name is absent from the FamilySearch index but legible on the original. The bond does not state Jesse's relationship to Nelly or confirm that the marriage was solemnized.

The 1828 and 1830 purchases from Jesse Henderson's heirs now carry more weight as a family-network correlation. They still do not prove that the purchaser was the 1809 groom, that Jesse was Nelly's father, or that Nelly and the later Eleanor were one woman.

Nicholas County marriage records add direct follow-down evidence. Four originals now identify daughters of Henry: Maria in 1832, Catharine in 1842, Juliann in 1845, and Dulcina in 1849. The first three bonds use the explicit form **daughter of Mr. Henry Selby of Nicholas County**; the 1849 certificate calls Dulcina **daughter of Henry Selby, resident of this county**. Henry signed Maria's and Catharine's bonds by mark.

Parker Selby signed as John Stitt's co-obligor on Juliann's 13 May 1845 bond. This is an important direct association with Henry's named daughter seven years after the natural-affection deed to Parker, but the bond does not call Parker Juliann's brother or state any other kinship.

The 1850 Nicholas County census records **Henry Selby, 66, born Maryland**, with **Eleanor Selby, 55, born Maryland**, and Olive and Polly Selby. The same paired names and county strongly identify this household with the married 1838 deed grantors. The census does not state household relationships, and no reviewed record directly equates 1809 Nelly with later Eleanor.

This chronology materially changes the Major problem. If the 1838/1850 Henry was the son named in Major's 1833 will, Henry's estimated 1784 birth makes the Worcester boy who was about seven in 1785 impossible as his father. It also makes the 1820 Nicholas male age 26-44 implausible as Henry's father. Because the later Henry-to-Major identity still lacks an explicit estate cross-reference, the result is a strong chronological conflict rather than identification of the correct older Major.

A Parker Selby, age 35 and born in Maryland, was enumerated in Bourbon County in 1850 and married Tabitha Irvin there in 1851. Those facts are compatible with the 1838 grantee. His 1845 signature on Juliann Selby's Nicholas County marriage bond now places him directly in Henry's family network, but none of these records names a parent or proves Parker's relationship to Henry.

### Earlier Parker and Captain John wording

A Worcester County administration de bonis non bond dated 15 June 1791 expressly identifies its deceased Parker Selby as **son of Captain John Selby**. It says John Selby, Esquire, had been the former administrator and was also deceased. This directly proves the stated Parker-to-Captain-John relationship for that estate, but it does not state John Esq.'s relationship to Parker.

Because several Parker and John Selbys coexist in these records, this finding is not merged with the ca. 1746/47 Parker reported in the legislative biography or with the Parker whose 1773 will named brothers James and John. The original estate opening, heirship, land division, or chancery pleading remains necessary.

### Benjamin and Sophia Selby in an 1872 Henry County deed

Henry County deed book 35, pages 525-526, directly identifies Sophia E. Selby as the titleholder and Ben Selby as her husband. The clerk's acknowledgment, dated 11 November 1872, again identifies the couple. This independently extends the Benjamin-Sophia relationship evidence beyond the 1854 license papers and 1880 census household.

The deed does not name either spouse's parents. It therefore strengthens the target couple's identity timeline without proving Major Selby as Benjamin's father.

### Benjamin Selby and the 1871 federal disability act

Private act chapter 171, approved 3 March 1871 and printed at 16 Stat. 694-695, removed Fourteenth Amendment legal and political disabilities from a list that includes "Benjamin Selby, Henry county." The target lawyer's established Henry County chronology makes the identity correlation strong, but the enacted list alone does not conclusively identify him or prove military service.

Contemporary Library of Congress newspaper images place a Col. Ben Selby in Henry County political activity in 1861 and 1866, in legal work in 1874, and in Henry County or Eminence public life through the 1880s. These notices support the correlation and point toward Reconstruction petition records, but none names his parents.

The relevant archival series is the U.S. House *Petitions and Other Records Relating to the Removal of Political Disabilities, July 1867-March 1871*, National Archives Identifier 563356. A targeted request for the Kentucky papers underlying private act chapter 171 is now a higher-value parentage and identity route than assuming Confederate military service from the title "Colonel."

### Henry County probate and deed searches

FamilySearch full-text review of the Henry County will index and wills volumes 15-16, 1873-1910, did not reveal a will of Benjamin Selby. This remains an OCR-dependent negative search.

The 1799-1888 deed index returned fifteen Selby-bearing images. It led to the verified 1872 Ben-Sophia deed, but no inspected entry or deed named Benjamin's parents. Exact-name searches across the deed-film groups were not technically conclusive because result counts did not consistently render; manual index and book-and-page work remains necessary.

### Older Adair County Benjamin separated

Kentucky historical summaries place a Benjamin Selby as an Adair County deputy sheriff in 1812 and at a Columbia law office around 1825. He cannot be the target groom who reported age 29 in January 1854. Original Adair records remain desirable, but the chronology is sufficient to keep the older lawyer's records out of the target identity cluster.

## Working rule

The family history can now accurately present the bounded upstream generation **Parker Selby, died 1746/47 -> eldest son John Selby** and the confirmed later chain **John Selby Esq. -> son John -> Major Selby**. The identification of Parker's eldest son with the 1790 John Selby Esq. is strong but still lacks an explicit later primary cross-reference. The younger Worcester Major is carried from minority through guardian accounts and into his own September 1803 receipt from grandmother Ann's estate.
