# FTDNA Selby profile summary - evidence-aware version

## Copy-ready profile text

**Paternal surname:** Selby  
**Tester:** Hamrick Aethelred Selby II  
**Earliest working paternal ancestor:** Major Selby, reportedly born 1 Aug. 1783 in Maryland and died 13 Nov. 1861 in Hamilton County, Indiana; reportedly married Harriet Messick/Mezick in 1817 and migrated through Kentucky to Indiana.

**Identity correction:** a different Major Selby of Nicholas County, Kentucky, made a will in 1833 naming wife Peggy and children Henry and Polly and was dead when that will was proved in August 1833. He cannot be the Hamilton County, Indiana, man alive in 1850 and 1860. The reported Kentucky-to-Indiana migration for the paternal target is therefore not established by the Bourbon/Nicholas records.

I am seeking to identify Major Selby's parents and to place his direct paternal line within the colonial Eastern Shore Selby families of Maryland and Virginia. The paper trail before Major remains unresolved.

An important identity issue is under investigation: the 1783 Maryland tax assessment lists an adult landholder named **Major Selby** with 140 acres of "Choice" in Acquango Hundred, Worcester County. That taxpayer cannot automatically be the Major Selby reportedly born in 1783; he may be an older man of the same name. No father-son relationship is assumed.

A reviewed 1766 will establishes limited family context: Angelo Atkinson called Sarah Selby and William Atkinson Selby his grandchildren, and identified them as the daughter and son of John Selby. The will does not say whether John was Angelo's son or son-in-law, name John's wife, or name Major Selby. Any connection from Major to this documented group remains unproved. I welcome comparison with testers who have a documented, unbroken direct male line to early Eastern Shore Selby ancestors, especially Thomas Selby Sr. (c. 1635–1702), but I do not claim that descent at present.

**Research request to project administrators:** Please compare this kit's STR and SNP results with documented Selby paternal-line groups, identify its closest tested matches and confirmed terminal SNP, and advise which additional Y-DNA testing would best distinguish the competing Maryland Selby lines.
