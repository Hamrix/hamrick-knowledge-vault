# Research Lead Sweep - 2026-07-28

This note records a targeted public-source lead hunt for unresolved Aetheling Evidence Project questions. The sweep focused on [[Major Selby Sr parentage remains unproven]], [[Etheldred Peters parentage remains unproven]], Thomas Selby/Selby network leads, and the previously unresolved Frederic/Frederick Ethelred Selby identity target.

## Bottom line

No parentage question was solved in this sweep. The strongest new value is a sharper set of record targets:

- Major Selby's 1797 Poplar Hill / Exon Enlarged chancery case now has a wider land-estate network: John Selby held the same tract pair in 1783, and the same tract pair appears in a later Zadock Selby estate chancery case in 1810.
- Etheldred Peters has a specific 1787 Craven County land-caveat lead that points to his patent, courses, distances, and neighboring landholders.
- A public indexed person record appears for a specific Frederick Ethelred Selby, born 1911 and died 1992, which changes the Frederic/Frederick target from "no reliable identity-specific hit found" to "specific candidate found; source images and identity context still unverified."

## Major Selby Sr.: new land-estate cluster

### New lead: John Selby held Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged in 1783

The Maryland 1783 Worcester County assessment index records a John Selby entry for **Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged**, 855 acres. This matters because the already-known 1797 case involving Major Selby concerned the same tract pair.

Working implication: the 1797 packet may not merely be about land near Selbys; it may explain whether Major Selby had an interest flowing through a John Selby estate, sale, trust, marriage right, debt, or other legal route.

This does **not** prove John was Major's father.

Source: [Maryland State Archives, 1783 Worcester County assessment](https://www.msa.maryland.gov/msa/stagser/s1400/s1437/html/1437wo.html).

### Existing high-value lead confirmed: 1797 Major Selby chancery packet

The chancery index lists:

- *Daniel Patrick, William Patrick, Patrick Waters Jr., and Matilda Waters v. Major Selby*
- Filed 22 Dec. 1797
- Worcester County
- Petition to sell land
- Tracts: Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged
- MSA S512-6-4300
- Accession 17,898-4177
- Location 1/37/1

This remains the first packet to retrieve.

Source: [Maryland State Archives, Chancery Court Papers index, 1797-1798](https://msa.maryland.gov/msa/stagser/s500/s512/html/s512d.html).

### New lead: Zadock Selby estate case also names Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged

The Maryland chancery index for 1810 lists:

- *George Hall vs. William Selby of Zadock*
- Date: 1810
- Worcester County
- Subject: estate of Zadock Selby
- Tracts: Nums Green, Poplar Hill, Exon Enlarged, Key, Addition to Gander, Duers Request
- Recorded in Chancery Record 91, p. 233
- MSA S512-3-2427

Working implication: Poplar Hill and Exon Enlarged may have moved through or been litigated around multiple Selby estates. This creates a tract cluster to reconstruct across John, Major, Zadock, and William Selby of Zadock.

This does **not** prove Major's parentage. It creates a record-retrieval path.

Source: [Maryland State Archives, Chancery Court Papers index, 1809-1810](https://msa.maryland.gov/msa/stagser/s500/s512/html/s512j.html).

### Supporting Selby estate context: Parker Selby cases

The chancery index also points to Parker Selby estate litigation:

- *Joseph Delestatius and Sarah Delestatius v. John Selby*, filed 12 Nov. 1795, Worcester County, subject: estate of Parker Selby; tracts Venture, Cedar Grove, Proventun, and Robertsons Inheritence; Chancery Record 38, p. 341; MSA S512-6-4837; accession 17,898-4668; location 1/37/1.
- *John Selby v. Anthony Bacon and George S. Gunby*, filed 22 Jul. 1808, Worcester County, subject: estate of Parker Selby; tracts Prevention and Addition to Willetts Outlet; Chancery Record 105, p. 770; MSA S512-6-5087; accession 17,898-4936; location 1/37/1.

These corrected index entries do not share the Poplar Hill / Exon Enlarged tract pair, so they are broader Selby-estate context rather than direct support for a Major-Selby land connection. See [[Maryland State Archives - Parker Selby chancery case indexes]].

Working implication: Parker/John/Zadock/William Selby estate files may be necessary to place Major within or outside the Worcester Selby network.

Sources:

- [Maryland State Archives, Chancery Court Papers index, 1795-1796](https://msa.maryland.gov/msa/stagser/s500/s512/html/s512c.html)
- [Maryland State Archives, Chancery Court Papers index, 1807-1808](https://msa.maryland.gov/msa/stagser/s500/s512/html/s512i.html)

### MSA biographical dictionary clue: William of John identity problem

The Maryland State Archives biographical dictionary entry for William Atkinson Selby discusses the John Selby / Mary Atkinson family and warns that the identity of "William of John" is complicated. It notes that John Selby died in 1790 and that his will bequeathed all real and personal estate to his son William, in trust for William's children.

Working implication: the John Selby will and trust structure should be read before attaching Major to any John/William/Zadock line.

### Evidence update — 2026-08-03

The reviewed manuscript will of Angelo Atkinson (dated 3 February 1766; proved 3 May 1766) independently confirms that Sarah Selby and William Atkinson Selby were his grandchildren and the daughter and son of John Selby. It does not identify John's wife, say whether John was Angelo's son or son-in-law, or connect Major Selby to this group. See [[FamilySearch - Angelo Atkinson 1766 Worcester County will images]] and [[Angelo Atkinson was grandfather of Sarah Selby and William Atkinson Selby, children of John Selby]].

Source: [Maryland State Archives, A Biographical Dictionary of the Maryland Legislature, vol. 426, p. 721](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc2900/sc2908/000001/000426/html/am426--721.html).

## Etheldred Peters: new land-caveat lead

### New lead: 1787 Peters v. Vanpelt land caveat

A published abstract of Craven County court minutes includes a September 1787 caveat involving **Etheldred Peters** and **Anthony Vanpelt**.

The abstract says the issue concerned a warrant and survey made outside Peters's lines. It states that Peters had "his compliment of land" according to the courses and distances of his patent. It also gives a jury list that may help reconstruct the neighborhood.

Named men in the abstract include:

- Brisco Davis
- John Philips
- George Browning
- Joseph Allen
- Thomas Coleman
- Francis Rountree
- William Rountree
- Brittain King
- Moses Spivey
- Ezekiel Adams
- Edward Clark
- Jonathan Killeren

Working implication: this is a concrete path to Etheldred's patent, survey, and neighboring landholders. It should be correlated with the 1786 tax-list lands: 400 acres where he lived and 120 acres on Little Contentnea/Contentney known as Warehouse Landing.

This does **not** identify Etheldred's father.

Source: [East Carolina Roots, Craven County court-minute abstracts, 1767-1791](https://eastcarolinaroots.com/king-mentions-craven-county-court-minutes-1767-1791/).

## Frederick Ethelred Selby: identity lead found

### New lead: exact indexed person page

An exact search surfaced a public indexed FamilySearch person page for **Frederick Ethelred Selby**, FamilySearch id **LLWQ-6MV**. The indexed summary identifies a man born 21 Dec. 1911 in Louisiana, son of James Benjamin Sanford Selby, and died in November 1992 with New Orleans/Louisiana context.

This is enough to revise the prior negative-search status: an exact candidate now exists.

It is not enough to make a biography. The linked source images and identity chain have not been reviewed in this sweep.

Source: [FamilySearch person page, Frederick Ethelred Selby, LLWQ-6MV](https://ancestors.familysearch.org/en/LLWQ-6MV/frederick-ethelred-selby-1911-1992).

## Compiled-tree cautions

Compiled trees and genealogy forum posts continue to claim variants of:

- a younger Major Selby born around 1781/1783;
- marriages to Leah Larmer and Harriet Messick/Mezick;
- an older Major Selby and Margaret/Peggy Parker as parents.

These may be useful as search terms, but they are not proof. They also intensify the identity problem: an adult Major Selby appearing in a 1783 Worcester assessment cannot be the same person as a Major Selby born in 1783. The next step is to separate the older Maryland adult from the later Kentucky/Indiana man by records, not by tree narrative.
