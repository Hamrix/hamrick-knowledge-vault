## Assertions not promoted

- The submitted Stafford sequence from Adam Stafford to Ethelred R. Stafford was not promoted because no original wills, marriage record, or other source images for those asserted relationships were supplied in this batch.
- The submitted Peters sequence was not promoted as a proved lineage. The 1904 volume is a compiled genealogy and contains an internal conflict about William Peters's first wife.
- The 1904 volume does not name Etheldred or Ethelred Peters, Stafford, Chaney, or Cheney anywhere in the extracted text. It therefore does not support William Peters as Etheldred's father, Etheldred as Elizabeth Peters's father, or a Stafford-Peters bridge.
- The 1786 tax-list phrase identifies John Peters as Etheldred's son, not Etheldred as John's son. See [[Etheldred Peters parentage remains unproven]].
- "Lord Petrie" is preserved as printed. Interpreting that phrase as Baron Petre remains a research hypothesis.

## Follow-up Peters assessment

A second pasted assessment described the 1904 book as a definitive primary source that confirms the user's Peters lineage and connects Etheldred Peters to its Liverpool family. No new record image, Stafford Society citation, or independent source accompanied that assessment.

### What the assessment accurately reports

- The book presents a compiled line from Ralph Peters through William Peters, Judge Richard Peters, Ralph Peters, and Richard Peters.
- The book says that Judge Richard was William Peters's son by Mary Brientnall and reports later generations of that family.
- Printed page 64 reproduces a family claim of connection to the noble house of "Lord Petrie" and reports matching arms and motto.
- The book says some information came from family letters, a family Bible or prayer book, and other family materials.

These statements describe what the 1904 book reports. They do not independently verify that the user descends from the people named in the book.

### Corrections and proof boundaries

- The book is a **compiled genealogy**, not one primary source for every event and relationship. A Bible entry, letter, or recollection quoted by a later compiler retains its own authorship, date, provenance, and reliability questions. The original item should be retrieved where possible.
- No supplied record connects the user or the established Selby/Stafford research segment to Judge Richard Peters or Richard Peters of Atlanta. Calling either man a direct ancestor is therefore unsupported.
- The book contains no `Etheldred` or `Ethelred` entry and supplies no descent from its Liverpool family to Etheldred Peters of Craven County.
- The phrase "claims to be a branch" documents a tradition, not descent. Reported similarity of arms and motto is a research lead, not relationship-level evidence.
- A Stephen Stafford-Elizabeth Peters marriage record, if retrieved, could prove that the named couple married. By itself it would not prove Elizabeth's parents, connect her to Etheldred Peters, or connect Etheldred to the Liverpool family. Each of those is a separate generational claim requiring its own evidence.
- The uncited reference to confirmation by a Stafford Society is a lead only. The exact publication, author, page, sources, and any underlying original records must be identified before its statements can be evaluated.

No person note, relationship claim, or lineage merge was created from the follow-up assessment.
