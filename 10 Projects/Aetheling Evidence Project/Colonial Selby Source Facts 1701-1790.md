# Colonial Selby Source Facts, 1701–1790

## Purpose

This ledger extracts source-supported facts already present in the Hamrick Knowledge Vault. It preserves each record as a bounded fact and does not join same-named people unless the underlying document states the relationship or identity.

## Evidence rule

- A relationship is recorded as confirmed only when the reviewed record uses explicit relationship language.
- A land, tax, probate, or guardianship entry confirms only the person, event, property, or relationship actually stated.
- Same names, overlapping counties, repeated tract names, and chronological plausibility remain correlation clues rather than identity proof.

## Confirmed source facts

### 1701–1702 — Thomas Selby named a son Thomas Selby

Thomas Selby of Somerset County, Maryland, made a will dated 28 October 1701. The reviewed manuscript twice identified another Thomas Selby as his son: once in a bequest and again when appointing him sole executor.

- Claim: [[Thomas Selby of Somerset County was father of a Thomas Selby named in his 1701 will]]
- Source: [[Maryland State Archives - Thomas Selby 1701 will images]]
- Confirmed fact: the testator Thomas Selby had a son named Thomas Selby.
- Limit: the son is not identified in the will as the Thomas Selby whose later Worcester County will was proved in 1758.

### 1725 — The Simpleton grantor was grandson of the tract’s Thomas Selby patentee

A Somerset County deed dated 12 October 1725 records Thomas Selby and Margaret Selby conveying the remaining land and marsh in the 1,250-acre tract called *Simpleton* to William Selby. The deed calls the grantor Thomas Selby the grandson of the deceased Thomas Selby who held the patent.

- Claim: [[Thomas Selby who conveyed Simpleton in 1725 was grandson of its Thomas Selby patentee]]
- Source: [[Maryland State Archives - Thomas Selby 1725 Simpleton deed]]
- Confirmed fact: the 1725 grantor Thomas Selby was a grandson of the Thomas Selby identified in the deed as the *Simpleton* patentee.
- Limit: the deed does not identify the intervening father sufficiently to connect this descent automatically to the 1701 will or the 1758 Worcester will.

### 1757–1758 — Thomas Selby of Worcester County named John Selby as his son

The reviewed Worcester County manuscript will identifies the testator as Thomas Selby Senior, planter, and gives a 120-acre plantation to “my Son John Selby.” The will was dated 15 December 1757, and the witnesses’ probate oath was recorded on 8 March 1758.

- Claim: [[Thomas Selby of Worcester County was the father of John Selby]]
- Source: [[FamilySearch - Thomas Selby Sr 1757 Worcester County will images]]
- Confirmed fact: the testator Thomas Selby was the father of the John Selby named in that will.
- Limit: the record does not prove the testator’s parents or connect him to the 1701 testator, the 1725 *Simpleton* grantor, Major Selby, or a modern family.

### 1766 — Angelo Atkinson identified Sarah Selby and William Atkinson Selby as grandchildren and children of John Selby

In his Worcester County will, Angelo Atkinson called Sarah Selby his granddaughter and William Atkinson Selby his grandson, then identified them as the daughter and son of John Selby. The will was dated 3 February 1766 and proved 3 May 1766.

- Claim: [[Angelo Atkinson was grandfather of Sarah Selby and William Atkinson Selby, children of John Selby]]
- Source: [[FamilySearch - Angelo Atkinson 1766 Worcester County will images]]
- Confirmed facts: Angelo Atkinson described Sarah Selby as his granddaughter and William Atkinson Selby as his grandson; he identified Sarah and William as the daughter and son of John Selby.
- Limit: the reviewed clause does not identify John Selby’s wife, connect Major Selby to this group, or establish the parent of a separately named grandson John Selby.

### 1783 — An adult Major Selby was indexed with part of *Choice*

The Maryland State Archives’ official 1783 assessment index lists Major Selby in Acquango Hundred, Worcester County, with part of the tract *Choice*, totaling 140 acres.

- Claim: [[Major Selby was assessed for part of Choice in Worcester County in 1783]]
- Source: [[Maryland State Archives - 1783 Worcester assessment Major Selby]]
- Confirmed fact: the official index associates a person named Major Selby with a 140-acre assessed interest in part of *Choice* in 1783.
- Limit: this is an archival index rather than a reviewed original assessment image. It gives no age, parentage, ownership history, or chain of title.

### 1785 — A minor Major Selby was recorded as the orphan of John Selby, deceased

A Worcester County guardian bond dated 1 July 1785 identifies Major Selby as about seven years old and as the orphan of John Selby, deceased. John Selby Esquire was appointed guardian until Major reached fourteen. Zadock Selby and Parker Selby signed as co-obligors. A related estate-view certificate dated 25 October 1785 again calls Major the orphan of John Selby.

- Claim: [[1785 Worcester guardianship record identifies Major Selby as orphan of John Selby]]
- Source: [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1785 Worcester guardianship estate-view image]]
- Confirmed facts: the minor Major Selby was reported to be about seven on 1 July 1785; his deceased father was named John Selby; John Selby Esquire served as guardian; Zadock Selby and Parker Selby were bond co-obligors.
- Limit: the records state no relationship between the guardian, co-obligors, and the child beyond the guardian role. They do not identify Major’s mother or prove that this minor was any later Major Selby.

### 1790 — John Selby named James, Daniel, and William Selby as sons

A Worcester County will dated 20 November 1790 and proved 10 December 1790 names James Selby, Daniel Selby, and William Selby as sons of the testator John Selby.

- Claim: [[John Selby named James Daniel and William Selby as sons in his 1790 will]]
- Source: [[FamilySearch - John Selby 1790 Worcester County will images]]
- Confirmed fact: the testator John Selby was the father of the named James, Daniel, and William Selby.
- Limit: the will does not identify John’s parents and does not prove that he was the John named in the 1758 Thomas Selby will, the father in the 1785 Major Selby bond, or another same-named Worcester County man.

## Major Selby identity conflict

The 1783 assessment and 1785 guardianship records must not be merged without further evidence:

- the 1783 assessment concerns a person functioning as an assessed adult land-interest holder;
- the 1785 guardian bond concerns a child about seven years old;
- both records use the name Major Selby in Worcester County;
- the records therefore create an identity conflict, not a continuous biography.

The project must preserve both entries independently until original assessment, guardianship, deed, release, marriage, probate, and migration records distinguish the individuals.

## What this ledger does not establish

This ledger does not prove:

- a continuous descent from the *Simpleton* patentee through the 1701, 1725, and 1758 Thomas Selby records;
- that the John Selby in the 1758 will is the John Selby in the 1766 Atkinson will, the deceased father in the 1785 guardianship, or the 1790 testator;
- that the minor Major Selby of 1785 became the later Maryland-born Major Selby found in Kentucky or Indiana;
- any connection from these colonial records to the modern Selby–Stone or Selby–Stafford families;
- any noble, Plantagenet, Wessex, or royal descent.
