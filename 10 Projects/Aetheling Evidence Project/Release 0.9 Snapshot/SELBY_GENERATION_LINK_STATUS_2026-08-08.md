## Identity quarantines

1. **Medieval lookalikes:** the GEDCOM's William Atkinson Selbee (1457–1509) and Sarah White Townsend (1462–1509) cannot represent the 1782 Maryland couple.
2. **Angelo Atkinson collision:** Joshua Atkinson's son was under twenty-one and living in 1773, so he cannot be the older Angelo whose will was proved in 1766. At least two identities are required.
3. **Major Selby collision:** the adult 1783 Worcester landholder cannot be the man a compiled profile says was born about 1781–1783. The 1811 Bourbon and two original 1820 Nicholas records require their own correlation.
4. **William Selby collision:** a William Selby buying at an 1813 Bourbon estate sale cannot be the W. A. Selby described as deceased in January 1811 unless a date or reading is wrong.
5. **Polly attachment error:** the original 1825 file names Major—not William Atkinson Selby—as Polly's father.
6. **Benjamin name collision:** the original 1840 Frankfort row gives its Benjamin head age fifty to fifty-nine and no male age fifteen to nineteen. It neither represents nor places the Benjamin born about 1824–1827.
7. **Docia index error:** Robert C. Selby's original 1938 certificate reads Docie/Docia Stone; the FamilySearch index's “Dora Stone” is not the controlling reading.

## Proof standard used

- **Verified, high:** original or official record directly states the relationship and the identity and chronology fit.
- **Sourced, unverified, moderate:** an authoritative derivative or older transcript states the relationship, but the underlying original is missing or incomplete.
- **Inference, low/moderate:** multiple facts suggest a link without directly naming it; competing identities and negative evidence are explicitly weighed.
- **Tradition/myth:** family tradition or ceremonial narrative is preserved as history of the claim, not promoted as genealogical fact.
- **Conflicted:** a cited record says something different, cannot be located as described, or merges incompatible identities.
