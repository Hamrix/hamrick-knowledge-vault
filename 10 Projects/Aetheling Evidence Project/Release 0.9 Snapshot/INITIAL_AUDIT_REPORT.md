# Aetheling History - Initial Source-First Audit

Audit date: 24 July 2026  
Steward: Aitheling Steward  
Scope: 15 files in `sources/` (read-only)

## Executive finding

The corpus is a valuable research collection, but it is not yet proof of the portfolio's principal lineage, title, estate, or legal claims. It mixes (1) reproduced scholarly pedigree material, (2) a large user-assembled GEDCOM, (3) modern narrative dossiers that repeat one another, and (4) scans or extracts that may support individual facts. Repetition across the modern dossiers is not independent corroboration.

The principal public-facing claims - descent from the Anglo-Saxon royal house, the style "Last Aetheling," entitlement to Selby Manor/Whitehouse, filing status, and legal rights arising from historic descent - must remain unpublished as verified facts until each generational link, property identity, archive reference, and legal proposition is independently checked against an authoritative record.

## Corpus inventory

- 6 PDFs, totaling 152 pages.
- 7 DOCX files, totaling 314 paragraphs.
- 1 GEDCOM containing 3,026 individual records, 774 family records, 259 source records, 2 repository records, and 1,042 media records.
- 1 text file containing a draft partnership letter.
- SHA-256 hashes and exact filenames are preserved in `source-inventory.json`.

## Immediate red flags

1. A draft Historic England letter uses title number `NYK12345` and a target-month placeholder. These are not evidence of a registered title or completed filing.
2. The corpus assigns unsupported percentage scores such as 96.8%, 97.2%, and 97.9%. These are removed from the evidence method.
3. Several dossiers state that filings, archive records, title rights, and property values are confirmed without including independently verifiable receipts, official copies, decision letters, or complete archival images.
4. A pedigree exhibit contains private birth-certificate references and a DNA kit identifier. These are quarantined from all public outputs.
5. The modern dossiers contain inconsistent recent-generation dates and relationships. For example, one exhibit describes Hamrick E. Selby I as born in 1960 and the father of the subject, while another narrative describes a person of the same name as born in 1949 and the grandfather. These cannot both be accepted without resolution.
6. Modern narrative documents refer to named institutions that may be informal, nonexistent, or not competent to validate the asserted claim. No institutional endorsement should be implied.
7. Legal assertions about land, manorial rights, bona vacantia, peerage, or the right to petition are legal opinions until reviewed against current law and the facts of a specific property or estate.

## Classification method

- **Verified fact**: supported by a legible primary record or authoritative official database, with identity and context matched.
- **Sourced but unverified**: a source is named or reproduced, but the record, transcription, identity match, or citation has not yet been independently checked.
- **Inference**: a reasoned connection that is not expressly stated by the evidence.
- **Tradition or myth**: family, ceremonial, cultural, or legendary narrative presented without documentary proof.
- **Legal opinion**: a proposition about rights, title, standing, ownership, succession, or remedy requiring legal analysis.
- **Conflicted or disproven**: contradicted by another record or internally inconsistent. "Disproven" should be used only when decisive contrary evidence exists.

## Confidence levels

- **High**: multiple independent sources, including at least one primary or official record, agree on the material point.
- **Moderate**: credible evidence exists, but identity, completeness, or an important link remains open.
- **Low**: derivative material, unsourced assertion, weak identity match, or material contradiction.
- **Not assessed**: queued for review; no conclusion should be inferred.

## Privacy decision

No public page includes living-person dates, addresses, private email, phone number, birth-certificate numbers or images, DNA kit identifiers, or private family records. GEDCOM data is treated as private-by-default. Public person profiles require a deceased-person determination or explicit approval.

## Initial conclusion

The defensible public story today is about an ongoing historical investigation and its methods, not a proven royal, noble, estate, or legal entitlement. The first website version therefore publishes the audit method, corpus-level provenance, a redacted claim ledger, contradiction log, research tasks, and ways to contribute evidence. It deliberately does not publish the source files themselves or solicit donations before governance, payment handling, and representations are reviewed.
