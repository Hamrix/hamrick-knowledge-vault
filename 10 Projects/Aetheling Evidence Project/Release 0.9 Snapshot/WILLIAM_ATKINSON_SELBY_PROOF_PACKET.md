# William Atkinson Selby proof packet

Steward review date: 8 August 2026  
Scope: the four requested proof targets and the immediate generation links they can or cannot establish  
Rule: confidence is qualitative and based on source quality, identity fit, independence, and completeness—not an unsupported percentage.

## Executive finding

The packet proves two parent-child links:

1. Angelo Atkinson → John Selby → William Atkinson Selby: **verified, high confidence**. Angelo's proved 1766 will directly calls William a son of John Selby and Angelo's grandson.
2. William A. Selby → Rhody Selby: **verified, high confidence**. A 7 January 1811 Bourbon County original calls Rhody the infant orphan of W. A. Selby, deceased.

A newly accessioned August 1780 Worcester County Orphans' Court order also names John Selby and William Atkinson Selby in a seven-person group of John Atkinson's residuary legatees. Its appeal bond supplies the testator's will date—21 March 1779—and repeats the group. This is strong Atkinson-family-network evidence, but neither page states the legatees' individual relationships; the underlying will is therefore the best immediate proof target.

The packet does **not** prove William → Major Selby. No inspected original names Major as William's child. The 1825 Nicholas County marriage file sometimes attached to William's online profile actually names **Major Selby** as Polly Selby's father. That attachment is a false bridge.

The requested “1809 Bourbon probate” has not been found. The record recovered is an 1811 orphan order, not a will, inventory, settlement, guardianship packet, or complete heir list.

## Target 1 — 1782 Selby–Townsend marriage

**Claim:** William Atkinson Selby married Sarah White Townsend on 28 February 1782, associated with Snow Hill/Worcester County, Maryland.

**Status:** sourced but unverified  
**Confidence:** moderate

**Evidence inspected:** a 1929 published transcript attributed to the Headen family Bible, preserved as `acquisitions/secondary/G006169.pdf`, page 15. The file is 1,770,307 bytes; SHA-256 `0690212592D12B114BF80A747E16732CFE6F0404140C0F1B85484FD5CB6DEC15`.

**What it supports:** the couple and exact marriage date have a named derivative source earlier than the present online trees.

**What it does not yet support:** the original Bible has not been inspected; the title page, publication date, handwriting, custody, and full family-register context are unknown. No contemporaneous church or civil entry has yet been found. Worcester County's surviving official marriage series does not cover 1782.

**Required closure evidence:** RR-012—locate and image the original Bible and independently search parish/church registers, minister returns, license or bond material, family papers, newspapers, and neighboring jurisdictions.

## Target 2 — 1779 Worcester County treason matter

**Claim:** William Atkinson Selby was indicted in June 1779 for going over to the enemy.

**Status:** sourced but unverified  
**Confidence:** moderate

**Evidence inspected:** the official Maryland State Archives legislative biography reports the event and points to the Worcester County Court criminal series. The likely underlying volume is C2035-2, 1778–1779, K reverse, location 01/56/02/010, MdHR 9706-2; the microfilm surrogate is CM1112-2, pages 1–120, with search-room access indicated.

**What it supports:** an authoritative repository publication identifies the accusation and archival series.

**What it does not yet support:** the indictment, recognizance, securities, witnesses, plea, continuances, and final disposition have not been viewed. The biography cannot substitute for those originals.

**Required closure evidence:** RR-013—obtain every page in the criminal sequence and related loose papers; RR-014—correlate the reported 1780 militia record as a possible identity and outcome test.

## Target 3 — death, estate, and Bourbon County connection

**Requested claim:** an 1809 Bourbon County probate or estate file names William's wife, children, and heirs.

**Status:** conflicted as a record citation  
**Confidence in the correction:** high

**Evidence inspected:** 

- The 1929 notes in `G006169.pdf`, page 13, label the exact 2 August 1809 Worcester death as tradition.
- The official Worcester County estate index, `acquisitions/official/maryland-state-archives/mdsa_c2517_1.pdf`, contains no matching 1809 William Atkinson Selby estate entry. SHA-256: `8AF33F822328CB905A02D2F1C7F154282409F911A28F57799F6198C89C14D85D`.
- A 7 January 1811 Bourbon County original binds out Rhody Selby and directly describes Rhody as the infant orphan of W. A. Selby, deceased. The preserved image is `acquisitions/official/familysearch/3Q9M-CSLX-V5KP/image.jpg`, 1,019,836 bytes, SHA-256 `9FD7124D128DC89F261F4C3A74797755EB6FC2D2FF0A19B6C703C60C05DFA574`.

**What it proves:** William died before 7 January 1811; Rhody was his child; Rhody would turn eleven on the following 17 April, implying a birth date near 17 April 1800.

**What it does not prove:** the exact death date or place, a 1809 Bourbon probate, William's wife, all children, Major as a child, or the complete estate.

**Required closure evidence:** RR-016—search Bourbon and Worcester probate, administration, inventory, sale, settlement, guardian, apprenticeship, deed, tax, and adjacent court-order series for 1808–1812.

## Target 4 — Angelo Atkinson's 1766 will

**Claim tested:** Mary Atkinson's parentage and the origin of William Atkinson Selby's middle name.

**Status of Angelo → William and John → William:** verified  
**Confidence:** high

**Original source:** Angelo Atkinson's proved will, Maryland Prerogative Court, Liber 34, folio 156, in MSA S538-50. The complete official volume is preserved as `acquisitions/official/maryland-state-archives/msa_s538_000050.pdf`; the clause is on PDF page 158. File size: 89,143,054 bytes; SHA-256 `679C9B3E01BC65D6A46C8933FD2E65E341EEE40FB903E19A83CB1DA4C5D0C7AC`.

**Direct relationship content:** Angelo calls Sarah Selby and William Atkinson Selby his grandchildren and identifies both as children of John Selby. He also makes a separate bequest to grandson John Selby.

**Important correction:** the clause does **not** name Mary Atkinson. It proves that William was Angelo's grandson and John's son, but it does not directly state which of Angelo's children was William's mother. The Maryland State Archives biography identifies Mary Atkinson as William's mother, so that link remains sourced but unverified at moderate confidence pending a direct record.

**Ethical note:** the bequest includes an enslaved woman named Phillis. Any public exhibit must preserve her personhood and historical context and must not present the clause solely as a pedigree device.

**Required closure evidence:** RR-015—obtain Angelo's full probate packet, inventory, administration accounts, legatee receipts, guardian material, deeds, releases, and any John Selby–Mary Atkinson marriage evidence.

## Evidence discovered while testing the bridge

### John Atkinson estate dispute, Worcester County, 1780

The August 1780 distribution order names Angelo Atkinson, John Atkinson, John Selby, William Atkinson Selby, Rachel Flemming, Mary White, and Nancy White as residuary legatees of John Atkinson. The 25 August appeal bond repeats the obligees, recites John Atkinson's will of 21 March 1779, and identifies widow/executrix Mary as the later wife of Levin Blake. Both original pages are preserved and hashed in ACC-025.

This proves that John Selby and William Atkinson Selby stood together in John Atkinson's residuary estate. It does not prove whether the group were children, grandchildren, children-in-law, or a mixture. RR-020 now requests the will, accounts, receipts, and final appeal disposition specifically to resolve those relationships.

### Scarborough Parker estate account, Worcester County

An estate-account continuation records an allowed payment to William Atkinson Selby after Scarborough Parker's inventory was exhibited in 1789. Both original pages are preserved in ACC-024. FamilySearch full-text discovery labeled the item as Bourbon County material, but the document's heading and parties identify Worcester County; the original controls the citation. This adds another contemporary Maryland identity and associate cluster without proving parentage.

### At least two men named Angelo Atkinson

Joshua Atkinson's will, proved in 1773, names his underage son Angelo and directs that Solomon Townsend care for him, with William Townsend as successor before age twenty-one. That young man cannot be Angelo Atkinson, deceased by the proof of his own will in 1766. The one-person identity claim is disproven at high confidence, and the two Angelos are now separated in the ledger.

### Major Selby, Bourbon County, 1811

A deed dated 27 June 1811 and recorded 3 February 1812 conveys about 100 acres to Major Selby “of the County of Bourbon.” The deed is in Book H, page 456. Original and index images are preserved with SHA-256 hashes in ACC-020. This proves an adult Major Selby in Bourbon County, but no kinship to William.

### Major Selby, Nicholas County, 1820–1825

Two 1820 census index entries exist for Major Selby on Nicholas County pages 111 and 129. Both distinct original pages are now preserved and hashed. They are not yet correlated as separate households, duplicates, or an index problem; household-column transcription and tax/deed correlation remain required.

On 16 August 1825, William Antrobus's marriage authorization and bond both name Polly Selby as Major Selby's daughter. The authorization says her father personally consented; Major joined the bond by mark. These are direct, high-confidence records.

### False online attachment

The 1825 Polly record is attached in FamilySearch to William Atkinson Selby's compiled profile, even though the original names Major as her father and William was already dead by January 1811. The attachment must not be cited as evidence that Polly was William's daughter or that Major was William's son.
