# Aetheling Evidence Project

## Objective

Audit, document, and present the Selby, Stafford, Plantagenet, Wessex, Haskins, and related lineage claims using explicit evidence standards.

## Canonical research workflow

- Begin every Aetheling or family-history task with the Hamrick Knowledge Vault.
- Treat the local vault at `C:\Users\hamri\OneDrive\Documents\Hamrick-Knowledge-Vault` as the canonical compiled working layer and `Hamrix/hamrick-knowledge-vault` as its versioned GitHub copy.
- Treat reviewed primary-source images, original records, and exact transcriptions as the ultimate evidentiary authority.
- Preserve the exact name written in each source while recording correlated identities separately.
- Exclude the current GEDCOM from evidentiary analysis unless the user explicitly requests it; it requires updating and may contain duplicated, merged, or incorrect identities.
- Do not promote an online tree, family narrative, AI report, surname match, or repeated assertion into a proved relationship without identity-specific evidence.

See [[Vault-First Research Rules]].

## Current state

The project now contains both:

1. confirmed historical context and atomic medieval parent-child relationships; and
2. reviewed modern family records establishing several bounded Selby and Stafford relationships.

The modern records do not yet supply a continuous bridge to the colonial Maryland Selbys, the medieval Stafford nobility, the Plantagenets, or the House of Wessex. Each proposed bridge remains a separate proof problem.

See [[Modern Selby Stafford Evidence State]].

## Evidence standard

Use the Genealogical Proof Standard:

- reasonably exhaustive research;
- complete and accurate source citations;
- analysis and correlation;
- resolution of conflicts;
- a soundly reasoned written conclusion.

Confidence labels apply to the precise claim stated in each note. A confirmed census enumeration or reported parent field does not automatically confirm every larger lineage conclusion built around it.

## Modern documented anchors

### Benjamin Selby and Sophia Docia Elizabeth Stone Selby family

- [[Frederick Payne Selby 1884 marriage license identifies Benjamin Selby as his father]] — confirmed from the reviewed 1884 Orleans Parish license and corroborated by the 1880 census.
- [[Frederick Payne Selby 1884 marriage license identifies Docia Stone as his mother]] — confirmed from the reviewed 1884 license and corroborated by the 1880 census.
- [[Sophia E Docia and Dora Stone Selby are treated as one correlated identity]] — strong project-level identity correlation uniting the record forms Sophia E. Stone, Sophia Stone, Sophia Selby, Docia/Docie Stone or Selby, and Dora Stone under the preferred name [[Sophia Docia Elizabeth Stone Selby]].
- [[A marriage license was issued for Benjamin Selby and Sophia E Stone in 1854]] — confirmed license issuance; the reviewed papers do not yet contain a minister's return.
- [[Sophia Bryan identified Sophia Stone as her daughter in 1854 marriage-license consent]] — confirmed direct maternal statement for the named 1854 parties.
- [[Benjn Selby and Sophia Selby were enumerated in the same 1860 Oldham County household]] — confirmed co-enumeration; the 1860 schedule does not state relationships.
- [[Robert C Selbys 1938 death certificate reports Benjamin Selby and Dora Stone as parents]] — confirmed as the information reported on the certificate.

The preferred full identity **Sophia Docia Elizabeth Stone Selby** is retained as the project's correlated conclusion. The reviewed 1854 primary record gives only **Sophia E. Stone**; an explicit record spelling out **Elizabeth** remains to be cited.

### James B. Selby and Sarah E. Stafford or Selby family

- [[Frederick E Selby was enumerated as Sarah E Staffords son in 1930]] — strongly supported by the reviewed New Orleans census image.
- [[Frederick E Selby 1953 marriage license identifies James B Selby as his father]] — strongly supported by the reviewed official license.
- [[Frederick E Selby 1953 marriage license identifies Sarah E Selby as his mother]] — strongly supported by the 1953 license and the independent 1930 census.
- [[Frederick E Selby married Frances Marion Haskins in 1953]] — confirmed by the reviewed license and certificate.

These records support the bounded conclusion that James B. Selby and Sarah E. Stafford/Selby were Frederick E. Selby's reported parents. They do not yet identify James's or Sarah's parents.

## Unresolved bridges and cautions

- Do not merge the Benjamin–Sophia/Docia family with the James–Sarah Stafford family without a documented generational connection.
- Do not infer a connection from either modern family to Major Selby, William Atkinson Selby, Thomas Selby Sr., or another colonial Selby merely from the surname.
- Do not infer that a Louisiana or North Carolina Stafford descends from the medieval Stafford nobility without the complete intervening chain.
- The Maryland-to-Caswell-to-Mississippi-to-Louisiana Stafford migration remains a research hypothesis until each parent-child and migration step is supported independently.
- The Selby, Stafford, Townsend, White, Atkinson, Schoolfield, Parker, and related gateway theories must remain separate until their specific colonial-to-English links are proved.
