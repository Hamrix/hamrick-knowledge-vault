# Aetheling Evidence Project

## Objective

Audit, document, and present the Selby, Stafford, Plantagenet, Wessex, Haskins, and related lineage claims using explicit evidence standards.

## Current state

No lineage assertion is treated as proven merely because it appears in an online tree, GEDCOM, family narrative, or AI-generated report.

The initial vault audit found no underlying lineage files or identity-specific evidence. The first completed batch therefore records only a verified historical-language baseline. No family-tree connection has been added.

The second batch confirms one bounded medieval Stafford relationship to Edward III. It does not connect that Stafford branch to Selby, Haskins, or any modern person.

The 2026-07-31 public-tome update presents 52 sourced records with proof boundaries, source links, fact permalinks, a proof desk, source desk, and pixel-art learning interface. The latest records cover the John, Zadock, and Parker Selby estate network, Etheldred Peters's land caveat, a Frederick Ethelred Selby identity candidate, and William Atkinson Selby's wartime indictment and Townsend marriage. It remains a public presentation layer, not a new lineage conclusion.

The 2026-07-31 land-record follow-up confirms the original 1725 Thomas Outten devise to William Selby, the adjoining 1,250-acre Simpeton/Simpleton patent to Thomas Selby, and the original 1677, 1679, and 1725 deeds affecting Simpleton. The 1725 deed proves a Thomas Selby-to-William Selby title transfer of the unsold or unconveyed remainder, estimated at 520 acres more or less. This establishes a land chain and possession history, but not a parent-child relationship.

The 2026-08-08 [[Aetheling Evidence Release 0.9 Integration - 2026-08-08|release 0.9 integration]] imports the complete non-private audit snapshot: 71 atomic claims, 27 accessions, and 21 governed record requests. It adds direct William Atkinson Selby → Rhody and Major Selby → Polly evidence, the John Atkinson 1780 legatee group, Benjamin Selby's 1849 bar and 1860 probate-network anchors, and decisive same-name separations. William → Major and Major → Benjamin remain unproved.

The same-day [[Generation Proof Continuation - 2026-08-08|generation proof continuation]] now proves the bounded chain John Selby Esq. → son John → Major Selby and carries Major from minority through his own 1803 receipt as grandson of Ann Selby. It also proves from Parker Selby's 1746 will that Parker had an eldest son John. Identifying that son as the John Selby Esq. who died in 1790 is strong but not yet confirmed by an explicit later primary cross-reference. The Worcester-to-Bourbon identity remains a strong candidate across a gap of less than four years, not a proved migration.

Continued review adds Major's consecutive 1811-1813 Bourbon tax entries, a related 1811 Dudley-heirs deed bearing his marked witness attestation, the 1812 ejectment service, and an 1815 survey labeling Major Selby's house and improvement. This extends the reviewed Bourbon cluster through 1815 and narrows the unbridged interval to the 1820 Nicholas household to five years. Major's 1833 Nicholas County will names wife Peggy, son Henry, and daughter Polly Anterbus, independently confirms Major-to-Polly, and proves the Kentucky testator was not the Hamilton County, Indiana, Major alive in 1850 and 1860. A derivative Worcester index reports Major Selby and Hester Cottingham on 8 December 1808 in reciprocal entries; the exact original target is now Worcester County Court Marriage Licenses, MSA C2027, but the record and identity remain unresolved.

The 2026-08-09 [[Generation Proof Continuation - 2026-08-09|generation proof continuation]] adds Jesse Henderson's original 1821 heir division, Henry Selby's 1823 acquisition of heir Jesse's lot 2, and Margaret Selby's 1836 will. Margaret directly identifies Major as her deceased husband, Charles Parker as her brother, Mary Antrobus as her daughter, William Preston Antrobus as her grandson, and Kitty and Duland as her granddaughters and daughters of Henry Selby. These records strongly identify the later Nicholas County Henry as Major's son while preserving the final cross-record identity below confirmed status. They do not prove Margaret was Henry's mother or identify Nelly Henderson with Eleanor Selby.

The 2026-08-13 [[Generation Proof Continuation - 2026-08-13|generation proof continuation]] follows an older Worcester generation downward. A visually reviewed 1948 compiled genealogy reports Major among the children of Thomas and Elizabeth Selby and cites Thomas-family deeds involving *Choice* and *Dull Folly*. Because the list derives from later family testimony, it remains a targeted hypothesis. Authenticated probate images now distinguish two Charles Parker estates: Collins Parker administered the first in 1761, while a second Charles made a will in 1787 that directly named wife Ann and sons John, Charles, Selby, and Thomas. The will proves Selby Parker was Charles's son; Selby then administered that estate in 1788. Prerogative Court Liber 38 independently corroborates the earlier administration and inventory but shows that two additional indexed folios are unrelated surety appearances, not kinship evidence. The same records do not connect Selby Parker to a person named Major Selby or identify the Maryland testator as Margaret Selby's Kentucky brother Charles.

## Evidence standard

Use the Genealogical Proof Standard:
- reasonably exhaustive research;
- complete and accurate source citations;
- analysis and correlation;
- resolution of conflicts;
- soundly reasoned written conclusion.
