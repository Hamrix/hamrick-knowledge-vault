# Generation Proof Continuation - 2026-08-09

## Result

This continuation found two relationship-bearing record groups that materially strengthen the Nicholas County generation proof: Jesse Henderson's original 1821 land division and Margaret Selby's 1836 will with related estate settlements.

## Jesse Henderson division and Henry Selby's accumulation

The December 1821 court record divides fifty acres between the heirs of Jesse Henderson into eight equal 6.25-acre lots. It assigns Susan Henderson lot 1, Jesse Henderson lot 2, Henry Selby lot 3, Levin Henderson lot 4, Polly Henderson lot 5, William Henderson lot 6, Hetty Henderson lot 7, and a second interest written as "Henry Selby's" lot 8.

An original 1823 deed then records Jesse Henderson of Franklin County, Indiana, conveying his inherited lot 2 to Henry Selby of Nicholas County. Combined with Levin's 1828 lot-4 deed and the 1830 Susan lot-1 and William lot-6 deeds, Henry controlled at least six of the eight equal shares by 1830.

This sharply strengthens the connection between the 1809 Henry Selby-Nelly Henderson bond, where Jesse Henderson was co-obligor, and the later Nicholas County Henry. It does not state Jesse's relationship to Nelly, identify Nelly with Eleanor, or explain whether Henry's original lots 3 and 8 came through Nelly or another right. Polly's lot 5 and Hetty's lot 7 remain untraced.

## Margaret Selby's relationship statements

Margaret Selby's will, dated 24 November 1836 and proved in March 1838, directly identifies:

- Major Selby as her deceased husband;
- Charles Parker, executor of Major's estate, as her brother;
- Mary Antrobus as her daughter;
- William Preston Antrobus as her grandson; and
- Kitty and Duland Selby as her granddaughters and daughters of Henry Selby.

The probate order also records that Henry Selby opposed establishment of Margaret's will. An 1835 settlement for Major's estate paid for a dress for Mrs Margaret Selby and left about $203.30 with Charles Parker. Margaret's will refers to $203.33 in Charles's hands, and Robert Parker's 1839 settlement carries that amount into Margaret's estate administration.

## Accuracy corrections

- The survey is dated 14 December 1821, not 1825.
- The 1828 grantor is readable as **Levin Henderson**; earlier OCR alternated among Seven, Leven, and Levin.
- William Henderson received lot 6 in the original division. The automated 1830 deed transcript's lot-4 reading conflicts with the primary division and is treated as OCR error.
- "Mary Antrobus" and "Polly Anterbus" are strongly compatible but retained as separately written names.
- "Kitty" and "Duland" strongly correlate with later Catharine and Dulcina, but the different spellings remain visible in the evidence notes.
