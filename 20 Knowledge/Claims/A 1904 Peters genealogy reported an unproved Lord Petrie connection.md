# A 1904 Peters genealogy reported an unproved Lord Petrie connection

## Claim

Nellie Peters Black's 1904 compiled genealogy reports that an English Peters family claimed to be a branch of the noble house of "Lord Petrie" and reports the same arms and motto. This confirms the existence of the published tradition only. It does not prove a genealogical connection.

## Subjects

- The Liverpool Peters family described in the 1904 volume
- Ralph Peters
- William Peters
- The family described by the quoted Burke passage as connected with "Lord Petrie"

## Evidence supporting

- [[Black 1904 - Richard Peters ancestors and descendants]], printed page 64, visually reviewed: the passage states that the family "claims to be a branch" and reports matching arms and motto.
- The same book, printed page 60, describes the Peters arms and motto displayed at Belmont.

## Evidence against

- The book provides no generation-by-generation pedigree connecting its Liverpool Peters family to the noble house.
- No heraldic grant, visitation, probate sequence, or contemporary relationship record proving the connection has been reviewed.
- The 1899 search report on printed page 63 admits that reliable information about William Peters was scarce before introducing the Burke excerpt.

## Conflicts

The source spells the title "Lord Petrie," while the submitted research interprets it as Baron Petre. That normalization has not been independently verified. The book's assertion that Burke showed a direct connection is not accompanied by a relationship chain connecting the American family to the family in the Burke passage.

## Analysis

The wording is explicitly a claim of affiliation. A similar or even identical coat of arms and motto, as reported by a later compiler, is not kinship evidence. The proper evidentiary use of this passage is as a documented family tradition and a lead for heraldic and probate research.

This note does not connect the Liverpool Peters family to Etheldred Peters of Craven County, Elizabeth Peters, any Stafford family, or the user's proven ancestry.

## Confidence rationale

**Confirmed only as a publication event:** the 1904 page was visually reviewed and accurately records the claim. **Unproven as descent:** no relationship-level evidence establishes the alleged noble-house connection.

## Next research action

Identify the exact Burke edition and underlying lineage cited on printed pages 63-64, then test each generation against original wills, parish registers, deeds, and heraldic records. Preserve "Petrie" as the source wording until the title and family identity are independently resolved.
