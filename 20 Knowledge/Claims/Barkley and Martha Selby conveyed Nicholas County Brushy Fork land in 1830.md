# Barkley and Martha Selby conveyed Nicholas County Brushy Fork land in 1830

## Claim

On 23 September 1830 Bartley or Barkley Selby and wife Martha conveyed Nicholas County Brushy Fork land to William Royse and to Daniel and Hugh J. McClintock. The Royse parcel contained about one acre; the McClintock tract contained 92 acres, 3 roods, and 27 poles.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Barkley Selby Nicholas deeds 1824 and 1830]] contains the original recorded deeds, Martha's dower relinquishment, and the related William and Catharine Royse conveyance to Barkley.
- [[FamilySearch - Barkley and Patsey Selby Nicholas Haws survey deeds 1816-1820]] documents the earlier Brushy Fork title sequence and a wife named Patsey.

## Analysis and limitations

The 1830 deeds directly prove the transactions and the Barkley-Martha marriage statement. They also continue Barkley's documented Nicholas County land chronology beyond 1820.

Patsey is a common historical nickname for Martha, so the wife named Patsey in 1817 and Martha in 1830 is a strong one-woman interpretation. No reviewed instrument explicitly equates the names. The 1824 deed's wife-name **Milly** is a genuine unresolved conflict. None of the instruments states that Barkley was related to Major Selby.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the date, parties, spouse relationship, parcels, and dower acknowledgment are directly recorded. This confidence does not extend to the Patsey-Martha identity correlation, the Milly discrepancy, or any relationship to Major Selby.
