# John Selby Esq was grandfather of Major Selby through his son John

## Claim

John Selby Esq. of Worcester County was the grandfather of a Major Selby whose father was John Selby.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - John Selby 1790 Worcester County will images]] directly calls Major a grandson and later identifies him as **"Major Selby son of John"** among four named grandsons.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby Esq 1795 Worcester final account]] records payment to the guardian of **"Major Selby Orphan of John Selby"** for a legacy **"left him by his Grandfather's will."** Because this is the final account of John Selby Esq.'s estate, the grandfather is the decedent.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1785 Worcester guardianship estate-view image]] independently calls the same-age Worcester minor Major the orphan of deceased John Selby.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1797-1800 Worcester guardian accounts]] repeats the father-child wording in four consecutive court accounts and carries the bounded ward identity through February 1800.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1803 Ann Selby estate distribution]] reciprocally calls Major a grandson of Ann Selby, the testator's widow, and records his receipt of his share from her estate in September 1803.

## Analysis and limits

The will and final account provide two independent forms of direct evidence inside the grandfather's estate, and the guardianship record independently repeats the father-child relationship. Ann's later estate account confirms the grandchild relationship from the other grandparent's estate and carries Major into an adult receipt in 1803. Together they prove the bounded colonial chain:

**John Selby Esq. -> his son John Selby -> minor Major Selby.**

This conclusion does not identify the minor with the adult Major Selby assessed in Worcester County in 1783 or with the later Kentucky man. It also does not connect this John Selby household to the separate Atkinson-line John and William Atkinson Selby without an additional identity-bearing record.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the 1790 will expressly calls Major the testator's grandson and identifies him as the son of John; the 1795 final account repeats both relationships while distributing Major's legacy.
