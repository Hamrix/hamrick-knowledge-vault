# Sophia E., Docia/Docie, and Dora Stone Selby are treated as one correlated identity

## Claim

The Aetheling Evidence Project treats the woman recorded as **Sophia E. Stone**, **Sophia Stone**, **Sophia Selby**, **Docia/Docie Stone or Selby**, and **Dora Stone** as one correlated historical identity. The preferred project name is **Sophia Docia Elizabeth Stone Selby**.

## Evidence supporting

- The reviewed 4–5 January 1854 Shelby County, Kentucky, license papers record Sophia Bryan's consent for “Col Ben Selby” to marry “my daughter Sophia Stone” and issue a license to Benjamin Selby and Sophia E. Stone. See [[FamilySearch - Benjamin Selby and Sophia E Stone 1854 Shelby County marriage-license images]].
- The reviewed 1860 Oldham County census enumerates Benjn Selby and Sophia Selby together in one household. See [[FamilySearch - Benjn Selby 1860 US census image]].
- An indexed 1874 Kentucky birth entry for Roberts Selby reports parents Benjamin Selby and Docie Stone. The underlying image has not yet been reviewed.
- The reviewed 1880 Henry County census records Ben Selby, wife Docia Selby, son Payne Selby, and son Robert Selby in one household. See [[FamilySearch - Ben Selby 1880 US census image]].
- Frederick Payne Selby's reviewed 1884 Orleans Parish marriage license names Benjamin Selby and Docia Stone as his parents. See [[FamilySearch - Frederick Payne Selby 1884 Orleans marriage license image]].
- Robert C. Selby's reviewed 1938 Kentucky death certificate reports Benjamin Selby and Dora Stone as his parents. See [[FamilySearch - Robert C Selby 1938 Kentucky death certificate image]].

## Correlation analysis

The repeated association with Benjamin or Ben Selby, the Kentucky setting, the chronological sequence, and the recurring children Payne/Frederick Payne and Robert support treating Sophia, Docia/Docie, and Dora as variants belonging to one woman rather than unrelated surname matches.

This is a project-level correlation across records. Each source note must continue to quote the name exactly as it appears in that source.

## Conflicts and limitations

- No single reviewed record displays all of the name forms together.
- The reviewed 1854 papers abbreviate the middle name as **E.**; they do not spell out **Elizabeth**. The expansion to Elizabeth is retained as the user's established project identification but still needs an explicit source citation.
- The 1854 papers reviewed so far contain a consent and license but no minister's return proving that the ceremony was performed.
- The 1860 census does not state household relationships.
- The 1874 birth entry is currently represented by an index rather than a reviewed image.
- The 1938 death-certificate parentage was supplied decades after Robert's birth by an informant.

## Confidence rationale

`strong`: the multi-record identity pattern is coherent and specific, but an explicit alias record, minister's return, probate statement, or record spelling out Elizabeth would strengthen it to confirmed.

## Next research action

Locate the minister's return or marriage register for the 1854 license, review the original 1874 birth entry, and search probate, obituary, church, and civil records for a document that places Sophia and Docia/Dora name forms in the same identity context or spells out the middle name Elizabeth.
