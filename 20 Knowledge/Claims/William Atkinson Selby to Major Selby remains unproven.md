# William Atkinson Selby to Major Selby remains unproven

## Claim status

No inspected relationship-bearing record names Major Selby as a son of William Atkinson Selby.

## Evidence considered

- [[FamilySearch - Angelo Atkinson 1766 Worcester County will images]] proves William's earlier relationship to John Selby and Angelo Atkinson but does not name Major.
- [[FamilySearch - Rhody Selby 1811 Bourbon orphan order]] proves Rhody as William's child but does not name Major.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1785 Worcester guardianship estate-view image]] proves that one minor Major Selby was a child of a deceased John Selby, not William. It does not identify that minor with either the Kentucky man dead by 1833 or the Indiana man alive after 1833.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby 1790 Worcester County will images]] calls that colonial Major the testator's grandson and expressly identifies him as "Major Selby son of John."
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby Esq 1795 Worcester final account]] repeats that Major was the orphan of John and received a legacy under his grandfather's will.
- [[FamilySearch - William Antrobus and Polly Selby 1825 Nicholas marriage file]] proves Major as Polly's father and does not name William.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1811 Bourbon deed and index]] proves an adult Major in Bourbon County but no kinship.

## Analysis

The inherited link remains a hypothesis for later Major Selby identities and is contradicted for the 1785 minor. Three relationship-bearing originals identify that child as John's son, and two place him as John Selby Esq.'s grandson. If the child is eventually identified with either later man, a William-parent assignment is untenable. If the Indiana target instead descends from the adult Major taxed in 1783, that man is necessarily distinct from the 1785 child and still has no proven parents. The William-to-Major link must not be published as fact or used to attach downstream pedigrees.

## Confidence rationale

`strong`: multiple relevant originals have been reviewed without a William-to-Major relationship statement, one previously used attachment proves a different relationship, and the leading 1785 child record names John as that Major's father. This is a research-status conclusion, not proof that William could not have been the father of some other person named Major Selby.
