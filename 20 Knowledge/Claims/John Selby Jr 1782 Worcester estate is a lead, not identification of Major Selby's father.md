# The 1782 John Selby Jr estate is strongly identified as Major Selby's father's estate

## Claim

Correlation of the original 1782-1783 estate records with the 1785 guardianship, the elder John Selby's 1790 will, and his 1795 final account strongly identifies the John Selby Jr. estate opened in 1782 as the estate of the minor Major Selby's father.

## Evidence supporting

- [[Maryland State Archives - John Selby Jr 1782 Worcester estate index]] and the manually reviewed original docket record John Selby Jr., Leah Selby as administrator, a bond filed 20 October 1782, an inventory filed 18 October 1783, accounts dated 31 October 1783 and 13 March 1793, and bond sureties Jacob Selby and Nehemiah Dorman.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby of Captain John 1782 Worcester inventory]] calls the decedent "John Selby (of John, Capt.)," dates the appraisal to 8 November 1782, identifies James and Parker Selby as nearest kin, and records Leah as administratrix in 1783.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby Jr 1793 Worcester estate account images]] identifies the 13 March 1793 entry as the estate's third account and names its accountants as Kendal Smock and Leah his wife. It names no child, heir, guardian, or distributee.
- [[Maryland State Archives - Worcester County sheriffs John Selby Jr]] places a John Selby Jr. in the office of Worcester County sheriff from 1779 through 1782. The matching name, county, and terminal year are useful identity context, but the list does not state that the sheriff was the 1782 decedent.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1785 Worcester guardianship estate-view image]] directly identifies a minor Major Selby as the orphan of a deceased John Selby on 1 July 1785, but gives neither that John's suffix nor an estate-file reference.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby 1790 Worcester County will images]] calls Major the testator's grandson and expressly identifies him as "Major Selby son of John."
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby Esq 1795 Worcester final account]] calls Major the orphan of John Selby and records a legacy left to Major by his grandfather's will.

## Analysis and limit

The probate sequence now contains a coherent identity chain. The father died in the narrow interval required by the 8 November 1782 appraisal and the July 1785 guardianship. The decedent was identified as John connected to Captain John; the elder John later called Major his grandson and Major's father John; and the elder estate's final account again called Major the orphan of John while paying the grandfather's legacy. The official sheriff list ending John Selby Jr.'s term in 1782 is additional context.

No single surviving record says "the John whose estate opened in 1782 was Major's father." For that reason this estate-file correlation is rated `strong`, not `confirmed`. The narrower generational statement that Major was the son of John and grandson of John Selby Esq. is directly proved and recorded separately in [[John Selby Esq was grandfather of Major Selby through his son John]].

Leah's exact relationship remains unresolved. The records establish her administrative role and marriage to Kendal Smock but do not expressly call her John's widow or Major's mother.

## Next research action

Retrieve the still-missing 1782 bond and first and second accounts. Search Kendal and Leah Smock's deeds and court records for a dower, remarriage, or maternal statement. Then trace Major's guardian accounts, majority release, and land title to bridge the proved colonial minor to or away from the later Kentucky man.
