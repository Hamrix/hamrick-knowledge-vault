# The later Nicholas County Henry Selby is strongly identified as Major Selby's son

## Claim

The Nicholas County Henry Selby documented from the Henderson land division through the 1850 census is strongly identified as the son Henry named in Major Selby's 1833 will.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1833 Nicholas County will and 1835 inventory]] directly names Major's son Henry.
- [[FamilySearch - Margaret Selby 1836 will and estate settlements]] names Kitty and Duland as Margaret's granddaughters and daughters of Henry Selby; Henry later opposed probate of Margaret's will.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby daughter marriage records 1832-1849]] independently names Catharine and Dulcina as daughters of a Nicholas County Henry.
- [[FamilySearch - Jesse Henderson 1821 division and 1823 deed to Henry Selby]], [[FamilySearch - Henderson heir 1828 deed to Henry Selby]], and [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby 1830 Nicholas Henderson estate deeds]] establish Henry's local land chronology before Major's will.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1838 Nicholas deed to Parker Selby]] and [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1850 Nicholas census]] continue the same uncommon-name, county, family, and land cluster after both wills.

## Analysis and limitations

The convergence is unusually specific: one Henry in the same county and family period appears before Major's will, is father of daughters whose names closely match Margaret's granddaughters, personally contests Margaret's probate, and continues in the same local chronology afterward. Major's will supplies the direct father-son statement for a son Henry; Margaret's will supplies the cross-generational family bridge.

No reviewed post-1833 instrument literally calls the later landholder "Henry Selby, son of Major Selby." Margaret also does not call Henry her son, and the girls' mother is unnamed. The conclusion is therefore `strong`, not `confirmed`. If the 1850 age of 66 is approximately correct, this Henry was born about 1784, which rules out the Worcester child aged about seven in 1785 as his father and deepens the conflict with identifying that child as the Nicholas testator.

## Confidence rationale

`strong`: multiple independent original records converge on one local Henry and bridge him into Major and Margaret's immediate family, but the final identity depends on correlation rather than a single explicit later cross-reference.
