# Sarah E. Stafford and Evelyn Stafford were Sarah Eveline Stafford

## Claim

Sarah E. Stafford or Selby, identified in Frederick Ethelred Selby's records, and Eveline or Evelyn Stafford, wife of James Benjamin Sanford Selby, were the same historical person: Sarah Eveline Stafford, born 18 March 1890 in Orleans Parish, Louisiana.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Sarah Eveline Stafford 1890 Orleans birth index]] gives the full name Sarah Eveline Stafford, an 18 March 1890 Orleans birth, and parents Ethelred H. Stafford and Fannie B. Coe.
- [[FamilySearch - Eveline S Stafford 1900 US census image]] enumerates Eveline S. Stafford, born March 1890 in Louisiana, as Fannie's daughter in the Stafford household.
- [[FamilySearch - Eveline Stafford 1910 US census image]] enumerates single Eveline Stafford, age 20, as daughter of Ethelred H. and Fanny Stafford in New Orleans shortly before the marriage.
- [[FamilySearch - James Benjamin Sanford Selby 1910 Orleans marriage license image]] records James Benjamin Sanford Selby's July 1910 marriage to Evelyn Stafford and associates the bride with Ethelred H. Stafford and Fanny B. Coe.
- [[FamilySearch - Frederick E Selby 1930 US census image]] enumerates Frederick E. Selby as the son of Sarah E. Stafford in New Orleans.
- [[FamilySearch - Frederick E Selby 1953 Lenoir County marriage license image]] names Sarah E. Selby as Frederick's mother.
- [[FamilySearch - Frederick Ethelred Selby NUMIDENT record]] names the exact full-name Frederick Ethelred Selby and reports his parents as James B. Selby and Sarah E. Stafford.

## Evidence against

No reviewed record conflicts with the combined identity. The underlying 1890 birth certificate remains unreviewed because FamilySearch restricts the image to a center or partner site.

## Analysis

The unusual full-name birth entry joins the Sarah and Eveline forms in one person. The exact birth month and year, the repeated parent pair, the continuous New Orleans locality, the pre-marriage census, the July 1910 marriage, and the later records of the couple's son form a coherent chronology. The change from Eveline or Evelyn to Sarah E. is therefore treated as a documented name-form variation, not as two women merged only because they shared a surname.

This identity finding establishes a historical family unit. It does not prove the user's private modern descent, connect this Stafford family to any medieval Stafford line, or validate an online tree beyond the cited adjacent relationships.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: one official birth index supplies the complete name and exact parent pair, and multiple original census and marriage images independently correlate the same age, place, parents, spouse, and child.
