# Barkley Selby and wife Patsey held Nicholas County Haws survey land from 1816 through 1820

## Claim

Barkley Selby acquired 116 acres in Samuel Haws's survey on Brushy Fork in Nicholas County in April 1816. Barkley and wife Patsey conveyed 18.5 acres from that tract in June 1817, and an October 1820 title-confirmation deed described the remaining 97.5 acres as in Barkley's possession.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Barkley and Patsey Selby Nicholas Haws survey deeds 1816-1820]] contains the original recorded acquisition, partial conveyance, spousal acknowledgment, and title-confirmation sequence.
- [[FamilySearch - Burns 1936 Nicholas deed abstract 1796-1818]] is a derivative locator for the 1817 conveyance and is superseded by the original for claim proof.
- [[FamilySearch - Thomas Rule 1817 Nicholas County Haws survey deeds]] places Thomas Rule of Bourbon County in the same broad Haws-survey and Brushy Fork land system in 1817.
- [[FamilySearch - Barkley Selby Nicholas deeds 1824 and 1830]] directly continues the 1820 title through a 53-acre Dyer sale in 1824 and later Brushy Fork transactions.

## Analysis and limitations

The deeds prove Barkley's landholding continuity and his marriage to Patsey for the bounded 1816-1820 interval. They also establish that a Selby landholder other than the still-unidentified 1820 Major was present in Nicholas County's Haws-survey network during the Bourbon-to-Nicholas gap.

No reviewed instrument calls Barkley a brother, son, father, or other relative of Major Selby. The evidence is therefore collateral cluster evidence and cannot be used to assign Barkley to Major's family.

Later originals call Barkley's wife Milly in 1824 and Martha in 1830. Patsey is a common historical nickname for Martha, but the Patsey-Martha identity is not explicitly stated, and the Milly discrepancy remains unresolved.
