# Thomas Selby of Somerset County was father of a Thomas Selby named in his 1701 will

## Claim

Thomas Selby of Somerset County, Maryland, who made the will dated 28 October 1701, was the father of a son named Thomas Selby.

## Subjects

- Thomas Selby of Somerset County, Maryland, testator of 1701
- Thomas Selby, his son and sole executor
- [[10 Projects/Aetheling Evidence Project/Project Index|Aetheling Evidence Project]]

## Evidence supporting

- [[Maryland State Archives - Thomas Selby 1701 will images]] is an official digitized manuscript will-book image. The testator twice uses the filial wording: "my son Thomas Selby" in a bequest and "my son Tho Selby" when appointing his sole executor.

## Evidence against

No evidence against the stated father-son relationship was located in this review.

## Conflicts

The son named in this 1701 will is not identified in the document as the Thomas Selby who made the 1757 Worcester County will proved in 1758. The shared name alone is insufficient to merge them.

## Analysis

The will itself supplies explicit parent-child language, so this is a confirmed atomic relationship. It is deliberately limited to the testator and the son named in the same document.

This record does not establish that the 1701 testator was [[Thomas Selby Sr c1635-1702|Thomas Selby Sr. (c. 1635-1702)]], although that profile is a research lead. It also does not establish any relationship between this son and [[Thomas Selby of Worcester County was the father of John Selby|the later Thomas Selby of Worcester County]], Major Selby, Etheldred Peters, the Staffords, the Plantagenets, Wessex, or any modern family.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the reviewed official manuscript directly describes Thomas Selby as the testator's son twice. The identity of either man beyond the wording of this will remains limited by the record.

## Next research action

Seek estate, land, tax, or court records that distinguish the 1701 son from other men named Thomas Selby and explicitly connect him, if possible, to the separately documented 1758 Thomas Selby family.
