# Henry Selby and Nelly Henderson executed a Bourbon County marriage bond in 1809

## Claim

Henry Selby and Nelly Henderson were the intended parties in a Bourbon County, Kentucky, marriage bond dated 5 June 1809; Jesse Henderson was the second obligor.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby and Nelly Henderson 1809 Bourbon marriage bond]] is the visually reviewed original bond.
- [[FamilySearch - Jesse Henderson 1821 division and 1823 deed to Henry Selby]] assigns Henry two interests in Jesse Henderson's heir division and records his later acquisition of heir Jesse's lot 2.
- [[FamilySearch - Henderson heir 1828 deed to Henry Selby]] records Henry's acquisition of Levin Henderson's lot 4.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby 1830 Nicholas Henderson estate deeds]] later records a Henry Selby's purchases from two heirs of Jesse Henderson.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1838 Nicholas deed to Parker Selby]] later names a Nicholas County Henry Selby's wife as Eleanor.

## Analysis and limitations

The bond directly proves the intended couple and Jesse Henderson's participation, not Jesse's relationship to Nelly or completion of the marriage. The 1821 division places Henry inside the heir-allotment structure with two interests, and the 1823, 1828, and 1830 deeds show him acquiring four more Henderson shares. This makes the Henderson family-network correlation substantially stronger than the bond alone.

Nelly is compatible with Eleanor, but no reviewed record explicitly states that Nelly and Eleanor were the same woman. The 1809-1838 appearances therefore form a strong identity and family-network hypothesis rather than one proved continuous identity. The records also do not state whether Henry's original 1821 interests came through Nelly, an earlier transfer, or another right.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the bond directly states the intended marriage and parties. The confidence does not extend to the later identity correlation or to any Henderson parent-child link.
