# Gundreda de Ver was reported as mother of Walter de Ver

## Claim

Gundreda de Ver was reported as the mother of Walter de Ver.

## Subjects

- Walter de Ver
- Gundreda de Ver

## Evidence supporting

- [[Wilkinson 1999 - Thirteenth-Century Women in Lincolnshire]], printed page 105 and pedigree at page 247, gives this parentage and cites *Registrum Antiquissimum* II, pages 211-212, no. 509n, and *Early Yorkshire Charters* III, page 61.
- [[Loyd and Stenton 1950 - Sir Christopher Hattons Book of Seals entry 73]] independently proves that Adam of Goxhill was Walter's father, but it does not name Walter's mother.
- [[Farrer 1916 - Early Yorkshire Charters Volume III Goxhill pedigree]], printed page 61, reconstructs Gundreda, daughter and heir of Guy de Vere, as Adam's wife and Walter's mother. This is editorial synthesis rather than a quoted maternal clause.
- [[Hunter 1844 - Pipe Roll 1 Richard I page 61]] directly proves that Adam was Guy de Ver's son-in-law, but does not name Guy's daughter.
- [[Fowler 1893 - Coucher Book of Selby Volume II page 296]] directly proves that Gundreda de Ver was Nicholas de Chavencurt's wife, but does not mention Adam or Walter.

## Evidence against

No directly reviewed record disproves the relationship, but the reviewed underlying records establish only adjacent facts. Neither names Gundreda as Adam's wife or Walter's mother.

## Conflicts

[[The Genealogist Volume 20 - 1904]] presents competing placements for Walter: either a son of the second Guy de Ver or a grandson through Guy's daughter and Adam de Gousel. The charter transcription now resolves Adam's paternity but does not name the daughter or prove that she was Gundreda.

## Analysis

Farrer and Wilkinson provide a specific, source-cited maternal reconstruction. Direct review now proves that Walter's father was Adam, Adam was Guy's son-in-law, and Gundreda was later Nicholas's wife. The missing identity step remains: the Pipe Roll leaves Guy's daughter unnamed, while the Selby charter does not call Gundreda Adam's widow or Walter's mother. These adjacent facts make the reconstruction plausible but do not convert it into a direct maternal statement.

This is an upstream medieval hypothesis only. It does not establish a connection to the Selby surname, the earls of Oxford, or the user's documented ancestry.

## Confidence rationale

**Tentative:** two scholarly reconstructions agree and the reviewed adjacent relationships fit them, but no reviewed record explicitly identifies Gundreda as Adam's wife or Walter's mother.
