# Etheldred Peters' parentage remains unproven

## Key correction from the original tax-list image

An abstract of the 1786 Swift Creek tax list incorrectly renders the entry as “Etheldred Peters, son of John Peters.” Direct visual inspection of the original tax-list fragment shows the relevant wording is: **“three poles, my self, Son John Peters, and one negro ….”**

The ordinary sense is that Etheldred was the taxpayer and **John Peters was Etheldred's son**, one of the taxable white males in Etheldred's household. The entry does **not** identify Etheldred's father.

It further records 400 acres where Etheldred lived, 120 acres on Little Contentnea/Contentney known as Warehouse Landing, and one enslaved person.

## Repository citation

North Carolina State Archives, *Treasurer's and Comptroller's Papers, County Settlements with the State, Settlements of Individuals, Craven County*, Box 9, 1786 Swift Creek tax list, entry for Etheldred Peters.

The list pertains to the Swift Creek area of **Craven County, North Carolina**; much of that area later became part of Pitt County. It survives in fragments rather than as a complete intact list.

## What is established and what is not

- **Established:** Etheldred Peters was present in the Swift Creek area in 1786, held the stated land, and had a son named John Peters who was then taxable.
- **Not established:** Etheldred's father, mother, birth date, birth place, English origin, or occupation as a tailor.
- **Not established:** A connection to any Pennsylvania Peters family.

## Claims evaluated and not adopted

The following statements are currently **unsupported** and must not be entered as facts in a pedigree or biography:

- birth in Lancaster, Lancashire, in 1730;
- immigration from England to Craven County as a young man;
- marriage to Elizabeth Chaney/Cheney about 1750;
- occupation as a tailor in England or North Carolina;
- a specific day or month of death in 1788; and
- the proposition that the unusual given name proves royal Anglo-Saxon descent, an educated social class, or a preserved family tradition.

Searches of publicly indexed Lancashire parish resources and general web indexes did not produce a contemporaneous record linking an Etheldred/Ethelred Peters to any of those claims. Absence from these indexes is not proof that no record survives, but a database assertion is not evidence in its place.

## Death and estate evidence

The original loose estate packet has now been reviewed. Its cover is labelled **“Peters, Etheldred 1788 Estate,”** and its inventory calls itself the “true and perfect Inventory of the Estate of Etheldred Peters Decd.” The packet was returned to the December 1788 Craven court. This directly supports Etheldred's death by that court return, but not an exact date of death.

The associated sale-account leaves name Elizabeth Peters and John Peters among purchasers. They do **not** describe either person as Etheldred's wife, widow, child, heir, administrator, guardian, or kin. Their names and the shared surname must therefore not be used to create a family-tree connection.

The reviewed packet contains an inventory and sale material, but no administration bond, heir list, guardianship, distribution, or land partition identifying Etheldred's parents or descendants. The court minutes remain the next appropriate source for any administration appointment or kinship language.

The underlying repository series is FamilySearch catalog **456868**, *Wills and estate papers (Craven County), 1663-1978*, filmed from originals at the North Carolina State Archives. The catalog explains that its loose estate files may contain administrators' and guardians' bonds, inventories, sale accounts, divisions, petitions, and receipts. This confirms that the 1788 Peters file is the correct evidentiary target, but it does **not** itself supply Etheldred's parents or identify a particular family relationship.

An additional locator is Stephen E. Bradley, *New Bern District, North Carolina Loose Estates Papers, 1775–1810* (1994), an abstract volume covering the regional loose-estate material. Search the Peters entries in that work, then verify every relevant abstract against the original 1788 estate images.

The companion court-minute source is FamilySearch catalog 646166, *County court minutes, 1712–1868*, filmed from originals at the North Carolina State Archives. For the 1788 administration entry, consult **County court minutes 1787–1801**, film 1689304, item 2, image group **DGS 7640400**. Search the 1788 terms for “Peters,” “Etheldred,” “estate,” “administrator,” “administration,” “widow,” and “guardian.” The preceding 1784–1787 minute group is DGS 8356811.

## DGS-specific full-text search path

FamilySearch's **Full-Text Search** accepts an Image Group Number (DGS), so DGS **7640400** can be searched separately with the keyword variants `Etheldred`, `Ethelred`, `Peters`, `Etheldred Peters`, `estate`, `administration`, and `guardian`, then narrowed to 1788-1789. This is a discovery aid: it uses AI-generated handwriting transcripts, and not every DGS has yet been processed. Any hit must be opened and read against its image; a zero-result search would not prove the name absent from the minutes.

## 1787 land-caveat: original minute reviewed

A reviewed original Craven County court-minute page records the sheriff's return “upon the Caveat of Etheldred Peters agt Anthony Vanpelt.” It calls Etheldred the plaintiff and Vanpelt the defendant in a land caveat. The return is dated **31 August 1787**, not merely an undated or secondary-source lead.

The jury found that Vanpelt's warrant and survey were outside Peters's land lines and that Peters had his complement of land according to the courses and distances of his patent. The original minute names the jurors, but their participation does not establish kinship with Etheldred.

This is important land-identity evidence. The patent, warrant, survey, plat, and any related caveat papers should be retrieved and correlated with the 1786 Swift Creek tax-list land descriptions: 400 acres where Etheldred lived and 120 acres on Little Contentnea/Contentney known as Warehouse Landing.

This lead does **not** identify Etheldred's father.

## William Peters hypothesis: limited supporting context

In the 1779 Craven County assessment, **Joseph Peters** and **Etheldred Peters** were both named in Captain Allen's company. William Peters's 1760 Craven will explicitly names a son Joseph and refers generally to his surviving children. The shared tax company means the two men belonged to the same civil-military tax district; it does **not** establish residence next door or a relationship.

This makes the proposition that Etheldred was another child of William Peters a **testable hypothesis only**. The decisive test is whether Etheldred's 1788 estate papers, or Joseph's later records, describe a familial relationship or an inherited interest in William's estate.

## Research sequence

1. Reconstruct Etheldred's North Carolina life through Craven County deeds, land warrants/patents, court minutes, tax lists, estate records, and any marriage/bible records.
2. Identify an American document that gives an English birthplace, former residence, immigration event, or parent/guardian.
3. Only then test English candidates in original parish registers, probate, and apprenticeship records.

## England research caution

There is no parish called “Cochrane” near Lancaster. The intended parish may be **Cockerham**, a Lancashire parish near Lancaster. Also, the publicly transcribed St Mary the Priory, Lancaster register coverage begins in 1749, so it cannot presently supply a 1730 baptism from its online transcript. Do not attach a Lancashire baptism to Etheldred without an independent record tying the North Carolina man to that parish.

If a Lancashire origin is independently demonstrated, the next sources are:

1. National Archives series IR 1, *Registers of Duties Paid for Apprentices' Indentures* (1710-1811), searched for Etheldred/Ethelred/Aldred Peters and variant surname spellings. Before 1752 these often give a parent or guardian; later ones usually do not.
2. Probate in the Archdeaconry of Richmond's western deaneries, with the jurisdiction checked against the exact parish.
3. Original parish-register images and bishops' transcripts for the exact parish and period.

## Additional, unverified family lead

A 1999 published query reports that a family Bible named **Mary Peters** (born 3 May 1775 in North Carolina, died September 1852) as a daughter of **Etheldred and Elizabeth Peters**. This is useful for targeting an original Bible, later estate records, and the marriage of Mary Peters to James Moore; it is not evidence until the Bible record itself, its provenance, and an independent identity link are examined.

The North Carolina Archives’ statewide will index is a discovery tool, while the original Craven County probate or estate file remains the evidentiary target. It confirms a **William Peters** will probated in Craven in 1760, record ID **5200.28.1618**, recorded copy reference **Craven Deed Book 8:227**. A published abstract reports that the will names daughter Elizabeth and son Joseph, then directs division among all surviving children; it does not name Etheldred. This makes William only a possible research candidate, not an established father.

The North Carolina Land Grants index reports one Craven grant for Etheldred Peters, within six grants dated 1772–1788. The original grant, entry, warrant, survey, and patent need to be retrieved and correlated with the 1786 Warehouse Landing land before drawing any family conclusion.
