# Major Selby occupied a Bourbon County house and improvement from 1812 through 1815

## Claim

Major Selby was among Bourbon County land occupants served in an ejectment action on 3 November 1812, and an August 1815 survey in the same dispute labeled Major Selby's house and improvement.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1812 Bourbon ejectment and 1815 survey]] preserves the service record and the survey/plat.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1811 Bourbon deed and index]] places Major in the related Dudley-estate land network in 1811.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1812 and 1813 Bourbon tax lists]] independently records him in the county during the early part of the same interval.
- [[Thomas Rule linked the 1815 Bourbon survey network to Nicholas County land in 1817]] shows that Rule, who furnished Major's deed for the 1815 survey, acquired Nicholas County Haws-survey land in 1817, including a tract bounded by fellow 1812 occupant Valentine Cress.

## Analysis and limitations

The records extend the adult Bourbon chronology from 1807 through a named house and improvement in 1815 and narrow the unbridged interval to the 1820 Nicholas County household from nine years after the 1811 purchase to five years after the survey. The 1817 Rule deeds add a concrete Haws-survey, Hinkston, Brushy Fork, and Cress associate bridge into Nicholas County records. They do not state that Major moved to Nicholas County or identify the 1820 household head. The nearby Henry Selby is not assigned as Major's son without a relationship statement.
