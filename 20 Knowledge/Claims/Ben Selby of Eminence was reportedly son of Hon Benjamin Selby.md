# Ben Selby of Eminence was reportedly son of Hon Benjamin Selby

## Claim

The Benjamin Selby who became a Kentucky lawyer, state librarian, and resident of Eminence was strongly reported to be the son of an older public official named Hon. Benjamin Selby.

## Evidence supporting

- [[Armstrong 1878 - Ben Selby biographical sketch]] explicitly calls the younger lawyer "son of the Hon. Benjamin Selby" and supplies a detailed life chronology.
- [[Courier-Journal - Col Ben Selby 1883 interview]] independently identifies the Eminence interview subject and says his father had been Kentucky state auditor.
- [[FamilySearch - Benjamin Selby and Lucy S Hunter 1844 Indiana marriage]], [[FamilySearch - Ben Selby 1850 Trimble census image]], and [[FamilySearch - Benjamin Selby Trimble court orders 1847-1849]] correlate the biography's first marriage, legal career, age, and Kentucky chronology.
- [[Kentucky Senate journals - Benjamin Selby auditor appointment and death succession 1834-1846]] proves that Benjamin Selby was appointed Auditor of Public Accounts in January 1834, signed the 1834 auditor report, and was the deceased predecessor replaced in January 1846.
- [[Boons Lick Times - Benjamin Selby death notice reprint 1845]] reports that the Frankfort *Commonwealth* announced the death of Benjamin Selby, Auditor of State, on 25 May 1845.
- [[Kentucky Auditor - historical auditor list]] conflicts with these original records by assigning the office to a different chronology and a 1796-1876 Benjamin Shelby.
- [[FamilySearch - Franklin County Selby probate negative search 1824-1854]] found no Franklin will under the Selby spelling and no relationship-bearing settlement in the bounded search around 1845.
- [[FamilySearch - Franklin County Benjamin Selby tax transition 1845-1847]] separately lists Benj. Jr and Benj. Senr in 1845, finds no Selby on the manually reviewed 1846 surname pages, and lists one adult Benjamin in 1847. This supports two-person identity and the reported elder death window, not the relationship.
- [[Kentucky General Assembly - Selby negative search 1845-1846]] found no Selby memorial or family-facing entry in either chamber's first session after the reported death.
- [[Kentucky House Journal - Ben Selby elected state librarian 1851]] proves the officeholder's name and election but does not resolve whether he was the younger lawyer or the 1796-1876 man described by the modern Auditor biography.

## Analysis and limits

The 1878 father statement remains an explicit near-contemporary report, and independent original records identify the younger subject described in that entry. The 1883 auditor description and the 1878 office chronology are now independently corroborated by original Senate records and a contemporaneous death-notice reprint. The modern Kentucky Auditor chronology is the conflicting derivative account, not a reason to reject the period office description.

The Franklin tax transition is useful independent evidence that an older and younger Benjamin were separately recognized in 1845 and that the older entry disappears before the 1847 unsuffixed adult entry. It does not call them kin. The expanded probate, bond, guardianship, order-book, and legislative searches found no parent-child or heir statement.

The 1851 librarian record also cannot be used as a unique identity bridge. Original records name Ben or Benjamin Selby and report Washington County, while two later biographies assign the office to different men. No reviewed original office record supplies age or family information.

No reviewed original birth, probate, guardianship, Bible, or parent-naming marriage record directly states the relationship. The new evidence verifies the reported father's office and death but does not state that the younger Benjamin was his son.

This evidence is stronger than the inherited Major Selby parent theory, which no reviewed record supports. It does not identify Hon. Benjamin's parents.

## Confidence rationale

`moderate`: a near-contemporary biography explicitly names the father, original records identify its younger subject, and independent official and newspaper records now verify the reported father's auditor service and 1845 death. The 1845 tax list also confirms distinct senior and junior names in the right place and period. No original record yet calls them father and son, and the librarian identity remains unresolved.
