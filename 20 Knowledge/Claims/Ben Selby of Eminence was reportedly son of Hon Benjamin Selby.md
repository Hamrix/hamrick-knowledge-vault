# Ben Selby of Eminence was reportedly son of Hon Benjamin Selby

## Claim

The Benjamin Selby who became a Kentucky lawyer, state librarian, and resident of Eminence was strongly reported to be the son of an older public official named Hon. Benjamin Selby.

## Evidence supporting

- [[Armstrong 1878 - Ben Selby biographical sketch]] explicitly calls the younger lawyer "son of the Hon. Benjamin Selby" and supplies a detailed life chronology.
- [[Courier-Journal - Col Ben Selby 1883 interview]] independently identifies the Eminence interview subject and says his father had been Kentucky state auditor.
- [[FamilySearch - Benjamin Selby and Lucy S Hunter 1844 Indiana marriage]], [[FamilySearch - Ben Selby 1850 Trimble census image]], and [[FamilySearch - Benjamin Selby Trimble court orders 1847-1849]] correlate the biography's first marriage, legal career, age, and Kentucky chronology.
- [[Kentucky Auditor - historical auditor list]] identifies the 1820-1834 auditor as a different Benjamin Shelby in its linked biography and places Thomas S. Page in office throughout 1834-1846.
- [[FamilySearch - Franklin County Selby probate negative search 1824-1854]] found no Franklin will under the Selby spelling and no relationship-bearing settlement in the bounded search around 1845.

## Analysis and limits

The 1878 father statement remains an explicit near-contemporary report, and independent original records identify the younger subject described in that entry. The 1883 auditor description can no longer be treated as independent corroboration of the father's identity: Kentucky's official list places a different Benjamin Shelby in the office from 1820 through 1834 and Thomas S. Page there from 1834 through 1846. The two later narratives may repeat the same office or same-name error.

No reviewed original birth, probate, guardianship, Bible, or parent-naming marriage record directly states the relationship. The explicit father name is still the best current lead, but the office conflict and negative Franklin search require a lower confidence than previously assigned.

This evidence is stronger than the inherited Major Selby parent theory, which no reviewed record supports. It does not identify Hon. Benjamin's parents.

## Confidence rationale

`moderate`: a near-contemporary biography explicitly names the father and original records identify its younger subject, but no original parent-child record has been found and the supposed auditor corroboration conflicts with the official officeholder chronology and biography.
