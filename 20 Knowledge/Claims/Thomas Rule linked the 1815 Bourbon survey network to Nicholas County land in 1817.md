# Thomas Rule linked the 1815 Bourbon survey network to Nicholas County land in 1817

## Claim

The Thomas Rule who furnished a Major Selby deed for the 1815 Bourbon survey is strongly correlated with Thomas Rule of Bourbon County, who acquired two Nicholas County tracts in 1817 within the Haws-survey, Hinkston, and Brushy Fork land network. One tract was bounded by Valentine Cress, an occupant served alongside Major Selby in the 1812 ejectment action.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1812 Bourbon ejectment and 1815 survey]] names Thomas Rule as the person who furnished Major Selby's deed and names Valentine Cress among the served occupants.
- [[FamilySearch - Thomas Rule 1817 Nicholas County Haws survey deeds]] identifies Thomas Rule as of Bourbon County and records his two Nicholas County acquisitions, including the Haws-survey and Cress-boundary language.
- [[Barkley Selby and wife Patsey held Nicholas County Haws survey land from 1816 through 1820]] establishes a collateral Selby landholder in the same broad Haws-survey and Brushy Fork system during the interval.
- [[FamilySearch - Burns 1936 Nicholas deed abstract 1796-1818]] independently led to the Book E, page 133 conveyance but is derivative.

## Analysis and limitations

The name, Bourbon residence, short interval, shared Valentine Cress associate, and connected Haws-survey geography make the Thomas Rule correlation strong. The Barkley Selby sequence adds a separate Selby landholder in that survey system from 1816 through 1820. Together, the deeds substantially improve the geographic, surname-cluster, and associate bridge between the 1815 Bourbon record and Nicholas County land records.

They do not name Major Selby or state that Major moved to Nicholas County. They do not state Barkley's relationship to Major, identify the 1820 Major household, or identify the 1833 testator. The evidence therefore strengthens a research route without closing the Bourbon-to-Nicholas identity gap.

## Confidence rationale

`strong`: the underlying transactions and shared network are direct in original records, but the conclusion that both Thomas Rule references identify one man is a correlation, and no 1817 deed names Major Selby.
