# Major Selby received his share as grandson of Ann Selby in 1803

## Claim

By 30 September 1803, Major Selby had received his part of his grandmother Ann Selby's estate in Worcester County under a receipt and order.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1803 Ann Selby estate distribution]] expressly calls Major a grandson of Ann and credits payment of his part of her estate.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby 1790 Worcester County will images]] names the testator's wife Ann, son James, and grandson Major, including "Major Selby son of John."
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby Esq 1795 Worcester final account]] and [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1797-1800 Worcester guardian accounts]] identify Major as the orphan of John and carry the same ward through February 1800.

## Analysis and limits

The 1803 account is reciprocal evidence from Ann's estate. It repeats the family structure in John Selby Esq.'s will, identifies deceased administrator James as "of John," and records Major as Ann's grandson. Payment in Major's own name by receipt and order, with no guardian named, carries this proved family identity into an adult transaction.

This record does not state Major's later residence or prove that he was the Bourbon County plaintiff in 1807. It narrows that migration interval from seven years to less than four years, from 30 September 1803 to 5 August 1807.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the original recorded account directly identifies Major's relationship to Ann and his receipt of the estate share.
