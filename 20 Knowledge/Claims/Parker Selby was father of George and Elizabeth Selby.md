# Parker Selby was father of George and Elizabeth Selby

## Claim

A Worcester County guardian bond dated 11 June 1790 identifies George Selby and Elizabeth Selby as children of deceased Parker Selby.

## Evidence supporting

- [[Worcester Digital Archive - Parker Selby children 1790 guardian bond]] calls George and Elizabeth "orphans of Parker Selby deceased," records both as minors, and places them under Nancy Gunby's guardianship.
- [[Scarborough 1951 - George Selby 1802 Pleasant Grove deed transcription]] reports that an enrolled 1802 deed calls George Parker's eldest son and heir at law. This is corroboration and an original-deed retrieval lead, not a substitute for Liber V.
- [[FamilySearch Catalog - Worcester Land Records Liber V-W]] locates the original target at film 14543 / DGS 8141714 and MSA CE30-21 but documents that the film is center-or-affiliate restricted; the deed itself remains unreviewed.

## Analysis and limits

In this probate context, the repeated identification as Parker's orphans is direct parent-child evidence for Parker -> George and Parker -> Elizabeth. The bond does not identify their mother or state Nancy Gunby's kinship.

[[Maryland State Archives - Parker Selby Worcester estate index]] records several separate Parker estate tracks, including an April 1789 estate under Nancy Selby, a January 1790 estate under James Selby, and a June 1791 de bonis non estate formerly administered by John Selby, Esquire. The guardian bond does not identify its estate docket. [[The 1790 and 1791 Parker Selby records describe different estate histories]], so proximity cannot extend this claim to Captain John or another Parker line.

[[Nancy Gunby is strongly identified as mother of George and Elizabeth Selby]] through the guardianship and John Selby's 1790 will. That cross-record conclusion remains strong rather than confirmed, especially for Elizabeth-to-Betsey.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: a contemporary guardian bond directly describes both named children as orphans of Parker Selby, deceased. The confidence does not extend to any same-name identification or earlier generation.
