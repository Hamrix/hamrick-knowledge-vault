# Major Selby remained John Selby's ward through February 1800

## Claim

Worcester County guardian accounts identify John Selby as guardian to Major Selby, orphan of deceased John Selby, in a consecutive series through February 1800.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1797-1800 Worcester guardian accounts]] contains the fourth, fifth, sixth, and seventh accounts passed in February 1797, 1798, 1799, and 1800. Each names Major as the orphan of John Selby and John Selby as guardian.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby Esq 1795 Worcester final account]] identifies the same guardian more fully as John Selby, son of Parker, and records his receipt of Major's legacy.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1785 Worcester guardianship estate-view image]] begins the wardship and describes Major as about seven years old.

## Analysis and limits

The repeated relationship language, same guardian, annual balances, and ordinal account numbers establish continuity of the 1785 ward into 1800. The 1800 account is the latest guardian account found in the full-text search, but it is not labeled a final account and does not record Major's receipt or release.

This closes the internal Worcester chronology from 1785 through 1800. It does not by itself prove that the ward was the Major Selby who sued in Bourbon County in 1807, purchased Bourbon land in 1811, headed a Nicholas County household in 1820, or fathered Polly Selby in 1825.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: four consecutive primary court accounts expressly identify the same guardian, ward, and deceased father.
