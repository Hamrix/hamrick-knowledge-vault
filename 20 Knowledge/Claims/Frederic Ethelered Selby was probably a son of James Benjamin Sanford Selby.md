# Frederic Ethelred Selby was probably a son of James Benjamin Sanford Selby

## Claim

Frederic Ethelred Selby, born about 1911–1912 in Mississippi and later resident in New Orleans, was probably a son of James Benjamin Sanford Selby and Sarah Eveline Stafford.

## Evidence supporting

- Hamrick Ethelred Selby directly identifies Frederic Ethelred Selby as his paternal grandfather and Hamrick Selby as Frederic's son.
- The uploaded GEDCOM places Frederic in family `F230` as a child of James Benjamin Sanford Selby and Sarah Eveline Stafford.
- The GEDCOM cites the 1930 United States Federal Census for both Frederic and the household, at New Orleans, page 19A, enumeration district 0209, with Frederic's relationship recorded as `Son`.
- The same GEDCOM associates James with the household-head role on that census citation.
- FamilySearch Family Tree profile `LLWQ-6MV` identifies a specific `Frederick Ethelred Selby (1911-1992)` candidate and may provide a source-discovery path. The collaborative profile itself is not proof.

## Evidence against

- No contrary parentage record has been identified in the materials inspected.

## Conflicts

- Legacy files give conflicting birth years for Frederic, including 1911, 1919, and 1930.
- The GEDCOM includes the misspelling `Ethelered`, variant forms such as Frederick/Fred, and a secondary birth entry of 1912.
- Birthplace and death-place labels vary across derivative sources.
- The actual 1930 census image and the source records attached to the FamilySearch profile have not yet been inspected in the vault.

## Analysis

The cited 1930 census relationship field would be strong evidence if the image confirms the transcription and household identity. At present, the vault holds the derivative GEDCOM citation, direct family testimony for the modern family structure, and a collaborative FamilySearch profile identifying a matching candidate. The relationship is stronger than a bare online-tree assertion but is not yet documentary-confirmed.

`Frederic Ethelred Selby` is the controlled project spelling based on the user's direct clarification. Historical records must still be transcribed exactly as written.
