# Betsy was associated with Benjamin Selby in an Adair County deed index

## Claim

An Adair County deed-book index identifies an indenture involving `Benjamin & Betsy Selby` during the 1811-1815 period.

## Evidence supporting

- The published index to Adair County Deed Book C includes an entry for `Benjamin & Betsy Selby`.
- Other entries in the same book identify Benjamin Selby individually, supporting that the joint entry concerns the same local legal and landholding context.
- `Betsy` was commonly used as a form of Elizabeth, making the entry consistent with—but not proof of—the GEDCOM's proposed wife called Elizab?.

## Evidence against or missing

- The original deed image has not yet been inspected.
- The index does not state Betsy's maiden surname or her exact legal relationship to Benjamin.
- The entry does not name children or establish Robert Benjamin Selby's parentage.

## Analysis

The index is meaningful evidence that a woman called Betsy was legally associated with Benjamin Selby in an Adair County deed. The likely interpretation is that she was his wife, particularly if the deed involved dower release or joint conveyance, but that cannot be asserted until the deed text is inspected.

The entry must not be used to normalize her identity to Elizabeth McClane without the marriage bond, deed, or another original record.

## Confidence rationale

`moderate`: a specific contemporary record index supports the association, but the underlying deed and relationship language remain uninspected.
