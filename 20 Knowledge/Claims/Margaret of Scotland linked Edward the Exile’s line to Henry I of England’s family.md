# Margaret of Scotland linked Edward the Exile’s line to Henry I of England’s family

## Claim

Margaret, daughter of Edward the Exile, married Malcolm III of Scotland. Their daughter Matilda married Henry I of England in 1100, linking Edward the Exile’s pre-Conquest English royal line to Henry I’s family.

## Subjects

- Margaret, queen of Scots
- Edward the Exile
- Matilda of Scotland
- Henry I of England
- [[10 Projects/Aetheling Evidence Project/Project Index|Aetheling Evidence Project]]

## Evidence supporting

- [[People of Medieval Scotland - Margaret queen of Scots]] identifies Margaret’s father as Edward the Exile, records her marriage to Malcolm III, and identifies Matilda as their daughter and Henry I’s wife.
- [[McDonald 2004 - Matilda of Scotland review]] independently identifies Matilda as the daughter of Malcolm III and Margaret and dates her marriage to Henry I to 1100.

## Evidence against

No evidence against the named parent-child and marriage relationships was found.

## Conflicts

Matilda is also known as Edith. This is an alternative personal name, not evidence of a different person. The claim does not depend on unresolved questions about more distant ancestry or later descendants.

## Analysis

The two sources independently corroborate the key links: Edward the Exile → Margaret; Margaret and Malcolm III → Matilda; and Matilda → marriage with Henry I. This documents a specific historical bridge between the pre-Conquest English royal family and the post-Conquest English monarchy.

It does not establish that a later Plantagenet, Stafford, Selby, Haskins, or modern person descends through this bridge. Each later generation requires its own evidence.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: a specialist scholarly database with cited documentary sources and an independent academic review agree on the relationships and marriage.
