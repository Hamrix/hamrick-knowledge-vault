# One Major Selby household appears in the reviewed 1820 Nicholas census images

## Claim

Of the two preserved Nicholas County images formerly indexed for Major Selby, only page 111 contains a handwritten Major Selby household. It records one male age 26-44, one female age 10-15, and one female age 26-44.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1820 Nicholas census pages 111 and 129]] contains the reviewed original page 111 schedule and the nonmatching page 129 image.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1812 Bourbon ejectment and 1815 survey]] places a Major Selby house and improvement in neighboring Bourbon County five years earlier, but does not directly identify the Nicholas household head.
- [[Thomas Rule linked the 1815 Bourbon survey network to Nicholas County land in 1817]] places Major's 1815 deed supplier and fellow 1812 occupant Valentine Cress within a Nicholas County Haws-survey land network, but does not name Major.
- [[Barkley Selby and wife Patsey held Nicholas County Haws survey land from 1816 through 1820]] proves that another Selby held Haws-survey land in Nicholas County during the interval, but states no relationship to Major.
- [[William Selby and wife Priscilla conveyed Nicholas County Brushy Fork land in 1819]] proves a second collateral Selby couple in the land cluster, but states no relationship to Major or Barkley.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1833 Nicholas County will and 1835 inventory]] later names wife Peggy and daughter Polly, but does not explicitly cross-reference the 1820 household.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1850 Nicholas census]] reports a likely later Nicholas Henry born about 1784, creating a chronological conflict if the 1820 male is treated as Henry's father.

## Analysis and limits

The page review removes a false duplicate from the identity problem. The page 111 male's 26-44 bracket is chronologically compatible with the Major described as about seven on 1 July 1785, who would have been about 41 or 42 in 1820. That compatibility cannot be extended to the 1833 testator without resolving the likely son Henry's estimated 1784 birth, which would make the 1820 male implausibly young to be Henry's father. The two female age entries create a household profile that can be tested against the 1811 and 1817 marriage-license leads and the 1825 Polly Selby marriage file.

Compatibility is not identity. The census does not state relationships, exact ages, birthplaces, or prior residence. The 1815 Bourbon survey narrows the chronology to five years before the census, the 1817 Rule deeds establish a concrete associate-network route into Nicholas County, Barkley's 1816-1820 Haws-survey sequence establishes one collateral Selby in the same land system, and William and Priscilla add another in 1819. The later Nicholas County will's wife-and-daughter group is locally compatible, and the Polly relationship is independently proven in 1825, but the Henry chronology warns against merging the household with the testator. No record explicitly cross-references the 1820 household to either side or relates these Selby households. Identity with the 1785 minor, the 1807-1815 Bourbon man, and the 1833 testator remains unproved. The 1833 death conclusively separates the Nicholas testator from the later Hamilton County, Indiana, man.
