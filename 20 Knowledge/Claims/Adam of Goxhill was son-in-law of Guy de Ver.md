# Adam of Goxhill was son-in-law of Guy de Ver

## Claim

Adam of Goxhill was a son-in-law of Guy de Ver by 1189.

## Subjects

- Adam of Goxhill
- Guy de Ver

## Evidence supporting

- [[Hunter 1844 - Pipe Roll 1 Richard I page 61]] transcribes the royal-account entry `pro concordia ... facta inter eum et Adam generum suum in Curia Regis`, describing an agreement between Guy and Adam, his son-in-law, in the king's court. The next entry identifies `Adam gener Wido`, Adam the son-in-law of Guy.
- [[Farrer 1916 - Early Yorkshire Charters Volume III Goxhill pedigree]], printed page 61, independently reconstructs Adam as Guy's son-in-law and cites this exact Pipe Roll page.

## Evidence against

No reviewed evidence contradicts the relationship.

## Conflicts

The Pipe Roll does not name Adam's wife. It therefore cannot by itself identify Guy's daughter as Gundreda de Ver.

## Analysis

The relationship term is explicit in a published transcription of a contemporary royal account. This establishes an affinity relationship between Adam and Guy, but not the identity of the daughter through whom it arose.

This is an upstream medieval relationship only. It does not establish a connection to the Selby surname or the user's documented ancestry.

## Confidence rationale

**Strong:** the Pipe Roll twice describes Adam as Guy's son-in-law, including in Guy's own account entry.

## Next research action

Locate the underlying concord or another relationship-bearing charter that names Guy's daughter and test whether she was Gundreda de Ver.
