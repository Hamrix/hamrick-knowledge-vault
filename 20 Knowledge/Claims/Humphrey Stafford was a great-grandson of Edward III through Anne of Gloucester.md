# Humphrey Stafford was a great-grandson of Edward III through Anne of Gloucester

## Claim

Humphrey Stafford, first duke of Buckingham (1402–1460), was a great-grandson of King Edward III through his mother, Anne of Gloucester: Edward III was the father of Thomas of Woodstock; Thomas was Anne’s father; and Anne was Humphrey Stafford’s mother.

## Evidence supporting

- [[Rawcliffe 2008 - Humphrey Stafford]] states Humphrey’s parentage and identifies Anne of Gloucester as a daughter of Thomas of Woodstock, son of Edward III.
- [[Cokayne - Complete Peerage Stafford-Gloucester relationship]] independently gives the same Stafford–Gloucester relationship and identifies Thomas of Woodstock as a son of Edward III.

## Evidence against

No contrary evidence was found for this medieval relationship.

## Conflicts

No conflict affecting the relationship was identified. Some reference works vary in styling Anne as “of Gloucester,” “of Woodstock,” or “Plantagenet”; those naming conventions do not alter the documented parent-child chain.

## Analysis

The sources independently agree on all three links in the short chain:

1. Edward III → Thomas of Woodstock;
2. Thomas of Woodstock → Anne of Gloucester;
3. Anne of Gloucester → Humphrey Stafford.

This proves a specific medieval Stafford connection to Edward III. It does not prove that an unspecified Stafford, Selby, Haskins, or modern person descends from Humphrey Stafford or from this branch.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: a modern expert biography and an independent standard peerage reference agree on the complete, short relationship chain. The conclusion is deliberately restricted to the named medieval people.
