# The 1790 and 1791 Parker Selby records describe different estate histories

## Claim

The Parker Selby executor bond dated 29 January 1790 and the Parker Selby administration de bonis non bond dated 15 June 1791 describe different prior administrations and must not be merged as one estate. The official index also records a separate Parker estate opened under Nancy Selby in April 1789.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Parker Selby 1790 Worcester executor bond]] appoints James Selby executor under Parker's will.
- [[FamilySearch - Parker Selby 1791 Worcester de bonis non bond]] appoints James Selby and John Gunby administrators de bonis non and identifies deceased John Selby, Esquire, as the former administrator.
- [[Maryland State Archives - Parker Selby Worcester estate index]] places the filings in separate rows labeled Parker "of Wm." and Parker "of John," with different inventories and accounts. A third row records April 1789 filings under Nancy Selby.
- [[FamilySearch - Worcester estate docket Parker Selby index page]] independently exposes the early docket image and confirms the April 1789 Nancy Selby / Parker track.
- [[FamilySearch - Nancy Selby 1789 Parker Selby executor bond]] supplies the opening instrument for the third track and names Nancy Selby as Parker's executrix.
- [[FamilySearch - John and Nancy Gunby 1792-1795 Parker Selby estate accounts]] continues that same Nancy-administered track through its 1792 account and 1795 fourth and final account.

## Analysis and limits

The administrative histories conflict if treated as a single uninterrupted estate: the 1790 bond makes James executor, while the 1791 bond describes John Selby, Esquire, as the earlier administrator whose work remained unfinished. The official index also tracks the files separately.

This proves that the records describe distinct estate tracks. The January 1790 executor bond cannot be treated as the opening paper for the June 1791 de bonis non file, and neither can replace the April 1789 bond and later Gunby accounts. The index's parenthetical shorthand does not prove either man's father, and the reviewed Nancy-track records still do not assign the 1790 guardian bond for George and Elizabeth to that estate. The original wills, inventories, distributions, and land records remain the controlling identity evidence.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the different administrative histories and separate index rows are explicit. The confidence does not extend to the ultimate identity or parentage of either decedent.
