# Henry and Eleanor Selby conveyed their Nicholas County Brush Fork home place to Parker Selby in 1838

## Claim

Henry Selby and wife Eleanor conveyed Parker Selby Henry's Nicholas County Brush Fork home place of about 89 acres and an adjoining tract of about 45 acres by a deed dated 1 March 1838.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1838 Nicholas deed to Parker Selby]] contains the original recorded deed and its direct and reverse deed-index entries.
- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1833 Nicholas County will and 1835 inventory]] independently establishes that a Major Selby of Nicholas County had a son named Henry five years earlier.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby 1830 Nicholas Henderson estate deeds]] places a Henry Selby of Nicholas County in the same Brushy Fork land system before Major's will.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1850 Nicholas census]] records the same Henry-Eleanor name pair in Nicholas County twelve years later.

## Analysis and limitations

The deed proves the Henry-Eleanor marriage statement, Henry's residence and possession, the two parcels, and the conveyance to Parker. Its $1,200 and natural-affection consideration indicates a close or favored relationship but does not define one.

The same two names in one 1850 Nicholas household, combined with their documented 1838 marriage, strongly identifies the census household with the grantors. The grantor is also a strong candidate for the Henry named as Major's son in 1833 because the name, county, chronology, and Brush Fork setting agree. No reviewed record expressly joins that Henry to Major's estate. Parker's relationship to Henry is wholly unstated.

A Parker Selby, age 35 and born in Maryland, was enumerated in Bourbon County in 1850 and married Tabitha Irvin there in 1851. Those records are compatible with the 1838 grantee but name no parent and do not prove his relationship to Henry.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the transaction and marriage are directly recorded. The identification of the grantors with the 1850 Henry-Eleanor household is strong, the identification of this Henry as Major's son remains strong but unproved, and no kinship to Parker is assigned.
