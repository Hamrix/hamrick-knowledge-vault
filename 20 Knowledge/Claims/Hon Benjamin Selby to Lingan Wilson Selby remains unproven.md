# Hon Benjamin Selby to Lingan Wilson Selby remains unproven

## Claim status

No reviewed original record names Hon. Benjamin Selby as a son of Lingan Wilson Selby.

## Evidence considered

- [[FamilySearch - Lingan Wilson Selby 1821 Adair estate inventory]] confirms Lingan's Adair County estate but names no family member.
- [[FamilySearch - Lingan Wilson Selby Adair administration and widow orders 1821]] proves that Benjamin Selby was granted administration of Lingan's estate on 5 February 1821 and that Mary Selby was Lingan's widow on 2 July 1821. The court does not identify Benjamin as kin.
- [[FamilySearch and KDLA - Lingan Selby Adair probate sequence audit 1821]] verifies from the original county-order index that Lingan's recorded sequence consists of four 1821 actions: appointment of administrator, appointment of appraisers, inventory and appraisement, and allotment to widow. No indexed sale, settlement, distribution, heir, partition, or guardianship entry follows.
- [[FamilySearch - Benjamin Selby Adair probate appearances 1822-1823]] directly places a Benjamin in the same county's probate network immediately after Lingan's death, but neither appearance names Lingan or states a relationship.
- [[FamilySearch - Lingan W Selby 1809 Adair power of attorney]] strongly ties the Adair Lingan to the Prince George's County family cluster but names no child or heir.
- [[FamilySearch - Adair Selby deed indexes and Wilson conveyance 1819-1821]] shows Benjamin acquiring land from Ghalston Wilson through an attorney and records Jenny Wilson as Ghalston's wife. Neither the original deeds nor the indexed Selby entries name Lingan or state kinship.
- [[FamilySearch - elder Benjamin Selby 1811 marriage and 1830-1840 censuses]] places an older Benjamin in Adair County but names no parent.
- [[FamilySearch - Benjamin Selby Adair probate and guardianship negative searches]] records the failed OCR-dependent search for an elder-Benjamin estate or guardianship bridge.
- [[FamilySearch - Franklin County Selby probate negative search 1824-1854]] records the manual Will Book 2 index review and the bounded estate-settlement search around the reported 1845 death.
- [[FamilySearch - Franklin County Benjamin Selby tax transition 1845-1847]] establishes separate senior and junior entries in 1845 and a later tax transition, but names no parent, heir, or Adair relative.
- [[Kentucky General Assembly - Selby negative search 1845-1846]] found no family-facing memorial in the first legislative session after the reported death.
- The FamilySearch tree relationship was reviewed as a lead. The parent relationship has no relationship-bearing source, and the profile note attributes the assertion to a 2003 email rather than an original record.

## Analysis and limits

Shared county, chronology, surname, probate-network proximity, and an online-tree assertion are not proof of parentage. The 1809 power of attorney substantially strengthens the identification of Adair's Lingan with William Magruder Selby's Maryland son, but it does not name Benjamin. Benjamin's appointment as administrator is stronger evidence of direct association than the later probate-network appearances, but fiduciary service does not state a relationship. Mary is directly identified as Lingan's widow; no reviewed original equates her with Drucilla Fowler. Sequential FamilySearch transcript-assisted sweeps of Will Book B images 376-525 and Court Order Books D-E images 151-546 found no later Lingan sale, administrator settlement, distribution, child, or heir entry. The original county-order index independently bounds the recorded sequence to four entries at Book D, pages 213 and 238-239. The early Circuit Court order volume and general index produced no Lingan result, while the grantee-index review found no Lingan-to-Benjamin deed. Reverse-direction Franklin tax, probate, bond, guardianship, court-order, and legislative-journal work also found no Lingan or parent-level bridge. Because automated transcription and indexes are incomplete and error-prone, these are bounded negative searches rather than proof of absence. Kentucky's retention schedule confirms that pre-1870 unbound settlement files can contain family material omitted from the books. Lingan's estate bond or loose packet, deed partitions, tax cessation, church record, or a child-naming record for Benjamin remains necessary.

## Confidence rationale

`strong`: the gap is documented after review of the attached tree sources, the 1821 estate material, and targeted probate and guardianship searches. This does not prove that Lingan was not Benjamin's father.
