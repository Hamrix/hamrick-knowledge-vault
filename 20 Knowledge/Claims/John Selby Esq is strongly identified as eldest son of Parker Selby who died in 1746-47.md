# John Selby Esq is strongly identified as eldest son of Parker Selby who died in 1746-47

## Claim

The eldest son John named in Parker Selby's 1746 will is strongly identified as the Worcester County legislator and testator John Selby Esq., who died in 1790.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Parker Selby 1746 York County will]] names eldest son John and sons Parker and James in the Worcester County family near Snow Hill.
- [[FamilySearch - Parker Selby 1747 Worcester executor bond]] shows John and Parker acting as executors of the same estate in Worcester County.
- [[FamilySearch - Parker Selby 1773 Worcester County will images]] names James and John as the later Parker's brothers, repeating the sibling triad from the 1746 will.
- [[Maryland State Archives - John and Parker Selby legislative biographies]] explicitly identifies the John who died in 1790 as Parker's eldest son and reports the same sibling group and 750-acre inheritance.
- [[FamilySearch - John Selby 1790 Worcester County will images]] independently anchors the later John Selby Esq. and his family in Worcester County.

## Analysis and limits

The chronology, place, executor roles, repeated John-Parker-James sibling group, reported 750-acre inheritance, and institutional biography form a strong identification. No reviewed later primary record yet calls the 1790 testator "son of Parker" or expressly cross-references Parker's 1746 will. The identification therefore remains `strong`, while the narrower 1746 father-son statement is `confirmed`.

## Next research action

Retrieve the land conveyance or estate division carrying Parker's 750-acre home plantation to John and seek a later deed, chancery pleading, or probate instrument that identifies John Selby Esq. as Parker's son.
