# Edgar was a son of Edmund I

## Claim

Edgar, king of the English, was a son of Edmund I (also called Edmund the Magnificent) and Ælfgifu.

## Subjects

- Edgar of England
- Edmund I of England
- Ælfgifu
- [[10 Projects/Aetheling Evidence Project/Project Index|Aetheling Evidence Project]]

## Evidence supporting

- [[DNB 1885-1900 - Edgar]] identifies Edgar as the younger son of Eadmund the Magnificent and Ælfgifu.
- [[1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica - Edgar]] independently identifies Edgar as the younger son of Edmund the Magnificent and Ælfgifu.

## Evidence against

No evidence against the named parent-child relationship was found.

## Conflicts

No conflict affecting Edgar's paternal relationship was identified. The sources use variant spellings for Edgar, Edmund/Eadmund, and Ælfgifu.

## Analysis

The DNB biography and the *Encyclopaedia Britannica* entry independently corroborate the direct relationship Edmund I to Edgar. This supplies one further historical Wessex-context link before [[Æthelred II was a son of Edgar]].

It does not establish a connection from Edmund I, Edgar, Æthelred II, or their descendants to a later Stafford, Selby, Haskins, Plantagenet, or modern family.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: two independent historical reference works agree on the direct relationship. The conclusion is confined to the named medieval people.
