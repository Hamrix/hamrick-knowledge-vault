# Thomas Selby who conveyed Simpleton in 1725 was grandson of its Thomas Selby patentee

## Claim

Thomas Selby, who with Margaret Selby conveyed the remaining Simpleton land in Somerset County in 1725, was a grandson of Thomas Selby, the holder of the tract's 1,250-acre patent.

## Subjects

- Thomas Selby, Simpleton patentee
- Thomas Selby, 1725 grantor
- [[10 Projects/Aetheling Evidence Project/Project Index|Aetheling Evidence Project]]

## Evidence supporting

- [[Maryland State Archives - Thomas Selby 1725 Simpleton deed]] is an official digitized manuscript deed. Its title recital calls the patent holder the grantor's "eldest Grandfather," and its operative language calls the grantor "Grandson of Thomas Selby deceased."

## Evidence against

No evidence against the stated grandparent-grandchild relationship was located in this review.

## Conflicts

The deed does not separately identify the intermediate father. The same-name evidence is therefore insufficient to equate that father automatically with the Thomas Selby who made the 1701 will, the Thomas Selby who died in 1758, or any other Thomas Selby.

## Analysis

The deed explicitly states the grandparent-grandchild relationship while recounting title to a uniquely named tract. That makes this a confirmed, bounded relationship between the 1725 grantor and the Thomas Selby identified as the Simpleton patentee.

It does not establish the identity of the intervening father, prove that the 1701 will concerns either of these men, or bridge this line to Major Selby, Etheldred Peters, the Staffords, the Plantagenets, Wessex, or any modern family.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: a reviewed official manuscript deed twice supplies the descent wording and connects it to the specific Simpleton patent tract.

## Next research action

Locate the probate, deed, or land-commission record for the intermediate Thomas Selby that names both the 1725 grantor and the earlier patentee, then compare it with the 1701 will before treating them as one three-generation family.
