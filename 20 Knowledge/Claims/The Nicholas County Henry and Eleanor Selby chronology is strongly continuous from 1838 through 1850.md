# The Nicholas County Henry and Eleanor Selby chronology is strongly continuous from 1838 through 1850

## Claim

The Henry and Eleanor Selby enumerated together in Nicholas County in 1850 are strongly identified with the married grantors who conveyed Henry's Nicholas County Brush Fork home place in 1838.

## Evidence

- [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1838 Nicholas deed to Parker Selby]] directly names the married grantors and Henry's Nicholas County home place.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1850 Nicholas census]] records the same name pair in the same county and reports Henry age 66, born Maryland, and Eleanor age 55, born Maryland.
- [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby daughter marriage records 1832-1849]] extends a father Henry Selby's Nicholas County chronology on both sides of the 1838 deed.
- [[FamilySearch - Margaret Selby 1836 will and estate settlements]] names Kitty and Duland as Margaret's granddaughters and daughters of Henry, and records Henry's opposition to probate of her will.

## Analysis and limitations

The uncommon paired names, their documented marriage in 1838, the unchanged county, and the twelve-year interval support a strong identity. No single record explicitly cross-references the deed, states relationships in the census household, or says that the household lived on the same tract.

Margaret's 1836 will now provides a direct family bridge to daughters of Henry, while the marriage records independently identify Catharine and Dulcina as daughters of the local Henry. Kitty strongly matches Catharine, and Duland strongly matches Dulcina, but the different spellings remain visible. Together with Henry's participation in Margaret's probate, this makes the later Henry's identity as Major's son strong, though no later record literally repeats the father-son statement.

The 1850 Henry's estimated 1784 birth is therefore important negative evidence: the Worcester child who was about seven in 1785 cannot have been this adult Henry's father. That chronological conflict rules against identifying the Worcester ward as the Nicholas testator but does not itself identify Major's older generation.

## Confidence rationale

`strong`: the paired names, documented 1838 marriage, county, and chronology make one couple highly likely, but the records do not explicitly cross-reference each other.
