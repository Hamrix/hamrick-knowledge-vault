# 1785 Worcester guardianship record identifies Major Selby as orphan of John Selby

## Claim

A Worcester County guardian bond dated 1 July 1785 identifies Major Selby, about seven years old, as the orphan of John Selby, deceased. A related estate-view certificate repeats the father-child identification.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Major Selby 1785 Worcester guardianship estate-view image]] contains the reviewed guardian bond and estate-view manuscript. The bond says Major was about seven on 1 July 1785, calls him the orphan of John Selby deceased, and appoints John Selby Esq. as guardian until Major attained fourteen. The related certificate repeats that Major was the orphan of John Selby.

## Analysis and limit

This is direct evidence that the Major Selby named in these guardianship records was a child of the deceased John Selby named as his father. It is not proof that John Selby Esq., the guardian, was Major's father or another relative; the record assigns him the guardian role but does not state kinship. Zadock Selby and Parker Selby also appear as co-obligors on the guardian bond, but the bond states no relationship between them and Major.

The record cannot be used by itself to identify this minor Major with the separate adult Major Selby assessed in Worcester County in 1783, or with the Major Selby documented in later Somerset, Kentucky, and Indiana records. Those identities require an intervening, person-specific bridge. Therefore it does not yet prove the parental line of the later Major Selby branch or any colonial/royal descent claim.

## Next research action

Locate the original appointment or guardian bond for John Selby Esq. and Major Selby, then follow the minor's real-estate title, releases on attaining majority, marriage, deeds, and probate records. Compare those records against the later Major Selby's 1811/1817 Somerset license entries and the 1820-1860 census trail before treating the men as the same person.
