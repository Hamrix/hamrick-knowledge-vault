# Collins Parker administered Charles Parker's estate with Charles and John Parker bound in 1761

## Claim

An original Worcester County administration bond dated 11 February 1761 names Collins Parker, Charles Parker, and John Parker as bound parties for the estate of deceased Charles Parker, and the following entry grants letters of administration to Collins Parker on 12 February 1761.

## Evidence supporting

- [[FamilySearch - Charles Parker 1761 Worcester administration bond]] records the visually reviewed original bond and letters.
- [[Maryland State Archives - Charles Parker Worcester estate index]] separately indexes the administration on 12 February 1761 and names Collins as administrator with Charles and John Parker as sureties.

## Evidence against

No evidence against the literal administrative and bond-party roles was located.

## Conflicts

FamilySearch OCR misreads the bond and letters years. Visual review, the estate index, and the associated 1761 inventory resolve the event to 1761.

## Analysis

The record establishes a tightly associated Parker probate group. It does not state whether any bound party was a son, brother, nephew, creditor, neighbor, or other associate of the decedent. The bound Charles Parker is necessarily a living participant distinct from the deceased Charles Parker, but the record gives no relationship between them.

## Confidence rationale

`confirmed`: the original bond and letters expressly name the parties and roles. The confidence does not extend to an unstated kinship.
