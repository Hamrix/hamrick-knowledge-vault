# Kentucky Senate journals - Benjamin Selby auditor appointment and death succession 1834-1846

## Citation

Kentucky General Assembly, Senate, *Journal of the Senate of the Commonwealth of Kentucky*, sessions of 1833-1834, pages 47 and 70; 1834-1835, auditor's report facing page 44; and 1845-1846, page 37; original page images hosted by Internet Archive.

## Original entries reviewed

- On 8 January 1834, page 47 records Governor John Breathitt's 4 January nomination of **Benjamin Selby** as Auditor of Public Accounts in place of Porter Clay, resigned.
- On 14 January 1834, page 70 records the Senate's advice and consent to Benjamin Selby's appointment.
- The 1834 annual revenue statement, dated 10 October 1834, is signed **Benjamin Selby, Auditor of Public Accounts**.
- On 5 January 1846, page 37 records Governor William Owsley's nomination of Harry I. Bodley as Auditor of Public Accounts "in place of Ben. Selby, deceased," followed by the Senate's consent.

The page images were visually reviewed. The 1845-1846 OCR renders the surname as `Sebly`, but the original image plainly reads `Selby`.

## Analysis

These official entries prove that a Benjamin Selby was appointed Kentucky Auditor of Public Accounts in January 1834, acted in that office during 1834, and was the deceased predecessor replaced by Harry I. Bodley in January 1846. Combined with [[Boons Lick Times - Benjamin Selby death notice reprint 1845]], the office sequence and reported death date of 25 May 1845 are mutually consistent.

The records correct the modern [[Kentucky Auditor - historical auditor list|Kentucky Auditor summary]], which assigns `Ben Shelby` to 1820-1834 and Thomas S. Page to 1834-1846. Original Senate journals instead show Porter Clay serving through 1833, Benjamin Selby appointed in 1834, and Bodley appointed after Selby's death.

## Reliability and limitations

These are contemporaneous official legislative records and direct evidence for the appointment, officeholder spelling, service, death status by January 1846, and succession. They do not provide Benjamin's age, birthplace, spouse, children, parents, residence, or estate details.

The entries therefore corroborate the office description attached to the reported father in the 1878 and 1883 accounts, but they do not prove the younger Benjamin's parent-child relationship or identify Lingan Wilson Selby as the auditor's father.
