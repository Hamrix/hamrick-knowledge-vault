# FamilySearch - John Selby 1790 Worcester County will images

## Citation

Worcester County, Maryland, will of John Selby, dated 13 November 1790 and proved 10 December 1790, *Wills 1783-1790*, volume 13, images 138-139 of 142; FamilySearch image group 005080347, [opening image `33S7-9TBJ-SRVN`](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:33S7-9TBJ-SRVN?view=fullText&lang=en), accessed 8 August 2026.

## Description

Digitized manuscript will record. The will occupies the opening and continuation pages recorded in images 138-139.

## Reviewed content

- The testator signs as John Selby; the probate entry identifies him as John Selby, Esquire, late of Worcester County.
- The will calls James Selby, Daniel Selby, and William Selby the testator's sons.
- It calls Nancy Gunby the testator's daughter. It separately calls George a son of Nancy, Betsey Selby a daughter of Nancy, and John Selby a son of Nancy.
- It repeatedly calls Major Selby the testator's grandson and expressly names **"Major Selby son of John"** among four grandsons: William Selby son of Daniel, Major Selby son of John, John Selby son of Nancy Gunby, and Parker Selby son of James.
- It gives land and personal property to Major, directs delivery at maturity or marriage, and includes Major in a survivorship devise with son James and the other named grandsons.
- The will names Ann Selby as the testator's wife and executrix. The probate material records her oath and her election of dower on 10 December 1790.
- The manuscript dates the will to **13 November 1790**, correcting the prior 20 November reading.

## Reliability and limitations

This is a primary manuscript record reviewed in image form. It directly proves the stated child and grandchild relationships, including the John-to-Major link. Although secondary reference material may suggest the testator's own parentage, the will does not name his father. It therefore does not establish that he was the John Selby named in Thomas Selby's 1758 will or a son of Parker Selby.

The will proves the colonial minor Major's place in this family. It does not identify that minor with the adult Major assessed in 1783 or with the later Kentucky man.
