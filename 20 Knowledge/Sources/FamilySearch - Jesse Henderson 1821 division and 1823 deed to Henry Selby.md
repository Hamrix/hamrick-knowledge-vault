## 1821 division examined

The court record says the commissioners divided fifty acres between the heirs of Jesse Henderson. William Padgett's plat, dated 14 December 1821, contains eight equal lots of 6 acres 10 poles, or 6.25 acres each:

| Lot | Name written in the division |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Susan Henderson |
| 2 | Jesse Henderson |
| 3 | Henry Selby |
| 4 | Levin Henderson |
| 5 | Polly Henderson |
| 6 | William Henderson |
| 7 | Hetty Henderson |
| 8 | Henry Selby's |

The commissioners' report was produced at the December 1821 court term and ordered recorded. The original image, rather than the automated transcript, supplies the date and lot assignments. The transcript's 1825 date is an OCR error.

Henry therefore held two interests, lots 3 and 8, in a division expressly made among Jesse Henderson's heirs. The record does not explain whether either interest arose through a spouse, purchase, assignment, or another legal right, and it does not call Henry Jesse's son or son-in-law.

## 1823 deed examined

On 22 August 1823 Jesse Henderson of Franklin County, Indiana, conveyed Henry Selby of Nicholas County, Kentucky, lot 2 for two dollars. The deed recites that the parcel came from the general plat and division of the lands of Jesse Henderson deceased and had been allotted to the grantor Jesse Henderson by the commissioners. Jesse signed by mark and acknowledged the deed the same day. The record date appears to be 2 September 1823; a margin entry says it was delivered on 5 November 1832.

The deed proves that Henry acquired Jesse Henderson's inherited lot 2 after the original division. It does not state the grantor's relationship to Nelly Henderson or explain the nominal consideration.

## Correlation with later deeds

The complete reviewed sequence shows Henry accumulating at least six of the eight equal shares by 1830:

- lots 3 and 8 in the 1821 division;
- lot 2 from Jesse Henderson in 1823;
- lot 4 from Levin Henderson in 1828;
- lot 1 from Susan Henderson Williams in 1830; and
- lot 6 from William G. Henderson in 1830.

Polly Henderson's lot 5 and Hetty Henderson's lot 7 have not yet been traced. The sequence substantially strengthens the identity and family-network correlation between the Henry Selby-Nelly Henderson bond of 1809 and the later Nicholas County Henry. It still does not directly identify Nelly with Eleanor, state Jesse's relationship to Nelly, or identify Henry's Selby parents.
