# Adair County original record access map for Benjamin Selby

## Purpose

This note identifies the surviving original-record series required to establish the identity, wife, children, death, and parentage of the Benjamin Selby active in Adair County, Kentucky, from at least 1811 through 1823.

## Record survival

The Kentucky Department for Libraries and Archives does not list a courthouse-record disaster for Adair County. Adair County was created in 1802 from Green County. Research before county formation must therefore include Green County and any other jurisdiction in which the subject lived before 1802.

KDLA states that its holdings include original or microfilmed county deed books, marriage records, will books, tax lists, county order books, and court case files. Those are the correct record classes for resolving this identity.

## Tax books

FamilySearch catalog: `Tax books, 1802-1878`, Adair County Tax Assessor, catalog 154727.

Relevant image groups:

- 1802-1824 — film 7851; DGS 7834395;
- 1824-1831, 1833, 1835-1838, 1840-1844 — film 7852; DGS 8188340;
- 1845-1858 — film 7853; DGS 8516054.

### Research use

A year-by-year extraction should record:

- every Selby spelling variant;
- white male tithables;
- acreage and county;
- watercourse;
- original patentee or entry name;
- horses and enslaved persons where recorded;
- first appearance, last appearance, and gaps.

This can establish whether Benjamin remained in Adair County after 1823, whether more than one adult Benjamin existed, and whether Robert Benjamin's 1826 birth fits the same household.

## Marriage records

FamilySearch catalog: `Marriage records, 1802-1973; index, 1802-1954`, Adair County Clerk of the County Court, catalog 128449.

Relevant image group:

- general marriage index beginning 1802 — film 830574; DGS 7723361.

The GEDCOM cites a derivative 1811 marriage entry for Benjamin Selby and a woman transcribed as `Elizab? McClane`. The original index, bond, consent, return, and minister's record must be inspected. The derivative spelling must not be silently normalized.

## Deed records

The published index to Adair County Deed Book C, 1811-1815, identifies:

- index entry 186 — Benjamin Selby, indenture;
- index entry 188 — Benjamin Selby, indenture;
- index entry 596 — Benjamin and Betsy Selby, indenture;
- index entry 599 — Benjamin Selby, indenture.

The index states that the source is Adair County Deed Book C on Kentucky State Archives microfilm roll 828890. The original deeds should be inspected for residence, wife and dower language, signatures or marks, consideration, tract description, watercourse, witnesses, adjoining owners, and later acknowledgments.

A separate deed-index entry naming `Benjamin & Betsy Selby` is the strongest current lead to the wife's given name, but it does not establish her maiden surname.

## Probate and wills

FamilySearch identifies Adair County wills from 1804-1930, with an index extending to 1970. A separately digitized abstract volume covers Will Books A and B for 1804-1818.

The Kentucky probate collection includes wills, bonds, inventories, sales, settlements, and related county-court material. A negative will-index search alone is insufficient: an intestate estate may appear only in administrator bonds, appraisals, sale bills, settlements, or court orders.

### Required searches

Search for:

- Benjamin Selby;
- Betsy, Elizabeth, or Eliza Selby;
- Robert Benjamin or Benjamin Robert Selby;
- every Selby administrator, executor, guardian, appraiser, purchaser, witness, creditor, debtor, and heir;
- estate proceedings after Benjamin's last tax or court appearance.

## Court orders and civil cases

Published abstracts already place Benjamin in county office and conveyancing activity. The original county order books and the indexed civil-case compilation for 1802-1826 should be checked for:

- guardianships and apprenticeships;
- estate appointments;
- road orders and jury service;
- debt or land litigation;
- commissioners' reports;
- references to wife, children, heirs, or removal from the county.

## Access limitation during this audit

The catalogues confirm that the microfilms have been digitized, but FamilySearch notes that some images require sign-in, a FamilySearch Center, an affiliate library, or onsite access. The underlying tax, marriage, deed, and probate images were not available for direct inspection in the current audit session.

This is an access gap, not evidence that the records do not exist.

## Priority order

1. Deed Book C entries 186, 188, 596, and 599.
2. Original 1811 marriage index, bond, consent, and return.
3. Tax books 1810-1835, expanded backward and forward as needed.
4. Wills, administrator bonds, inventories, sale bills, settlements, and court orders after the last known appearance.
5. Green County records before Adair County's creation in 1802.
