# HM Land Registry - Practice Guide 22 Manors

## Citation

HM Land Registry, *Practice guide 22: manors*, last updated 2015-06-24, accessed 2026-07-29, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/manors/practice-guide-22-manors.

## Description

Official guidance distinguishing a lordship title, manorial land, and manorial rights, and explaining how each may affect land registration.

## Relevant extracts

- A lordship title, physical manorial land, and manorial rights are separate elements and may exist separately.
- Since 2003-10-13, an application cannot be made for first registration of a lordship title.
- Physical manorial land remains registrable in the same way as other corporeal land.
- A registered lordship title has no title plan, and HM Land Registry does not hold a definitive record of the original manor's extent.
- The existence of a lordship does not necessarily mean that legally exercisable manorial rights remain attached to it.
- A person seeking to protect or note manorial rights must satisfy HM Land Registry that the rights exist.

## Reliability and limitations

This is authoritative procedural guidance from HM Land Registry. It does not decide whether any particular lordship, parcel, or right exists, nor does it establish ownership of The White House at Cliffe.

## Claims supported

- [[First registration of a lordship title has not been possible since 13 October 2003]]

## Claims contradicted

- The legacy proposition that a new lordship title for Whitehouse or Selby Manor can be first-registered alongside the freehold.
- Any assertion that a lordship title alone establishes the ownership or boundaries of physical land.
