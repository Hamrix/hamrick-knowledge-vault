# FamilySearch - Thomas Rule 1817 Nicholas County Haws survey deeds

## Citation

Nicholas County, Kentucky, Deed Book E, pages 132-134, Humphrey Marshall and William Gunnett to Thomas Rule, 25 August 1817, and Laban Shipp and wife Elizabeth to Thomas Rule, 14 October 1817, both recorded 31 October 1817; [FamilySearch image 358 of 621](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSK4-6Y4B?view=fullText&keywords=Laban+Shipp%2CThomas+Rule%2CShipp&lang=en) and [image 359 of 621](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSK4-6YDB?view=fullText&keywords=Laban+Shipp%2CThomas+Rule%2CShipp&lang=en), DGS 8141837; [FamilySearch catalog record](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/koha:432725); accessed 8 August 2026.

## Records examined

The first deed identifies Thomas Rule as of Bourbon County and conveys 92 acres commonly called surplus in Haw's survey. The land lay on the north side of Hinkston in Nicholas County, described parenthetically as "formerly Bourbon." Its bounds include the upper corner of Haw's original survey, the division corner between Shipp and Haws, Brushy Fork, and Hinkston.

The second deed conveys Thomas Rule about 58 acres, one rood, and 10 poles, part of Samuel Haws's 5,000-acre survey on Brushy Fork in Nicholas County. Its bounds include Daniel Talbert, Valentine Cress, Kennett lines, and the division between Coleby and Shipp. Laban Shipp and wife Elizabeth acknowledged the conveyance in Bourbon County, and it was recorded in Nicholas County on 31 October 1817.

## Genealogical value

Thomas Rule is the named person who furnished a Major Selby deed for the 1 August 1815 Bourbon survey. Valentine Cress was among the occupants served with Major Selby in the related 1812 ejectment action. The 1817 originals therefore place Rule and Cress in a Nicholas County Haws-survey and Brushy Fork land context two years after the Major Selby survey.

This is meaningful associate and land-geography evidence. It helps explain how the Bourbon dispute network intersects Nicholas County records and gives specific tract language for further deed comparison.

## Reliability and limitations

The grantee, county residence, acreage, survey, waterways, boundary owners, execution dates, and recording date appear in the original recorded deeds. FamilySearch's automated transcript was used as a finding aid and checked against the images; obvious OCR renderings such as "Hull" for Rule are not adopted.

Neither 1817 deed names Major Selby, a Selby relative, or the 1820 Nicholas household. "Formerly Bourbon" describes the older jurisdiction of the land and does not itself prove a post-1815 county move. The records support an associate-network bridge, not identity or migration proof.
