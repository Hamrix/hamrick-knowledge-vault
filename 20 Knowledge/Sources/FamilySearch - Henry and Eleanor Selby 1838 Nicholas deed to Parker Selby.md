# FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1838 Nicholas deed to Parker Selby

## Citation

Nicholas County, Kentucky, Deed Book L, pages 444-445, Henry Selby and wife Eleanor to Parker Selby, instrument dated 1 March 1838 and acknowledged and recorded 1 March 1839, [FamilySearch image 540 of DGS 8402418](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSBY-MSBQ-N?view=fullText&keywords=Selby&lang=en); Nicholas County general deed index, Henry Selby to Parker Selby, page 444, DGS 7900678, image 204; [FamilySearch catalog record](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/koha:432725); accessed 8 August 2026.

## Record examined

The deed is dated 1 March 1838 and names Henry Selby and Eleanor his wife as grantors and Parker Selby as grantee. For $1,200 and "natural affection," Henry and Eleanor conveyed the land then in Henry's possession on the waters of Brush Fork in Nicholas County.

The property included the home place where Henry then lived, described as about 89 acres, together with a small adjoining tract of about 45 acres. Henry and Eleanor warranted the title to Parker. Both signed, and Eleanor relinquished her dower interest. The clerk's certificate says the deed was acknowledged and recorded on 1 March 1839, one year after the instrument date.

The general index independently locates the transaction under Henry as grantor and Parker as grantee at page 444. Its reverse entry locates the same deed under Parker as grantee.

## Genealogical value

The deed directly establishes Henry and Eleanor as husband and wife, Henry's Nicholas County Brush Fork residence and home place, and a substantial conveyance from Henry to Parker supported partly by natural affection. It does not state what relationship, if any, existed between Henry and Parker.

Major Selby's 1833 Nicholas County will directly names a son Henry Selby. The later deed's same uncommon surname, given name, county, land neighborhood, and chronology make the grantor a strong candidate for Major's son. The deed itself does not mention Major, his estate, Peggy, Polly, Charles Parker as executor, or an inheritance, so the identity remains a correlation rather than a proved continuation.

Two 1830 deeds in [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby 1830 Nicholas Henderson estate deeds]] independently place a Henry Selby of Nicholas County acquiring small Brushy Fork and Hinkston parcels before Major made his will. They tighten the local chronology but do not cross-reference the 1838 home place or state Henry's father.

The 1850 census in [[FamilySearch - Henry and Eleanor Selby 1850 Nicholas census]] records the same two names in one Nicholas County household. When combined with their documented 1838 marriage, that is a strong identity correlation with the grantors, though the census does not cross-reference this deed or state household relationships. A separate 1850 Bourbon County census record reports a Parker Selby, age 35 and born in Maryland, but does not name parents or connect him to this conveyance.

## Reliability and limitations

The original recorded deed directly supplies the parties, spouse, consideration, natural-affection wording, residence, acreage, and land location. The one-year difference between the instrument and recording dates is retained as written and should not be silently normalized.

The record does not call Parker Henry's son, brother, nephew, or other relative. It does not prove that the grantor was Major's son, and the shared Brush Fork setting does not by itself establish identity or kinship.
