# Bunce 1948 - Some of the Ancestors Selby genealogy

## Citation

Julia Locke Frame Bunce, compiler, *Some of the Ancestors of the Reverend John Selby Frame and His Wife Clara Winchester Dana* (privately printed, 1948), 48, digital PDF, Seeking My Roots, https://www.seekingmyroots.com/members/files/G002389.pdf, accessed 2026-08-13.

## Description

Privately printed compiled genealogy with a Selby section based on family statements and research in cited Maryland records.

## Provenance

The title page states that Bunce compiled the volume from data collected by and for her. In the preface, Bunce says that Selby-family data had been given to her grandfather, Rev. Reuben Frame, by her great-grandfather, Capt. John Selby. She also says that the Selby line received an extensive search of original sources, while asking readers to allow for errors and omissions.

The page-48 child list is followed by the attribution "Statement of Capt. John Selby." The compiler explains that destruction of court proceedings and land commissioners' views made the reported birth information difficult to substantiate. This places the list in a family-statement tradition transmitted through multiple generations, not in a contemporaneous parent-child record.

## Reviewed content

Visual review of book page 48 confirms that the compiled genealogy lists the children of Thomas Selby and Elizabeth, whose surname the compiler tentatively gives as Houston, as:

- Elizabeth;
- Margaret;
- Thomas, reported born at Indian Town in 1744;
- Henry;
- Major; and
- Daniel.

The preceding page abstracts a Worcester County deed chain for Thomas and Elizabeth Selby:

- 9 November 1749: Thomas bought 170 acres, part of *Choice* and *Dull Folly*, from his father, cited to Worcester County Deeds B:203.
- 20 September 1766: Thomas and wife Elizabeth sold 34 acres, part of *Choice*, cited to Worcester County Deeds F:542.
- 9 October 1767: Thomas and Elizabeth conveyed part of *Choice* to their son Thomas Jr., with a life-possession reservation, cited to Worcester County Deeds G:303.

The following generation states that Thomas Jr. later sold 136.25 acres of *Choice* and *Dull Folly* on 10 April 1802. These abstracts provide original-record targets, but the originals were not reviewed in this research pass.

## Reliability and limitations

The volume is a derivative compiled genealogy written in 1948. It is valuable because it preserves a named family informant and gives specific archival citations, but it is not independent proof of the reported parent-child relationships. The book does not cite a contemporaneous record that names Major as a child of Thomas and Elizabeth, and it does not identify its listed Major with the adult Major assessed on part of *Choice* in 1783 or with any later Kentucky or Indiana man.

The similarity between the reported Thomas-family tract and the 1783 assessment is a research lead only. The compiled deed chain appears to place the 136-acre residue in Thomas Jr.'s title, while the assessment lists Major with 140 acres. Original deeds, surveys, assessment pages, and probate distributions are required before deciding whether the interests are identical, overlapping, or unrelated.
