# Colonial Selby bridge GEDCOM audit

## Scope

This note audits the proposed bridge:

`Robert Benjamin Selby (1826-1902) -> Benjamin Selby (born 1779) -> Burridge H. Selby (born about 1720)`

The uploaded GEDCOM is treated as derivative research data, not as proof.

## Robert Benjamin Selby to Benjamin Selby

The GEDCOM places Robert Benjamin Selby (`I402`) as a child in family `F662`, with:

- father: Benjamin Selby Jr. (`I2283`), born 1779;
- mother: Elizab? McClane (`I2657`);
- marriage: 1811, Adair County, Kentucky;
- child: Robert Benjamin Selby, born 31 January 1826.

The only family-level citation is a derivative United States Marriages index entry supporting the marriage of Benjamin Selby and Elizab? McClane. The family record does not cite a birth, baptism, census, probate, guardianship, deed, Bible, obituary, or other record naming Robert as their child.

The marriage may establish a couple, but it does not independently establish every child later attached to that couple in an online tree or GEDCOM.

## Benjamin Selby to Burridge H. Selby

The GEDCOM places Benjamin Selby (`I2283`) as a child in family `F484`, with:

- father: Burridge Hutchins Selby (`I51`);
- mother: Mary Ann (Martin) Troop (`I64`);
- Benjamin's birth: 1779;
- family marriage entries: 1738 and 1784.

The child link has no cited probate, birth, baptism, guardianship, land, census, or family-record evidence.

The proposed mother's GEDCOM profile is demonstrably merged or corrupted. It contains eighteenth-century North Carolina events alongside a birth about 1946 and a 1947 New York passenger-arrival event. It therefore cannot be used as a stable identity record without reconstruction from original sources.

A marriage dated 1784 would also postdate Benjamin's stated 1779 birth. That is not impossible in every historical family situation, but the GEDCOM supplies no evidence resolving whether the marriage date, mother, birth date, or family assignment is correct.

## Burridge H. Selby identity conflicts

The Burridge record contains mutually inconsistent death assertions:

- before 3 May 1793;
- 1792;
- between 1785 and 1814.

It also carries an 1800 Hyde County census citation. If the 1800 citation belongs to the same man, a death in 1792 or before May 1793 is impossible. If the death date is correct, the 1800 citation belongs to another Burrage/Burridge Selby.

The 1790 Hyde County census is cited for the record. Independent transcriptions list a household headed by `Burridge H.` or `Burridge H. Selbey`, but the 1790 federal schedule named only household heads and recorded other household members as numerical categories. It cannot identify a son named Benjamin.

The same 1790 county transcription contains several distinct Selby household heads, including variants such as Burrage, Hutchins, Burridge H., Samuel, and others. The shared surname and similar given names create an identity-resolution problem that must be addressed with probate, deeds, tax lists, court minutes, church records, and the original census images.

## Samuel Selby to Burridge H. Selby

The GEDCOM places Burridge in family `F101` as a child of Samuel Selby and Sarah Smith. The family record cites marriage information for the couple, but no source is attached to Burridge's child link. A parental marriage citation does not identify all children of the marriage.

## Census limitation

The National Archives states that the 1790 schedule recorded only the name of the head of family and numerical counts in broad age, sex, freedom, and enslavement categories. The census can support the existence of a household headed by Burridge H. Selby, but cannot by itself prove that Benjamin Selby was in that household or was his son.

## Assessment

- Robert Benjamin -> Benjamin: `weak`; GEDCOM assignment and parental marriage lead only.
- Benjamin -> Burridge H.: `weak/speculative`; uncited child assignment plus corrupted maternal profile and chronology conflict.
- Burridge H. -> Samuel: `weak`; uncited child assignment.
- The continuous colonial bridge is not established.

## Required evidence

1. Inspect the original 1790 and 1800 census images and distinguish all Selby household heads.
2. Obtain Hyde County wills, estate files, deeds, tax records, and court minutes for Burridge/Burrage/Hutchins Selby variants.
3. Obtain Kentucky census, marriage, probate, land, and court records for Benjamin Selby and Robert Benjamin Selby.
4. Locate a record explicitly naming Robert as Benjamin's son and a separate record explicitly naming Benjamin as Burridge's son.
5. Reconstruct Mary Ann Troop/Martin as a clean identity rather than relying on the merged GEDCOM profile.
