## Records examined

- The fourth account, passed at February term 1797, identifies John Selby as guardian to **Major Selby, orphan of John Selby**, and carries forward a balance from an April term 1796 account.
- The fifth account, passed at February term 1798, repeats the guardian and father-child wording and carries forward the February 1797 balance.
- The sixth account, passed at February term 1799, again identifies Major as the orphan of John Selby and carries forward the February 1798 balance.
- The seventh account, passed at February term 1800, again identifies Major as the orphan of John Selby and carries forward the February 1799 balance. It charges the guardian with the prior balance and another year's profits, allows cash furnished to the orphan and register fees, and records a remaining balance.

The entries form a consecutive annual series. The 1797 account's reference to the April 1796 account extends the documented sequence one account earlier, although that earlier page was not separately reviewed in this search.

## Genealogical value

These primary court accounts carry the same bounded Worcester County identity from the earlier guardianship evidence through February 1800: **John Selby, guardian -> Major Selby, orphan of deceased John Selby**. They materially narrow the gap between the seven-year-old recorded in 1785 and the first discovered Kentucky appearance of an adult Major Selby in 1807.

## Reliability and limitations

The accounts were recorded and allowed by the Worcester County Orphans' Court. Each expressly repeats Major's status as the orphan of John Selby.

The seventh account is not labeled final and contains no receipt, release, migration statement, spouse, or new guardian. It does not itself identify the ward with the adult Major Selby in Bourbon County in 1807. The separate adult Major Selby assessed in Worcester County in 1783 remains chronologically distinct from the 1785 child.
