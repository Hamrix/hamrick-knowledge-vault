## Records examined

On 19 July 1830 Joseph Williams and Susan his wife, formerly Susan Henderson, of Franklin County, Indiana, conveyed Henry Selby of Nicholas County, Kentucky, a tract on the waters of Brushy Fork and Hinkston for $50. The parcel was part of the land of which Jesse Henderson died seized. Commissioners had allotted it to Susan as one of Jesse's heirs and designated it lot 1. It contained 6.25 acres.

A second deed, dated 13 July 1830, records William G. Henderson of Franklin County, Indiana, conveying Henry Selby another 6.25-acre parcel for $65. The tract was the portion set off to William as an heir of Jesse Henderson. William's wife Isabella relinquished her interest. A marginal entry and the transcript place later delivery or recording activity on 5 November 1832.

FamilySearch's automated transcript renders William's partition designation as lot 4. The original 1821 division in [[FamilySearch - Jesse Henderson 1821 division and 1823 deed to Henry Selby]] assigns William lot 6 and Levin Henderson lot 4. The deed transcript's lot-4 rendering is therefore treated as OCR error; William's parcel is lot 6.

## Genealogical value

The deeds directly place an adult Henry Selby in Nicholas County and in the Brushy Fork land system by July 1830, nearly three years before Major Selby's will named a son Henry. They also establish the Williams-Henderson relationships and the source of the two small parcels.

The uncommon name, county, chronology, and land neighborhood make this purchaser a strong candidate for Major's son. Neither deed names Major, Peggy, Polly, or a Selby parent, and neither says the purchase money came from an inheritance or family transfer.

An 1809 Bourbon marriage bond in [[FamilySearch - Henry Selby and Nelly Henderson 1809 Bourbon marriage bond]] names Jesse Henderson as co-obligor for Henry Selby's intended marriage to Nelly Henderson. This creates a strong family-network correlation with the 1830 purchases from Jesse's heirs, but neither the bond nor these deeds state Jesse's relationship to Nelly or prove that the 1809 groom was the 1830 purchaser.

## Reliability and limitations

The original recorded instruments directly state the parties, residence, consideration, Jesse Henderson heirship, acreage, and land location. The handwriting-recognition transcript was used as a locator and checked against the page sequence and general deed index. Susan's lot 1 and William's lot 6 are fixed by the original estate division; the automated 1830 transcript is not relied upon for William's numeral.

These are purchase deeds from Henderson heirs, not relationship statements about Henry's Selby family. They cannot by themselves identify Henry with Major's son or connect Henry to Parker Selby.
