# Modern Selby census citations extracted from GEDCOM

## Scope

This note records person-level census citations embedded in the uploaded GEDCOM. The cited census images or certified copies were not embedded in the GEDCOM and have not yet been visually inspected in the vault.

## Relevant citations

### 1930 census

GEDCOM citation:

- Frederick E. Selby: 1930 United States Federal Census, New Orleans, Orleans Parish, Louisiana, page 19A, enumeration district 0209, FHL microfilm 2340545.
- GEDCOM relationship field: `Son`.
- GEDCOM household context: James Benjamin Sanford Selby is recorded as head of household on the same census reference.

The 1930 federal schedule included a field recording each person's relationship to the head of the family.

### 1900 census

GEDCOM citation:

- James B. Selby: 1900 United States Federal Census, New Orleans Ward 13, Orleans Parish, Louisiana, page 3, enumeration district 0125, NARA microfilm publication T623, FHL microfilm 1240575.
- GEDCOM relationship field: `Son`.
- GEDCOM household context: Frederick Payne Selby is recorded as head on the same census reference.

The 1900 federal schedule recorded relationship to the head of household, month and year of birth, age, and parental birthplaces.

### 1880 census

GEDCOM citation:

- Frederick Payne Selby: 1880 United States Federal Census, Eminence, Henry County, Kentucky, roll 420, page 28C, enumeration district 092.
- GEDCOM relationship field: `Son`.
- GEDCOM household context: Robert Benjamin Selby is recorded as self/head on the same census reference.

The 1880 census was the first federal census to explicitly record each person's relationship to the head of the family.

### 1870 census

GEDCOM citation:

- Frederick Payne Selby and Robert Benjamin Selby: 1870 United States Federal Census, Port Royal, Henry County, Kentucky, roll M593_470, page 374A, FHL film 545969.

The 1870 schedule did not record relationship to the household head. Co-residence therefore supports identity correlation but does not by itself prove parentage.

## Assessment

The 1930, 1900, and 1880 citations are potentially direct evidence of the stated parent-child relationships because those census schedules contained relationship-to-head fields. Their evidential weight remains limited until the actual images are inspected for names, ages, household sequence, handwriting, enumeration errors, and possible step- or adopted relationships.

The GEDCOM itself is derivative evidence. A transcription field such as `Son` should not be treated as equivalent to an inspected census image.
