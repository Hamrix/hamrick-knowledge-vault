# Lincolnshire final concord CP 25-1-128-15 no 8 - 1220

## Citation

Final concord, Lincolnshire, quindene of St Hilary, 4 Henry III, modern date 27 January 1220, The National Archives, CP 25/1/128/15, no. 8; digital image, Anglo-American Legal Tradition. Published abstract: W. Boyd and W. O. Massingberd, *Abstracts of Final Concords, Lincoln, 1216-1272*, Lincoln Record Society 1 (1896), printed page 156, no. 8a.

## Description

Contemporary chirograph recording the settlement of a Lincolnshire dower plea, reviewed alongside the 1896 record-society abstract.

## Direct evidence

The original image and the published abstract name Nicholas de Schauencurt and Sibilla his wife in a plea with Andrew Wacelin over one third of a bovate in Gousle. Sibilla claimed the land as dower from the free tenement of Walter de Ver, formerly her husband. Andrew acknowledged the land as Sibilla's right, subject to an annual rent of eight pence.

The original Latin image visibly contains the relationship-bearing names and the phrase identifying Walter de Ver as Sibilla's former husband. The 1896 abstract supplies a controlled English rendering. No complete new diplomatic transcription was prepared in this project.

## Reliability and limitations

This is direct, contemporary relationship evidence for a Sibilla who was formerly wife of Walter de Ver and was wife of Nicholas de Schauencurt by January 1220. The record calls her Sibilla; it does **not** style her `de Kyme`, identify her parents, or name any child.

The 1904 Massingberd article cites this entry to page 150. Visual review of the 1896 volume shows that the entry is actually on printed page 156, no. 8a. The index also directs Schauencurt to page 156.

## Claims supported

- [[Sibilla wife of Nicholas de Schauencurt was formerly wife of Walter de Ver]]
- [[Walter de Ver was husband of Sibilla de Kyme]] - supports the Walter-Sibilla marriage core, but not the `de Kyme` identification.

## Claims contradicted

- Corrects the 1904 article's page-150 locator; the published abstract appears at page 156.
- Does not support treating `de Kyme` as wording found in this concord.
