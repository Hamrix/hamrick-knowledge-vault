# Headen family Bible transcript and Selby family notes - 1929

## Citation

Charles L. Stewart, editor, *Selby Family Notes: Some Materials for the 1929 Reunion of Families Having Members Descended from Martha Damarias Selby Brooks and from Josias Wright Brooks* (Bement, Illinois: distributed by Selby Anthony Clark, August 1929), 18-page FamilySearch PDF `G006169.pdf`. A user-supplied copy is preserved as [[20 Knowledge/Sources/Attachments/selbyfamilynotes (2).pdf]].

## Record examined

The complete PDF and the visually important pages were reviewed. The derivative publication reports that William Atkinson Selby married Sarah White Townsend on 28 February 1782. It separately labels his claimed Yorkshire birth about 1758 and exact 2 August 1809 Worcester County death as tradition. Printed page 11 transcribes entries attributed to the Headen family Bible, including the 1782 marriage and a child list. The original Bible itself is not reproduced.

## Migration-era research leads

The publication supplies the following leads for future record searches. They are statements in a 1929 compilation, not independently verified facts:

- Printed page 7 discusses early Selbys around Snow Hill and Pitt's Landing on the Maryland Eastern Shore and reports that a son of a Worcester County John Selby moved to New York and became a sea captain. The text does not establish that this John family was related to William Atkinson Selby.
- Printed page 9 reports William Atkinson Selby's birth in Yorkshire about 1758 expressly "by Tradition" and places him near Snow Hill during the Revolution. It gives no passenger list, naturalization, church, land-entry, or parent record.
- Printed page 10 preserves a family recollection of the Selbys traveling from Maryland to Kentucky and burying two children in the wilderness. It gives no date, route, names, or contemporary source.
- Printed pages 9 and 11 list William and Sarah's reported children and dates. These names can be tested in Worcester County records and then traced through land, tax, probate, church, militia, and migration records without assuming the whole family list is correct.

## Reliability and limitations

This is a 1929 derivative compilation containing family testimony, a DAR application, correspondence, historical excerpts, and a typed Bible transcript. Those components are not independent corroboration of one another when they repeat the same family account. The original Bible, its title page, binding, handwriting, custody, and full family-register context have not been inspected. The reported marriage is sourced but unverified; the Yorkshire origin, immigration implication, and death details remain tradition or research leads rather than proof. The English heraldic and Biddleston material does not connect William or any American Selby to that family.

## Biddlesden comparison lead

The 1904 [[The Genealogist Volume 20 - 1904]] reports on printed page 254 that an Elizabeth, wife of John Lawson, was daughter of Thomas William Selby, Esq., of Biddlesden. This is a specific English lead, but the later pedigree gives no bridge to William Atkinson Selby or Maryland. The similar Biddleston or Biddlesden context in this 1929 booklet does not make the two derivative publications independent proof of an American-to-English connection.
