## Records examined

The 1832 bond states that Henry J. Porter intended to marry "Miss Maria Selby, daughter of Mr. Henry Selby of Nicholas County." Henry Selby appears as an obligor and signed by mark.

The 13 January 1842 bond states that John DeWitt intended to marry "Miss Catharine Selby, daughter of Mr. Henry Selby of Nicholas County." Henry Selby signed the bond by mark as DeWitt's co-obligor. The FamilySearch event index gives 12 January 1842; the original bond is dated the following day, and both dates are retained rather than silently reconciled.

The 13 May 1845 bond states that John Stitt intended to marry "Miss Juliann Selby, daughter of Mr. Henry Selby of Nicholas County." Parker Selby signed as Stitt's co-obligor. The instrument does not state Parker's relationship to Juliann, Henry, or Stitt.

The 1849 certificate authorizes the marriage of Solomon Green and "Miss Dulcina Selby, daughter of Henry Selby, resident of this county." It is dated 22 September 1849.

## Genealogical value

The four originals directly identify Maria, Catharine, Juliann, and Dulcina as daughters of a Nicholas County Henry Selby and extend his documented family chronology from 1832 through 1849. Parker's signature on Juliann's 1845 bond places him in the same immediate marriage network seven years after Henry and Eleanor's deed to Parker, but neither record defines his kinship.

Margaret Selby's 1836 will in [[FamilySearch - Margaret Selby 1836 will and estate settlements]] independently names Kitty and Duland as her granddaughters and daughters of Henry Selby. Kitty is a strong familiar-form match for Catharine, and Duland is strongly compatible with Dulcina. Correlated with Major's will and Henry's participation in Margaret's probate, this strongly identifies their father as Major's son. The records do not name the daughters' mother, and the name matches remain correlations rather than literal spelling matches.
