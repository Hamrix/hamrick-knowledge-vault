## Records examined

The original manuscript images and their automated transcripts were reviewed. On 5 February 1821, the county court granted Benjamin Selby administration of Lingan Wilson Selby's estate after Benjamin took the required oath and executed a $3,000 bond with William Owens as security. The court appointed Elsey Creel, Lewis Lampton, John Anderson, and Benjamin Lampton, or any three of them, to appraise Lingan's enslaved people and personal estate.

On 2 July 1821, the court received the inventory and appraisement and ordered it recorded. A separate order that day directly identifies Mary Selby as the widow and relict of Lingan W. Selby and appoints John Anderson, Lewis Lampton, Elsey Creel, and Benjamin Lampton, or any three of them, to allot and assign her share and report to the court.

## Relationship analysis

The February order proves that Benjamin Selby administered Lingan's estate. It does not call Benjamin a son, heir, child, or other relative. Appointment as administrator is compatible with kinship but does not establish it. The July order directly proves Mary Selby's spousal relationship to Lingan but names no child or heir.

## Bounded negative search

Court Order Books D-E, images 151-546, were reviewed sequentially with the image-level transcript panel, covering January 1821 through the end of the filmed 1833 volume. Search variants included `Lingan`, `Ligan`, `Kingan`, `Selby`, and `Selly`. The Lingan-specific hits were the February administration order and the July inventory and widow orders. No later Lingan sale, administrator settlement, distribution, child, or heir entry surfaced in that transcript-assisted sweep. Benjamin appears repeatedly in unrelated civic, legal, and probate matters.

Automated transcription is incomplete and sometimes duplicates or misreads pages. The negative result is therefore a documented search boundary, not proof that no additional record exists. Estate bonds, loose papers, tax records, deeds, or an untranscribed entry may still identify the heirs.

A FamilySearch Catalog place-and-keyword search for Adair County and `administrator bonds` returned no catalog result. Broadening the keyword to `bonds` returned four guardianship or vital-record entries but no separate probate-bond series. This catalog result does not establish that the bond was destroyed or never retained; it redirects the request to the Adair County Clerk, Kentucky Department for Libraries and Archives, or an unlisted loose-record collection.
