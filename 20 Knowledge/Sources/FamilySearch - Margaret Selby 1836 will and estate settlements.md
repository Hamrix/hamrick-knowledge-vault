## Major Selby settlement

The 1835 settlement names Charles Parker as executor of Major Selby deceased. It includes cash paid "for dress for Mrs Margaret Selby" and leaves a balance of about $203.30 in the executor's hands. The settlement was confirmed at the May 1835 court term.

## Margaret Selby's will

Margaret Selby made her will on 24 November 1836. She directly identified:

- her deceased husband, Major Selby;
- "my brother Charles Parker," who was executor of her husband Major Selby;
- her daughter Mary Antrobus;
- her grandson William Preston Antrobus;
- her granddaughters Kitty and Duland Selby, daughters of Henry Selby;
- $203.33 in Charles Parker's hands and $100 in Robert Parker's hands; and
- Charles Parker and Robert Parker as her executors.

At the March 1838 probate hearing, the court recorded that witness William Parker was outside the Commonwealth and that Henry Selby opposed establishment of the will. After hearing evidence, the court established the will and ordered it recorded.

The will directly proves Margaret's relationship to Major, Charles, Mary, William Preston, Kitty, and Duland in the degrees stated. It does not call Henry her son or identify the girls' mother. It therefore does not by itself prove that Margaret was Henry's biological mother.

Major's 1833 will calls his daughter "Polly Anterbus," while Margaret's will calls her daughter "Mary Antrobus." The same surname, marriage context, and family structure strongly support one woman, and Polly is a conventional nickname for Mary, but the records use different given names and no single instrument explicitly says Mary was formerly Polly Selby.

## Margaret Selby estate settlement

Robert Parker's 2 October 1839 executor settlement includes the $203.33 balance described in Margaret's will and was confirmed at the February 1840 term. It confirms continuity of the estate administration but names no additional heirs.

## Genealogical value

When correlated with Major's will naming son Henry and daughter Polly, the 1825 Antrobus marriage file, and later marriage records naming Catharine and Dulcina as daughters of Henry, Margaret's will is the strongest relationship-bearing bridge yet found for the Nicholas County family. Kitty is a strong familiar-form match for Catharine, and Duland is a strong scribal or familiar-form match for Dulcina. Those name correlations are strong rather than literal spelling matches.
