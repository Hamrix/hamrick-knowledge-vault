## Records examined

The 1816 Buckner deed conveys Barkley Selby 116 acres, part of a larger tract patented to Samuel Haws of Virginia, on the waters of Brushy Fork of Hinkston Fork of Licking in Nicholas County. The deed states that Barkley assumed the title risk beyond claims through Buckner and Samuel Haws.

On 26 June 1817 Barkley Selby of Nicholas County conveyed 18.5 surveyed acres to Elijah C. Dazey for $129.50. The land was part of a larger Samuel Haws patent and began on the division line between Selby's and Dazey's land near the road from George Barnett's mill to William Holladay's tavern. Barkley signed, and Patsey Selby signed and relinquished her interest after private examination; the clerk calls her Barkley's wife.

In October 1820 Humphrey Marshall, William Gunnell, and Thomas A. Marshall conveyed or confirmed title to Barkley for the same 116-acre Haws-survey boundary on Brushy Fork. The deed excepted the 18.5 acres Barkley had already conveyed to Elijah Dazey and described the remaining 97.5 acres as in Barkley's possession.

## Genealogical value

The sequence directly identifies Barkley Selby as a Nicholas County landholder from April 1816 through October 1820 and identifies Patsey as his wife in June 1817. It also places Barkley's tract in the same Samuel Haws and Brushy Fork land system in which Thomas Rule acquired land in 1817. Rule had furnished Major Selby's deed for the 1815 Bourbon survey.

This supplies a new collateral Selby presence inside the land network being tested for the Bourbon-to-Nicholas transition. Barkley's relationship to Major Selby is not stated.

## Reliability and limitations

The parties, spousal acknowledgment, tract size, patent source, waterways, and title sequence appear in the original recorded deeds. FamilySearch OCR was used as a locator and checked against the images; spelling variants such as Hawes/Haws and Gunnell/Gunnett are retained as record variants where relevant.

The deeds do not name Major Selby, identify Barkley's parents or children, or state that Barkley and Major were related. Shared surname, county, and survey network are cluster evidence only. Patsey should not be conflated with Major's wife Peggy without a direct record.

The continuation in [[FamilySearch - Barkley Selby Nicholas deeds 1824 and 1830]] traces part of this title into an 1824 Dyer sale and identifies a wife as Milly in 1824 and Martha in 1830. Those later names do not alter the direct 1817 Patsey reading; they create an identity question that must remain visible.
