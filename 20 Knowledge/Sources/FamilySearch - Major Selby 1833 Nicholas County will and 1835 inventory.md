# FamilySearch - Major Selby 1833 Nicholas County will and 1835 inventory

## Citation

Nicholas County, Kentucky, will books 1800-1806, 1816-1829, and 1830-1839; Kentucky County Court (Nicholas County); FamilySearch image group 007553105; Major Selby will dated 4 January 1833 and proved August term 1833, [image 639 of 835](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L9DL-NLG3?view=fullText&keywords=Major+Selby&lang=en); inventory returned February term 1835, [image 702 of 835](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QSQ-G9DL-NGC5?view=fullText&keywords=Major+Selby&lang=en); accessed 9 August 2026.

## Record examined

Major Selby, describing himself as of Nicholas County, made his will on 4 January 1833. He:

- devised his property to his **beloved wife Peggy Selby**;
- gave one dollar to **his son Henry Selby**, in addition to what Henry had already received;
- gave one dollar to **his daughter Polly Anterbus, formerly Polly Selby**, in addition to what Polly had already received; and
- appointed Charles Parker executor.

Major signed by mark. John Throckmorton and Thomas R. Throckmorton witnessed the will. Nicholas County Court proved and ordered the will recorded at its August 1833 term.

A later inventory names Charles Parker as executor of Major Selby deceased. It lists cash, a brown mare, and cattle and was returned to court at the February 1835 term.

## Reliability and limitations

The will is direct primary evidence for the stated spouse and parent-child relationships and for Major's death by the August 1833 probate term. Its identification of Polly as formerly a Selby independently corroborates the 1825 marriage authorization naming Major as Polly's father.

The will does not name Major's parents, Peggy's maiden surname, the spouses of Henry or Polly, or every possible child. It does not name Benjamin Selby. Omission from the will is negative evidence, not proof that Benjamin was not Major's son. The will also does not identify the testator with the 1807 Bourbon plaintiff, 1811 Bourbon taxpayer/purchaser, or 1820 Nicholas household by an explicit cross-reference.

Margaret Selby's 1836 will directly calls Major her deceased husband, calls Charles Parker her brother and Major's executor, and identifies Kitty and Duland Selby as her granddaughters and daughters of Henry Selby. The probate order records Henry Selby's opposition to establishment of her will. Correlated with the daughter marriage records and Henry's local land chronology, this strongly identifies the later Nicholas County Henry as Major's son. No later instrument literally calls him "Henry Selby, son of Major Selby," so the cross-record identity remains strong rather than confirmed. Margaret's will also does not call Henry her son or name the girls' mother.
