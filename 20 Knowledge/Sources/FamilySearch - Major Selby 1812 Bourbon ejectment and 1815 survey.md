# FamilySearch - Major Selby 1812 Bourbon ejectment and 1815 survey

## Citation

Bourbon County, Kentucky, deed-book record of an ejectment declaration and service, 1812, [FamilySearch image 703 of 1,119](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QHV-N3ZB-5YN9?view=fullText&keywords=Major+Selby&lang=en); survey and plat in the same land dispute, 1 August 1815, [FamilySearch image 705 of 1,119](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QHV-N3ZB-5YQR?view=fullText&keywords=Major+Selby&lang=en); accessed 8 August 2026.

## Records examined

The 1812 record gives notice to tenants in possession including Valentine Cress, Andrew Ervin, Ann Dudley, Armiger Lilly, **Major Selby**, Henry Selby, Sarah Henderson, John Rule, William Kinnett, and William Johnson. It records service on Major and the other occupants on 3 November 1812 and their admission as defendants in place of the casual ejector.

The 1 August 1815 survey and plat labels **Major Selby's house & Improvement**. The accompanying survey text also refers to a **Major Selby deed** furnished by Thomas Rule and says it gives no boundary.

## Reliability and limitations

The dated originals directly establish Major's possession-related participation in the Bourbon land dispute in 1812 and locate a house and improvement bearing his name in the 1815 survey. The shared Dudley, Lilly, and Rule associates connect this evidence contextually to the 1811 land transactions.

Nicholas County Deed Book E adds a later geographic and associate link. In 1817 Thomas Rule of Bourbon County acquired land in Haw's survey on Hinkston and Brushy Fork in Nicholas County, and a second Haws-survey tract was bounded by Valentine Cress. Rule furnished Major's deed in this survey, while Cress was served alongside Major in 1812. See [[FamilySearch - Thomas Rule 1817 Nicholas County Haws survey deeds]].

The records do not name Major's parents, spouse, children, former residence, or later county. The 1817 deeds do not name Major and cannot establish that he moved to Nicholas County. Henry Selby's nearby appearance does not establish that he was Major's son; no relationship is stated, and the name must remain unassigned.
