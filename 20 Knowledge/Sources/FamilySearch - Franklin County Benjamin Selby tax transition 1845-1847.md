# FamilySearch - Franklin County Benjamin Selby tax transition 1845-1847

## Citation

Franklin County, Kentucky, tax books, 1837-1851, FamilySearch Image Group Number `8188355`; original images 478, 491, 545-548, 553, 583, and 596, accessed 20 August 2026.

## Original entries reviewed

The original 1845 surname page, image 478, separately lists **Selby Benj. Jr** and **Selby Benj. Senr**. Two following ditto entries concern property entered for Mary Selby's abbreviated `h[ei]rs` and for Jas. and Jn. McLain, but the schedule does not state how any of those people were related. Image 491 is the northern-district certification. It identifies the list as the 1845 tax list and is dated 21 May 1845.

The 1846 list is bounded by its 18 May 1846 certification at image 553. The original S-surname pages at images 545-548 were manually reviewed from Stephens and Smith through Scofield and Snapp and then into Taylor. No Selby or Shelby entry appears on those pages. The five exact `Selby` full-text hits for the complete image group occur outside this 1846 image interval.

The original 1847 surname page, image 583, lists one **Selby Benjamin**, without a senior or junior suffix. The row contains one mark in the column for white males over twenty-one years of age and a total value of $300. Image 596 identifies the surrounding list as 1847.

## Analysis

This sequence is consistent with two separately identified Benjamin Selbys in Franklin County in the 1845 tax list, neither surname entry in the manually reviewed 1846 S section, and one adult Benjamin in 1847. The younger Benjamin was born 31 January 1826 and had reached age twenty-one by the 1847 list, so the unsuffixed 1847 entry is compatible with him.

The transition independently supports the reported 1845 death window for the older Franklin County Benjamin and the existence of distinct older and younger men. It does **not** record a death, identify the 1847 man, or state that the two 1845 men were father and son. Annual tax omission is not proof of death, and the 1845 certification date is not necessarily the date on which either individual entry was taken.

## Reliability and limitations

The positive names and suffixes were visually read from original tax images. Year assignments are controlled by original certifications or recapitulations rather than FamilySearch's OCR year filter. The 1846 negative is bounded to the manually reviewed S-surname pages and the mapped exact-name hits; it does not prove that no variant or misplaced entry exists elsewhere in the volume.

The Mary Selby notation appears to abbreviate heirs, but neither Mary nor a relationship to either Benjamin is identified. It cannot be used as heir-level evidence for Hon. Benjamin, Lingan Wilson Selby, or any proposed parent.
