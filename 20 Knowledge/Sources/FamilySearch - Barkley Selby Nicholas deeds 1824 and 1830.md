## Records examined

On 2 August 1824 Bartley Selby and **Milly his wife**, of Nicholas County, conveyed William Dyer 53 acres on the waters of Brushy Fork for one dollar. The deed says the parcel was part of the same tract previously conveyed to Selby through the Humphrey Marshall title sequence and refers to the September and October 1820 instruments. That recital directly continues the title history preserved in [[FamilySearch - Barkley and Patsey Selby Nicholas Haws survey deeds 1816-1820]].

On 23 September 1830 William Royse and wife Catharine conveyed Bartley Selby a small adjoining Brushy Fork tract for $20. The bounds ran from Selby's line to the creek and William Victor's line.

The same day Bartley conveyed William Royse one acre on the waters of Bushy Fork of Hinkston for $20. Its bounds used the Millersburg-Carlisle road, Royse and Selby lines, and another road near William Holladay's tavern.

Also on 23 September 1830 Bartley Selby and **Martha his wife** conveyed Daniel and Hugh J. McClintock 92 acres, 3 roods, and 27 poles. The Brushy Fork boundary included William Dyer and Mrs. Hughes lines. Bartley said his title came from Humphrey Marshall and assigned the McClintocks his purchase-money lien remedy if a better title defeated the conveyance. Martha relinquished dower after private examination.

## Genealogical value

These originals extend Barkley's documented Nicholas County Brushy Fork land sequence from 1816 through 1830. The 1824 title recital directly joins the Dyer sale to the previously reviewed 1820 Marshall title-confirmation deed. The 1830 deeds identify Martha as Bartley's wife and place Barkley, Royse, Dyer, Victor, Holladay, Hughes, and the McClintocks in the same land neighborhood.

The 1817 original calls Barkley's wife **Patsey**, while the 1830 originals call her **Martha**. Patsey is a historically common nickname for Martha, making one woman a strong interpretation, but none of these deeds explicitly says that Patsey and Martha were the same person. The 1824 deed instead calls the wife **Milly**, which remains an unresolved name discrepancy.

## Reliability and limitations

The parties, spouse names, dates, acreage, waterways, roads, neighbors, and title recitals appear in the original recorded deeds. FamilySearch handwriting recognition helped locate the 1830 entries and was checked against the image and deed index.

The deeds do not name Major Selby, identify Barkley's or either wife's parents, name children, or state any relationship between Barkley and Major. Shared surname, county, and Brushy Fork land system remain collateral evidence only. The Patsey-Martha correlation should not be promoted to confirmed identity until another record joins the names or supplies a stable full identity.
