Research use:

- determine whether the spelling is actually Solbie, Selbie, or another surname;
- do not assume that this transported person was the later Thomas Selby of Maryland without continuity evidence;
- do not infer indentured-servant status merely from appearance in a headright list.

# Parker gateway hypothesis

The transcript proposes a royal descent running approximately:

Edward III → John of Gaunt → Joan Beaufort and Ralph Neville → Alice Neville and Henry FitzHugh → Elizabeth FitzHugh and William Parr → Alice Parr and Thomas Vaux → Nicholas Vaux → Alice Vaux and Richard Sapcote → Anne Sapcote and Henry Parker, Baron Morley → later Barons Morley and Lord Monteagle → an unidentified gentry branch → George Parker of Southampton → George Parker of Accomack.

The medieval noble segment may be independently documentable, but the key colonial bridge is hidden inside the phrase "gentry branch." Until the generations from the baronial Parkers to the Accomack immigrant are named and sourced, the route is not proved.

Further caution:

- do not confuse the medieval Barons Morley with the later Earls of Morley of Saltram;
- do not assume every Accomack Parker belonged to the same English Parker house;
- prove the exact Parker-to-Selby intermarriage through which the user's line would inherit the proposed descent.

# Schoolfield or Scholefield gateway hypothesis

The transcript proposes that Margaret Schoolfield may descend from the armigerous Scholefields of Rochdale, Lancashire, associated with Schofield Hall and recorded in Lancashire visitations.

Potentially useful leads:

Major limitation:

A rare or habitational surname does not prove descent from the armigerous manor family. The colonial Margaret must be connected to identified parents and then to a specific English pedigree.

# Overall evidentiary assessment

The transcript supports a reasonable research strategy but repeatedly overstates conclusions. Its most useful contribution is the identification of multiple independent avenues:

1. direct paternal Selby origin;
2. Schoolfield or Scholefield collateral ancestry;
3. Parker collateral ancestry;
4. Atkinson, Laws, Townsend, and other Eastern Shore maternal lines.

The cumulative presence of several colonial gentry networks makes eventual royal descent plausible, but probability cannot substitute for missing generations. A complete royal-line claim must be built from atomic, independently supported parent-child relationships.
