## Record examined

The packet identifies **Major Selby** as plaintiff and **William Gray** as defendant. The petition states that Major held Gray's note for five pounds, given for value received, and sought the debt, damages, and costs after it remained unpaid. The summons directed the Bourbon County sheriff to bring Gray before the Bourbon Circuit Court to answer Major's petition and was issued at Paris on 5 August 1807. A packet endorsement places the matter in the August 1807 term and notes dismissal.

## Genealogical value

This is the earliest reviewed primary record found so far that places an adult Major Selby in a Kentucky legal proceeding. It moves the Kentucky anchor from the 1811 Bourbon deed back to 1807 and reduces the documentary gap after the February 1800 Worcester guardian account to seven years.

## Reliability and limitations

The original court papers directly establish the named plaintiff and the Bourbon County proceeding. They do not state Major's age, birthplace, residence, parents, spouse, children, land, or previous location.

The shared rare name, adult status, county, and close chronology make this man a strong identity candidate for the Major Selby described as of Bourbon County in the 1811 deed. The case does not, by itself, identify him with the Worcester ward last recorded in 1800 or with the separate adult assessed in Worcester in 1783.
