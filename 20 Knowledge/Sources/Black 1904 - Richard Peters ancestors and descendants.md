# Black 1904 - Richard Peters ancestors and descendants

## Citation

Nellie Peters Black, editor and compiler, *Richard Peters, His Ancestors and Descendants, 1810-1889* (Atlanta: Foote & Davies Company, 1904). The title page was visually reviewed at scan page 7. A user-supplied copy is preserved as [[20 Knowledge/Sources/Attachments/richardpetershis00blac.pdf]].

## Description

This 1904 family history combines Richard Peters's recollections, family letters, published works, an 1899 commissioned English search, and information supplied after the volume was otherwise prepared. It describes a Liverpool, Pennsylvania, and Georgia Peters family. It is a derivative compilation, not a primary record for the relationships it reports.

## Relevant extracts

- Printed page 60 describes the Belmont arms as a bend between two escallops with two cinquefoils and gives the motto "Sans Dieu Rien." The author wonders whether a cinquefoil similarity implied an older family connection, but supplies no pedigree for that suggestion.
- Printed pages 62-63 summarize an 1899 English search by R. A. Grant. The report says reliable information about William Peters was scarce, then introduces a passage attributed to Sir Bernard Burke.
- Printed page 64 reproduces the statement that the English Peters family "claims to be a branch of the noble house of Lord Petrie" and says the coat of arms and motto are exactly the same. The source spells the title **Petrie**; this note does not silently change it to Petre.
- Printed page 66 says the American branch descended from William Peters's 1741 marriage to Mary Brientnall. This is a family-history assertion, not a relationship proved by an original record in the book.
- An unnumbered post-publication supplement at scan page 261 says Matthew Peters was Ralph Peters's father and names William's first wife as Elizabeth Bayley. The formal genealogy at printed page 116, scan page 265, instead says William's first wife's name was unknown to the editor. The two statements conflict within the same volume.
- Printed pages 116-118 provide a compiled Liverpool-to-Pennsylvania Peters genealogy, including Ralph Peters, William Peters, Rev. Richard Peters, Judge Richard Peters, and later descendants.

## Full-text negative search

A complete text extraction of all 334 scan pages was searched case-insensitively on 20 August 2026. It produced no occurrence of `Etheldred`, `Ethelred`, `Stafford`, `Chaney`, or `Cheney`. This is a negative search of the supplied scan, not proof that no relationship existed.

## Reliability and limitations

The book confirms that a noble-house tradition and heraldic comparison were published in 1904. It does not document a generation-by-generation descent from the Peters family to a Petre or "Petrie" family. Similar arms or a shared motto do not establish biological descent, and the book gives no heraldic grant, visitation pedigree, probate chain, or other primary relationship sequence for that claim.

The volume also does not mention the Craven County man Etheldred Peters, Elizabeth Chaney or Cheney, or any Stafford. It therefore cannot support William Peters as Etheldred's father, Etheldred as Elizabeth Peters's father, or a Peters-to-Stafford bridge. Its internal disagreement over William's first wife's identity further requires verification from original records before that marriage is entered as fact.
