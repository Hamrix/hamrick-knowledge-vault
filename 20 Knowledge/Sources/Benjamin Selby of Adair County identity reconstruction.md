# Benjamin Selby of Adair County identity reconstruction

## Scope

This note reconstructs the historically evidenced profile of the Benjamin Selby associated with early nineteenth-century Adair County, Kentucky, without assuming the rejected Burridge branch or importing unsupported GEDCOM details.

## Correlated Adair County evidence

### Land and legal activity

Adair County deed-index transcriptions record Benjamin Selby repeatedly during 1811-1818:

- several indentures involving Benjamin Selby;
- an indenture indexed to `Benjamin & Betsy` Selby;
- a power of attorney involving Benjamin Selby.

These entries establish an adult Benjamin Selby engaged in property and legal transactions in Adair County. The `Benjamin & Betsy` index is evidence that a woman called Betsy was associated with him in at least one deed, but the index alone does not establish her maiden surname.

### County office and civic activity

Published Adair County historical transcriptions report that:

- Benjamin Selby served as a deputy under Sheriff Robert Thomas beginning in 1812;
- Benjamin Selby was a member of Columbia's first fire company organized in February 1822;
- the Adair County Court appointed Benjamin Selby, John Montgomery, and William Caldwell as commissioners in 1823 to convey title after Matthew McDowell's death.

These records describe a settled adult participant in Adair County civic and legal affairs between 1812 and 1823.

### Marriage and community evidence

An Adair County marriage transcription records Benjamin Selby as an attesting witness to the 1820 marriage of Roderick Clark and Elizabeth Naylor.

The uploaded GEDCOM separately cites a derivative marriage index for Benjamin Selby and `Elizab? McClane` in Adair County in 1811. The indexed marriage is a useful lead, but the actual marriage record has not been inspected in the vault, and the spelling or maiden surname must not be normalized beyond the record.

### Census ambiguity

The published 1820 Adair County census index points to the surname `SELBY` on page 46. The transcription on that page identifies `Green Selby`; it also contains a separate household transcribed as `SEBTLER, Ben`. The current audit cannot safely reinterpret that second surname as Selby without inspection of the original census image.

No exact Benjamin Selby entry was located in the currently inspected published transcriptions of the 1810, 1830, or 1850 Adair County censuses. This absence does not prove that he was outside the county, because transcription, spelling, enumeration, and survival problems remain possible.

## GEDCOM assertions not established by the cited evidence

The uploaded GEDCOM assigns the following details to its Benjamin Selby profile:

- suffix `Jr.`;
- birth in 1779 in Maryland;
- death on 10 June 1856 in Kentucky;
- parents from the now-rejected Burridge branch;
- son Robert Benjamin Selby.

The GEDCOM's attached sources do not establish the suffix, exact birth, exact death, parents, or parent-child relationship to Robert Benjamin Selby. Its family-level marriage citation supports only a possible Benjamin-Elizab? McClane marriage in 1811, not the remainder of the profile.

## Duplicate-identity risk

The GEDCOM contains several overlapping Benjamin Selby profiles with different dates and locations, including Benjamin M. Selby, Benjamin Selby born about 1756, Benjamin Selby born 1779, Benjamin Martin Sr. Selby, and another Benjamin Martin Selby. This creates a substantial risk that names, dates, spouses, parents, and events were merged across different men.

## Current conclusion

A Benjamin Selby was demonstrably active in Adair County, Kentucky, during the 1811-1823 period. His exact birth, death, suffix, wife’s full identity, parents, and relationship to Robert Benjamin Selby remain unresolved.

The evidence-based profile should therefore be described as:

> Benjamin Selby, adult resident or landholder associated with Adair County by 1811 and active in county affairs through at least 1823; vital dates and parentage unresolved.
