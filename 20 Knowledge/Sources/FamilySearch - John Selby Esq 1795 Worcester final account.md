# FamilySearch - John Selby Esq 1795 Worcester final account

## Citation

Worcester County, Maryland, final account of James Selby and John Gunby, administrators with a copy of the will annexed of John Selby Esq., passed 10 March 1795, *Accounts 1792-1796*, manuscript pages 218-219; FamilySearch image group 005080358, [image `33SQ-GTBV-9T7X`](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:33SQ-GTBV-9T7X?wc=SNY4-T3D%3A146534501%2C146537701%26cc%3D1803986&cc=1803986&lang=en&i=116&groupId=1803986), accessed 8 August 2026.

## Record examined

The heading identifies the accountants as **James Selby and John Gunby**, administrators of the estate of **John Selby Esq.**, with a copy of the deceased's will annexed. The disbursements include:

- payment to **Ann Selby, the deceased's widow**, for her third of the estate;
- payment to **Sarah Selby, daughter of the deceased's son Joshua**, for her legacy;
- payment to **Daniel Selby** for his legacy;
- payment to **William Selby, son of the deceased**, for his legacy;
- payment to a William Selby who intermarried with the deceased's daughter **Betsey Selby**, for Betsey's legacy;
- payment to **"John Selby son of Parker guardian to Major Selby Orphan of John Selby"** for Major's legacy, which the account says was **"left him by his Grandfather's will"**;
- retained legacies for the deceased's grandchildren Betsey, John, and George, children of Parker; and
- retained legacies for Parker, John, and Nancy, children of James, which the account describes as left by their grandfather.

James Selby and John Gunby swore to the final account on 10 March 1795.

## Genealogical value

The Major entry is direct relationship evidence in the elder John Selby's own estate. It identifies Major as:

1. the orphan of a deceased John Selby;
2. a beneficiary under John Selby Esq.'s will; and
3. the grandson of John Selby Esq.

It also identifies Major's guardian in 1795 as John Selby, son of Parker. This both confirms the 1790 will's `Major Selby son of John` clause and explains the guardian's place in the elder Selby family network.

## Reliability and limitations

This is a primary recorded final account made by the estate administrators and accepted under oath. It directly records the relationships used to distribute the estate.

The account proves the colonial minor Major's father and grandfather. It does not identify that minor with the adult Major Selby assessed in Worcester County in 1783 or, by itself, with the later Major Selby documented in Kentucky. That identity bridge remains a separate problem.
