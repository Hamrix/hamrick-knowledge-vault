# Maryland State Archives - Charles Parker 1762 Worcester administration account

## Record examined

The accounts index identifies the target as Charles Parker, 1762, folio 128, Liber 48, Worcester County. Direct review of Liber 48 folios 128-129 shows:

- the heading describes the account of **Collins Parker, administrator**, of the goods, chattels, rights, and credits of **Charles Parker, late of Worcester County, deceased**;
- the administrator charges himself with the estate inventory admitted to the Prerogative Office;
- the account lists debts, fees, and administrative payments, including payments proved by several named creditors;
- after the listed payments, folio 129 carries a small balance in the administrator's favor; and
- the closing certification states that Collins Parker appeared on **21 May 1762**, swore the account was true to the best of his knowledge, and had it passed by Benton Harris, deputy commissary of Worcester County.

## Reliability and limitations

This is the contemporary recorded administration account and is strong evidence for the estate identity, Collins Parker's administrative role, and the filing date. It independently confirms that the estate was being administered through May 1762.

The account contains no distribution to heirs, no child list, and no statement defining Collins Parker's relationship to the decedent. It therefore does not prove Collins was a son, brother, or other specific relative. It also does not connect this 1761-1762 estate to the Charles Parker who made a will in 1787 or to the Kentucky Charles whom Margaret Selby called her brother.
