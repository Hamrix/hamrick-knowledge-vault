# Selby Family Notes — 1929 reunion compilation source audit

## Source identity

- Title: *Selby Family Notes*
- Subtitle/purpose: materials prepared for the 1929 reunion of families descended from Martha Damaris Selby Brooks and Josias Wright Brooks
- Editor: Charles L. Stewart
- Distributor: Selby Anthony Clark, 301 South Macon Street, Bement, Illinois
- Date: August 1929
- Format inspected: 18-page scanned PDF
- Supplied filename: `selbyfamilynotes.pdf`
- File size: 1,770,307 bytes
- SHA-256: `0690212592d12b114bf80a747e16732cfe6f0404140c0f1b85484fd5cb6dec15`

## Correct source classification

This is a **compiled family-history publication**, created for a 1929 reunion and incorporating:

- an older historical/genealogical narrative attributed to Thomas Henry Selby;
- correspondence with historical institutions;
- a DAR application;
- family letters and traditions;
- a transcription of a Selby–Headen family Bible;
- extracts from published historical and heraldic works.

It is valuable as a source-discovery document and as evidence of family tradition preserved by 1929. It is not an original seventeenth- or eighteenth-century record and cannot independently prove every lineage statement it repeats.

## Contents relevant to William Atkinson Selby

The table of contents includes dedicated sections titled:

- “The Selbys in America”;
- “William Atkinson Selby a Private in the Maryland Militia”;
- “Some Lines of Descent from William Atkinson Selby”;
- “Selby and Headen Family Record, 1782–1910.”

### Maryland militia evidence reported

The publication reproduces the substance of 1928–1929 correspondence from the Maryland Historical Society stating that a manuscript Revolutionary militia list, page 211, included **William Atkinson Selby** in Captain Quinton’s Company, Class 7, part of the Wicomico Battalion classed on 15 July 1780 in Worcester County, Maryland.

The publication concludes that the correspondence indicates William Atkinson Selby served as a private.

**Assessment:** This is a strong archival-reference lead because it identifies the repository, manuscript record type, page, company, class, battalion, county, and date. The underlying militia-list image or archive-certified transcription must still be obtained and inspected before the service is treated as independently verified in the vault.

### Marriage and family Bible evidence reported

The publication states that William Atkinson Selby married **Sarah White Townsend** on **28 February 1782**.

A 1929 transcription from the Bible of Mrs. Tryphena C. Selby Headen records:

- “Wm. A. Selby and Sarah W. Townsend were married February 28, 1782”;
- children including John, Barkley T., Sarah L., William, Joshua, Hasty, Rodah, and Polly Selby;
- “Wm. A. Selby departed this life August 2, 1809.”

The DAR application reproduced in the booklet likewise identifies Barkley Townsend Selby as a son of William Atkinson Selby and Sarah White Townsend.

**Assessment:** The Bible transcription is meaningful derivative evidence with stated provenance to a named owner and a transcription date. However, the original Bible pages, handwriting layers, publication history, and chain of custody should be inspected before the entries are treated as original family-record proof.

### Parentage limit explicitly preserved in the source

The reproduced DAR application states that William Atkinson Selby’s father’s name and mother’s name were unknown. The editor also says that William’s father may have been a John Selby but that this was not known.

This is important because the publication does **not** document William Atkinson Selby as a son of John Selby and Mary Atkinson. Any such parentage remains a later proof-path hypothesis requiring probate, deed, Bible, marriage, or other original evidence.

## English and medieval material

The booklet contains a broad narrative of English Selbys, Biddleston, Twizell, Ightham Mote, heraldry, crusading traditions, and medieval figures. It also speculates that the American family may have come from the English Selbys because of political troubles.

This material must be handled cautiously:

- much of it is copied from Burke, Fairbairn, historical compilations, or family tradition;
- the narrative does not provide a documented generation-by-generation bridge from the Maryland family to Northumberland or Kent;
- statements about a Norman/Gallic origin, arrival with William the Conqueror, destroyed title evidence, or political migration are speculative unless supported by separate primary evidence;
- the booklet cannot be used to attach William Atkinson Selby to Twizell Castle or a medieval northern pedigree.

## Conflicts and chronology cautions

The booklet contains internal uncertainty and some differing dates:

- William Atkinson Selby is variously described as born about 1758, while later derivative summaries may use 1757;
- the DAR text reports death in Worcester County, Maryland by tradition, while later trees may place death in Kentucky;
- the family Bible transcription gives death as 2 August 1809;
- the publication explicitly states that William’s parents were unknown;
- the English-to-American bridge is presented through inference and family tradition, not a documented chain.

These conflicts must be resolved from contemporaneous records rather than harmonized silently.

## Evidentiary conclusions

### Strong source leads

- William Atkinson Selby appears in a specifically identified 1780 Worcester County militia-list reference.
- William Atkinson Selby married Sarah White Townsend on 28 February 1782, supported by a reported family Bible transcription and repeated derivative material.
- Barkley Townsend Selby is presented as their son in the Bible/DAR-derived material.
- William Atkinson Selby’s death date is reported in the Bible transcription as 2 August 1809.

### Not established by this publication

- William Atkinson Selby’s exact birth date or birthplace.
- William Atkinson Selby’s exact death place.
- John Selby and Mary Atkinson as his documented parents.
- John Selby and Margaret as documented grandparents.
- Thomas Selby Jr. and Thomas Selby Sr. as a proven continuous ancestral chain to William.
- any connection from the Maryland family to Biddleston, Twizell, Ightham Mote, medieval Northumberland Selbys, the Norman Conquest, Stafford, Grey, de Lacy, Plantagenet, or Wessex.

## Required original-record follow-up

1. Obtain the Maryland Historical Society manuscript Revolutionary militia list, page 211, Captain Quinton’s Company, Class 7.
2. Locate the original or best surviving copy of the Tryphena C. Selby Headen Bible pages and document provenance.
3. Obtain the original Worcester County marriage entry for William Atkinson Selby and Sarah White Townsend, 28 February 1782.
4. Locate probate, land, tax, church, cemetery, or migration records proving William’s death place and Maryland-to-Kentucky continuity.
5. Search John Selby probate and land records for an explicit son William Atkinson Selby.
6. Keep all English and medieval Selby material separate until a documentary transatlantic bridge is established.

## Public-use wording

“*Selby Family Notes*, compiled for a 1929 family reunion, preserves valuable source leads for William Atkinson Selby, including a specific 1780 Maryland militia-list reference and a transcription of a family Bible recording his 1782 marriage to Sarah White Townsend and his 1809 death. The booklet is a compiled derivative source, however, and it explicitly leaves William’s parents unknown. Its broader English and medieval Selby narrative does not prove a connection between the Maryland family and the Northumberland or Twizell Selbys.”
