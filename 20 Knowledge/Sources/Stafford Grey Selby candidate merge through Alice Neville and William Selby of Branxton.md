# Stafford–Grey–Selby candidate merge through Alice Neville and William Selby of Branxton

## Purpose

This note preserves a user-supplied lead that the Stafford and Selby family networks may have merged through the Grey of Heton/Horton line: Margaret de Stafford → Alice Neville → Grey descendants → Elizabeth Grey → William Selby of Branxton.

This is **not yet a documented Stafford/Selby merger inside the vault**. It is a candidate bridge requiring source acquisition and correlation before it can be used in a lineage chart, public narrative, GEDCOM correction, or royal/noble descent claim.

## User-supplied proposition

The user supplied the following working structure on 2026-07-30:

1. Sir Thomas Grey III, born 30 November 1384 at or near Alnwick/Heton, Northumberland, was an English knight involved in the 1415 Southampton Plot and executed for treason at Southampton in August 1415.
2. Lady Alice Neville, born about 1384, was a daughter of Ralph Neville, 1st Earl of Westmorland, and Margaret de Stafford.
3. Alice Neville married Sir Thomas Grey III around 1401 and had issue including later Grey descendants.
4. A later Grey descendant, identified in derivative material as Elizabeth Grey, married William Selby of Branxton.
5. If this chain is proven, it may represent an early documented Stafford-adjacent merger into the Northumberland Selby family.

## Current evidence reviewed

### Alice Neville and Stafford descent

A Dictionary of National Biography profile for Ralph Neville states that Westmorland married first Margaret, daughter of Hugh, second Earl of Stafford, and lists Alice among the children of that first marriage. The same profile states that Alice married first Sir Thomas Grey of Heton and secondly Sir Gilbert Lancaster.

Status: **supported by published reference; should be checked against modern peerage/scholarly sources before final use**.

### Sir Thomas Grey of Heton

Published summaries and historical references identify Sir Thomas Grey of Heton/Heaton as one of the conspirators in the 1415 Southampton Plot and record his execution in August 1415.

Status: **historically supported for Thomas Grey’s identity and execution; not a Selby link by itself**.

### Grey to Selby bridge

A derivative genealogy source lists Elizabeth Grey as marrying William Selbye of Branxston. Stirnet’s Grey page reports this pairing and notes an Archaeologia Aeliana citation: Series 3, volume VI, 1910, “The Armorials of Northumberland,” page 185. A separate digitized pedigree of Selby of Ightham Mote begins with William Selby of Branxton, Northumberland, but the visible text does not fully resolve the Grey ancestry or the exact wife identity.

Status: **specific derivative lead; original/scholarly source not yet inspected**.

4. Distinguish this Northumberland/Border-gentry lead from the active **colonial Selby bridge acquisition docket**, which concerns John Cornelius, Thomas Selby Sr., Thomas Selby II, William Selby of Maryland, and later Maryland/Worcester Selby problems.

## Analysis

This lead is important because it could place Stafford blood into a Northumberland Selby family through a Grey intermediary earlier than later modern Stafford/Selby American connections. However, the crucial bridge is not the well-known Alice Neville marriage alone. The decisive bridge is whether a documented descendant or collateral member of the Grey of Heton/Horton line married a documented William Selby of Branxton, and whether that William belongs to the Selby line relevant to the project.

The user’s wording “earliest documented time the Stafford/Selby family merged” should not yet be used as public wording. The accurate vault wording is: **candidate earliest Stafford–Grey–Selby merge lead, pending original or scholarly confirmation of the Grey-to-Selby marriage and the relevant Selby line.**
