# Dumville 1979 - The ætheling

## Citation

David N. Dumville, “The ætheling: a study in Anglo-Saxon constitutional history,” *Anglo-Saxon England* 8 (1979): 1–33, https://doi.org/10.1017/S026367510000301X.

## Description

Peer-reviewed study of the position of the ætheling in Anglo-Saxon royal succession.

## Relevant extracts

- The article examines the term in the specific context of royal succession.
- Its analysis treats the ætheling as a prince of the royal house during the later Anglo-Saxon period, while examining changes and exceptions across the broader period.

## Reliability and limitations

This is a specialist scholarly article published by Cambridge University Press. It is strong secondary evidence for the historical meaning and constitutional setting of the title. It does not support any claimed line of descent to a modern family, and its conclusions should not be simplified into a rule of automatic primogeniture or guaranteed succession.
