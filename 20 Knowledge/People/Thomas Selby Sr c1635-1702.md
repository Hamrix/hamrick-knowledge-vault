# Thomas Selby Sr. (c. 1635–1702)

## Identity

Working profile for Thomas Selby Sr., born about 1635, associated first with Accomack and Northampton Counties, Virginia, and later with Worcester County, Maryland. He should not be conflated with the Thomas Selby whose will was proved in Worcester County in 1758. The direct relationship between these two men has not yet been documented in this vault.

## Family

- Married Mary, widow of John Cornelius, in Accomack County, Virginia, according to the cited land and probate abstracts.
- John Cornelius's 1656 will reportedly left his estate to his wife Mary; she subsequently married Thomas Selby.
- Thomas's 1701/02 will names a son Thomas as executor and heir after the widow's life estate. It also identifies several grandchildren, including Thomas Selby, Elizabeth Baker, Baker Clawell, Comfort Clawell, Rebecca Clawell, Selby Clawell/Clavett, John Onions, Selby Onions, Thomas Onions, and Jones Rickards.

## Timeline

- **1656:** John Cornelius's will leaves his estate to wife Mary.
- **1659/60:** Northampton County court ordered future sessions to be held at Thomas Selby's house.
- **1660:** Thomas and Mary Selbe sold 400 acres, tract N73 on the north side of Hungars Creek, Northampton County, Virginia, to John Willcocks.
- **1664:** Thomas and Mary sold the south half of tract A5, Allen's Quarter, in Accomack County, Virginia; later transactions reportedly returned the land to Selby before he sold the whole tract.
- **1666:** Thomas Selby Sr. patented 1,250 acres in Accomack County; the Virginia-Maryland boundary settlement reportedly placed the land in Maryland.
- **1 May 1675:** Patented 250 acres, Bishops Purchase (part of Simpleton), Worcester County, Maryland.
- **8 June 1677:** Sold Bishops Purchase to John Bishop Sr.
- **1681:** Witnessed Robert Richardson's codicil in Worcester County.
- **1696/97:** Listed as a Snow Hill Parish vestryman for Bogettenorton and Mattapany Hundreds.
- **28 Oct. 1701:** Made his will in Maryland.
- **24 Oct. 1702:** Will proved in Worcester County. The inventory was taken in 1703.

## Analysis

The profile establishes a long Selby presence on the Eastern Shore and records documented land, court, parish, and probate activity. It is important background for the later Worcester County Selby families, but it does **not** establish the parents of [[Major Selby Sr parentage remains unproven|Major Selby Sr.]]. Each generational connection must be proved separately.
